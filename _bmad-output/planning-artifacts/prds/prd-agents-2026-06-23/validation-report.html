Poor

This is a rigorous, chain-top PRD: the thesis in §1 is specific, every FR carries testable consequences, the Decision Register names what was given up (OQ-10, OQ-17, OQ-18), and the glossary and ID scheme are clean enough for UX, architecture, and story extraction to source from directly. What is at risk is the final label rather than the content: the PRD's own register records that a primary success metric is unattainable under a permitted configuration and that the metric set does not validate the thesis (OQ-19), §8 records seven of eight critical external dependencies as Uncommitted, and the document carries zero [ASSUMPTION] tags while naming external contract members that do not yet exist. The PRD is decision-ready as a build specification and not yet decision-ready as a launch instrument; the fixes are concentrated in §12 and §13, not spread across the FRs.

The adversarial and implementation-drift reviewers materially sharpen the picture. The adversarial pass would not sign off: it finds that FR-7's segregation-of-duties, access-guarantee, and configuration-time-rejection rules, read together, leave Confirmation Response Mode (UJ-3, the governance thesis) with no valid configuration or with orphan proposals nobody may approve; that the RQ-1 gate depends on adoption metrics (SM-2, and a circular SM-1) that can only exist after the enablement they gate; and that FR-24's "current read access" rule for audit content defeats the Compliance Operator job and orphans 365-day evidence once the Conversation is deleted. The drift pass confirms the FR-29 truth model, CapabilityVersion concurrency, and the fail-closed context policy are implemented as written, but shows the PRD naming public contract values the code does not use and FR-23 forbids renaming (PostFailed vs PostingFailed plus three extra proposal states; ApproverPolicySourceKind.ConversationOwner), and two binding rules the code contradicts today (reporting-only and accepted-risk cost postures pass the readiness gate; reused pricing versions are accepted). Together the three reviewers converge on the same verdict: the PRD is a strong build specification whose final label is not yet earned, with the blocking work concentrated in FR-7, FR-18, FR-24, §12, and §13.

Decision-readiness

adequate

The PRD states decisions as decisions. §13 is a binding register with owners, dates, and status, and the deferred rows are honestly marked ("must not be read as settled"). Trade-offs are named with the cost, not just the choice: OQ-10 says outright that "hexa is unavailable in Conversations that outgrow the budget, in exchange for never sending a silently reduced context to a Provider" and points at SM-C4 as the visibility mechanism; OQ-17 refuses to retroactively legitimize work done against Uncommitted dependencies; OQ-18 admits that "a single historical message that fails the active policy blocks generation in that Conversation permanently." A reader pushing back would find the objection already on the table.

The weakness is that one recorded tension is left standing inside a final document where it disables a primary gate. OQ-19 records that "SM-3's 26-hour terminal threshold is unattainable for any Agent configured with an expiry beyond 24 hours under OQ-3" and that "SM-3 measures the expiry setting rather than approval behavior" — and then defers it. FR-28 makes RQ-1 depend on live SM attainment, and FR-28's last consequence says an InsufficientEvidence primary metric forces NOT READY. So for any tenant that uses the 1-hour-to-30-day expiry range OQ-3 explicitly permits, the launch gate is either mechanically failing or measuring the wrong thing, and the PRD knows it. Escalation is the right process; leaving the metric in force as written is not a decision a release PM can act on.

A second, smaller tension is unresolved between configuration-time and call-time approval authority in FR-7. The PRD rejects at configuration time "a policy configuration that cannot satisfy this segregation of duties" and "a configured source that cannot satisfy the Conversation-access requirement at call time," but both properties depend on the membership of a Conversation that does not exist at configuration time. What the system does when the resolved Approver set at call time is empty, or consists only of the caller, is never stated.

critical

Primary metric SM-3 is known-unattainable under permitted configuration and left in force

§12 SM-3; §13 OQ-3, OQ-19; FR-28
OQ-3 permits expiry "from 1 hour through 30 days"; SM-3 requires "95% of Proposed Agent Replies ... reach a terminal state within 26 hours" and counts Expired as terminal, so the metric measures expiry configuration, not approval behavior, and cannot be met by a 30-day-expiry Agent unless 95% are decided by humans within 26 hours regardless. OQ-19 records both defects and defers them. A final PRD whose launch gate depends on a metric it declares broken cannot support a READY/NOT READY decision.
Fix: Either (a) re-express SM-3 in terms of human-decision latency (e.g. "95% of proposals receive an Approver decision — Approved, Rejected, or Abandoned — within N hours of creation, measured on proposals whose expiry is ≥ N hours") with expiry rate as a separate counter-metric, or (b) constrain OQ-3's range to ≤ 24 hours for launch tenants and re-open the range post-launch. Whichever is chosen, mark SM-3 as provisional in §12 until the OQ-19 escalation lands, so RQ-1 is not evaluated against a metric the PRD disowns.
medium

Call-time Approver resolution has no stated outcome for empty or caller-only sets

FR-7 consequences 9–11; FR-21
FR-7 says segregation-of-duties and Conversation-access failures are "rejected at configuration time," but a policy source such as "the Conversation Facilitator" resolves to zero Parties in a Conversation with no Facilitator, and to the caller alone when the caller is the Facilitator. Substance over theater

strong

The four UJs each carry real decisions: UJ-3's "no Party may approve a version it last edited" is the origin of FR-7's segregation rule and FR-17's human-edited provenance; UJ-4's "typed result before any side effect" drives FR-20, FR-23, and FR-29; UJ-1's edge case drives FR-5's eligibility set. No persona is decorative and none exceeds the four-persona ceiling. The Vision is not swappable — it names hexa, Party identity, the Conversation model, and the specific things V1 refuses to do. The addendum's differentiation (governed participation versus summarization) is grounded in named competitors and is reflected in the actual design of Proposed Agent Replies and Approver Policy, not left as a positioning paragraph.

NFRs are mostly earned. NFR-9, NFR-11, NFR-12, and NFR-14 carry numbers and sample sizes; NFR-8 and NFR-10 carry hard behavioral rules. NFR-1 through NFR-7 are closer to category statements, but each is concretely instantiated by FRs (NFR-6 by FR-4 and FR-22, NFR-3 by FR-10/FR-12/FR-18), so they read as headings over real content rather than boilerplate.

Strategic coherence

adequate

The PRD has a thesis and bets on it: §1 says "AI assistance must become part of the conversation model without weakening the model's existing identity and governance guarantees," and the feature set follows — Party identity (FR-2), governed Provider catalog (FR-4/5), approval workflow (FR-13–18), fail-closed context (FR-9), untrusted-content boundary (FR-31). The MVP scope kind is problem-solving/platform ("prove governed participation first," §2.2 and addendum), and §6.2's deferrals match it. Counter-metrics are present and well chosen; SM-C4 in particular is the honest cost of the OQ-10 decision made measurable.

The gap is between the thesis and the primary metrics, and the PRD says so itself. OQ-19 records "the finding that they measure adoption and throughput rather than whether governed AI participation is valued." SM-1 is a one-tenant existence proof, SM-2 is call frequency, SM-3 is workflow throughput. None measures whether attribution, approval, or audit changed anything for a Conversation — whether approvers reject or edit at a rate that shows governance is doing work, whether posted replies are acted on, whether tenants that could choose Automatic mode choose Confirmation for the reasons the thesis predicts. The rubric names exactly this pattern (activity metrics under an engagement-quality thesis) as a tell.

high

Primary metrics measure activity, not the thesis, and the PRD concedes it

§12 SM-1–SM-3; §13 OQ-19
the launch gate RQ-1 will pass or fail on adoption and throughput while the bet in §1 is about governance not weakening the Conversation model. The counter-metrics are closer to the thesis than the primaries are.
Fix: Add at least one primary metric that a governance-value hypothesis would falsify — e.g. edit-or-reject rate on Proposed Agent Replies within a band (too low means approval is rubber-stamping; too high means generation is unusable), or share of Conversations where an Agent Response is followed by a human reply within the window — and demote SM-2 to secondary. The measurement-contract machinery in §11 already exists to carry it. If Product declines, record the accepted risk in OQ-19 as a decision, not a deferral.

Done-ness clarity

strong

This is the PRD's best dimension. Every FR has a consequences block and almost all consequences are verifiable: FR-4's pricing rules ("a pricing version that starts at 1 on create and strictly increases"), FR-9's budget derivation, FR-18's explicit state machine with Approved/PostFailed/Posted distinguished, FR-26's enumerated always-block categories, FR-29's three write stages, NFR-9 and NFR-14 with percentiles and minimum sample counts. Adjective-only requirements are rare and mostly confined to admin-visibility statements. An engineer can write acceptance tests from most of this directly.

The residual gaps are of two kinds. First, the PRD uses the word "bounded" or "documented" in places where its own standard elsewhere is a number: the Unknown-reservation hold period (FR-28, OQ-6), the PostFailed retry bound (FR-18), the regeneration ceiling's "documented default and a valid range" (FR-32) — contrast expiry, which gets "24 hours ... 1 hour through 30 days." Second, one term that anchors both a primary metric and every latency gate — "accepted" Agent Call — is not defined. SM-2 counts "accepted Agent Calls"; NFR-9 and OQ-5 time from "accepted-call"; nothing in §3 or FR-8 says which check marks acceptance (authorization? membership join? reservation? context-policy pass?). The choice moves the SM-2 numerator and the NFR-9 start timestamp.

Shape fit

adequate

The shape matches the product on the axes that matter most: a multi-stakeholder B2B capability with a governance workflow, so UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing and present; chain-top, so the traceability investment (§3, stable IDs, §0 downstream-authority paragraph) is justified; brownfield, so the PRD is careful to say that Conversations must publish the contract members it names rather than implying they exist. All UJs are new, which is correct for a new capability, and no existing UJs are misrepresented as new.

A reader looking for "what does hexa do" has to step around "what proves it" repeatedly. This is a deliberate choice, and for a governed-AI launch it is defensible, but it is heavier than a "prove one thing well" V1 (§1) would predict, and it is where the process-in-FR leakage above comes from.

Adversarial review — 3 critical · 8 high · 12 medium · 10 low

review-adversarial-general.md

I would not sign off. The document is unusually precise about what must never happen (silent truncation, unapproved posting, secret leakage) and unusually vague about the things an implementer must actually decide on day one: who holds "Agent call permission", who owns cost caps, whether hexa is one Agent per tenant, what "accepted" means relative to membership and fail-closed checks, and how a proposal moves through its states when the Agent is disabled or the Conversation removes it. The single biggest way this PRD gets misbuilt is FR-7: read literally, its three simultaneous constraints — configuration-time rejection of any policy that "cannot satisfy" segregation of duties, rejection of any source "whose holders are not guaranteed Conversation access", and "the caller may not be the sole Approver" — make every realistic Approver Policy (Facilitator-only, a single named Party, a tenant role) either unconfigurable or capable of producing proposals no one is permitted to approve. A fully compliant implementation of §4.3 leaves Confirmation Response Mode, the product's central governance thesis (UJ-3), without a valid configuration. Close behind it: the release gate (FR-28 → RQ-1) is wired to adoption metrics that can only be produced after the enablement they gate.

Notes

  • EXT-* status: §8 says only EXT-HOST-1 is Committed; every consuming story is blocked from ready-for-dev under FR-21/OQ-17. The PRD is therefore final for a product no story can currently start. That is a process fact, not a defect in the text, but it makes the many "production-like" evidence requirements untestable for the foreseeable future.
  • Evidence Level 4 requires "an actual ... Provider ... process". Every Level 4 run costs real Provider money and counts against whatever cap the qualification tenant has; nothing exempts qualification from FR-32's "unconfigured cap blocks production-like generation", so qualification needs its own configured caps. Worth a sentence.
  • NFR-9's 2 s pre-Provider rejection includes the context-budget check, which requires loading and tokenizing the entire Conversation. For Conversations near the budget on large models this is the slowest pre-Provider step; the gate is testable but may be unrealistic for the exact cases it matters most.
  • NFR-11 "Provider attempts are at-most-once" is tautological per attempt; the intended invariant is "at most one Provider attempt with possible usage per Agent Call". FR-28 already states this correctly; NFR-11 should mirror it.
  • Approved → Expired is possible when ExpiresAt passes between approval and post; SM-3 then counts a human-decided proposal as Expired. Small, but it argues for freezing ExpiresAt semantics at Approved.
  • The addendum is explicitly non-normative and consistent with the PRD; nothing to report.
critical

Segregation-of-duties and access-guarantee rules make Confirmation Response Mode unconfigurable or produce orphan proposals

FR-7, UJ-3, OQ-14
FR-7 says "The caller may not be the sole Approver of their own Agent Call, and the approving Party must differ from the Party that last edited the version being approved. A policy configuration that cannot satisfy this segregation of duties is rejected at configuration time." Whether the caller is the sole Approver is only knowable at call time (it depends on who calls). A Facilitator-only policy is fine for Milan's call and violated for the Facilitator's own call. Two literal readings, both wrong: (a) reject Facilitator-only at configuration time because a Facilitator could call — then UJ-3's default configuration is illegal and, because the same FR also rejects "a bare tenant role whose holders are not guaranteed Conversation access" (predefined Parties are equally not guaranteed access), no single-source policy survives validation; (b) accept it and at call time either reject the Facilitator's call with no stated reason code, or create a Pending proposal that no Party may approve, which sits until Expired and pollutes SM-3's expiry rate. The edit rule has the same hole: with a single Approver, the moment she edits, the proposal becomes unapprovable ("a second authorized Approver would have to approve") and nothing says edit must be refused when no second Approver exists.
Fix: Split the rule into a configuration-time check (policy must be able to yield ≥2 distinct Parties or must include a fallback source) and explicit call-time and edit-time behaviors: if the resolved Approver set for this proposal is {caller} → typed rejection of the call before Provider work; if an edit would leave zero eligible approvers → typed rejection of the edit. Define "bare tenant role" or delete the phrase; decide whether predefined Parties are permitted at all under the access-guarantee rule.
critical

Adoption metrics gate production enablement but can only be produced after it

FR-28, SM-1, SM-2, §11, §12
FR-28: "Production enablement remains blocked until RQ-1 records READY from the required live evidence and launch metrics" and "When a primary metric evaluates to InsufficientEvidence, RQ-1 records NOT READY". SM-2 is a primary metric requiring "At least 20% of eligible Conversations ... record one or more accepted Agent Calls during a rolling 30-day window, measured only when the cohort contains at least 50 eligible Conversations." Before production enablement the only source is "production-like launch validation", where the population of Conversations and the callers are the test team. Either the qualification team scripts 50 conversations with 20% calls (metric is gamed and measures nothing) or a real single-tenant launch (SM-1 needs one tenant) with fewer than 50 eligible Conversations returns InsufficientEvidence → NOT READY indefinitely. SM-1 is also circular: it requires the tenant "satisfies launch-readiness gates", and readiness requires SM-1.
Fix: Move SM-2 and SM-3 out of the RQ-1 gate into post-enablement launch-health review (they are outcome metrics, not readiness evidence); keep in the gate only what can be produced pre-enablement (latency, isolation, safety, audit completeness on the qualification cohort). Strip "satisfies launch-readiness gates" from SM-1.
critical

Audit content access rule defeats the Compliance Operator job and orphans evidence when the Conversation goes away

FR-24, §9, §2.1, FR-30
FR-24: "Inspecting Audit Evidence that contains Conversation-derived content additionally requires current read access to that Source Conversation, and fails closed otherwise." §2.1 JTBD: "As a Tenant or Compliance Operator, prove who called the Agent, what it generated, what changed during approval, who approved it, and what was finally posted." A compliance operator is not a participant of every Conversation, so "what it generated" and "what changed" are unreadable unless the operator is injected into every Conversation, which is itself a governance violation. FR-30/§9 require an operator to "request authorized export" and apply legal hold to content the same rule forbids them to read. When Hexalith.Conversations deletes the Conversation under its own retention ("Posted Conversation Messages remain governed by Hexalith.Conversations retention"), "current read access" is impossible for everyone, so 365 days of retained Agent content becomes permanently uninspectable while still being retained.
Fix: Define a distinct compliance-inspection authorization (tenant-scoped role, audited, justification-required) that bypasses the participant check; define the behavior when the Source Conversation no longer exists (evidence remains inspectable under compliance authorization; participant-based access is simply unavailable).
high

The stated Conversations dependency surface cannot deliver several normative guarantees

§8, FR-2, FR-11, FR-17, NFR-11, EXT-CONV-AI-1, EXT-CONV-UI-1
§8 limits EXT-CONV-AI-1 to "IConversationClient.AddParticipantAsync and POST /api/v1/conversations/{conversationId}/participants" and EXT-CONV-UI-1 to the Call hexa action contribution. Nothing in the register scope covers: (1) posting a Conversation Message as an AiAgent participant with a trace reference ("The posted message references the Agent Call or equivalent trace identifier"); (2) idempotent posting so that "Restart or replay cannot duplicate ... Conversation posts" (NFR-11) — exactly-once across a process boundary requires an idempotency key on the Conversations side; (3) rendering "a participant-visible marker identifying it as AI-generated" (FR-11) and "whether the posted version was human-edited and by which Party ... exposed wherever the message's provenance is disclosed" (FR-17) — these are Conversations UI concerns; (4) notification of participant removal so that "removal ... moves every non-terminal proposal for that Conversation to Abandoned" (FR-2) can fire when removal happens in the Conversations UI rather than through Agents; (5) enumerating participants of every tenant Conversation to compute the "Eligible Conversation" denominator.
Fix: Either add these to the named dependency contracts in §8 with the same nine-field commitment record, or downgrade the affected consequences to what Agents alone can guarantee (e.g., marker lives in message metadata Agents controls; removal abandons proposals only when performed through the Agents surface).

Direct contradiction on proposal visibility after access loss

FR-13 vs FR-7 and UJ-3

The PRD's structural spine matches the code well: the FR-29 acceptance stages (Submitted / AuthoritativePending / ProjectionConfirmed) exist verbatim as AgentSetupTruthState, the CapabilityVersion optimistic-concurrency rule is implemented exactly as written, the FR-5 selection-eligibility set maps one-to-one onto ProviderSelectionValidationStatus, and the context policy fails closed on oversize with no silent truncation. Mechanical notes

  • ID continuity. FR-1 through FR-32, NFR-1 through NFR-14, OQ-1 through OQ-19, SM-1 through SM-6 and SM-C1 through SM-C4, UJ-1 through UJ-4: all unique, all referenced IDs resolve. FR headings are not in numeric order in the document — FR-29 sits between FR-23 and FR-24, FR-30 between FR-25 and FR-26, FR-31 between FR-26 and FR-27, FR-32 after FR-28. This is the correct outcome of the stated "globally stable" ID policy (§0) and should not be renumbered; downstream tooling that assumes document order equals ID order will need to key on IDs.
  • Assumptions Index roundtrip. Vacuous: zero inline [ASSUMPTION] tags and no index. See the Scope honesty high finding — the roundtrip passes only because nothing was tagged.
  • Callout tags. Zero [NOTE FOR PM] and zero [NON-GOAL for MVP] inline callouts. The §13 register's Deferred rows and §5/§6.2 do the equivalent work for a final-stage document; acceptable, noting that OQ-18's consequence is not surfaced where it applies (FR-27).
  • UJ protagonists. All four named with inline context (Nora, Milan, Anika, Omar). UJ-3 carries the segregation-of-duties rule inline, which matches FR-7.
  • Glossary drift (low). "Agent response(s)" lowercase in §2.2, UJ-2, UJ-3, FR-19, §6.2 (5 occurrences) versus glossary "Agent Response"; "Agent call" lowercase in UJ-3 and FR-8 consequence 1 (and "Agent calling" in FR-20) versus "Agent Call"; bare "Participant(s)" in UJ-2, the Eligible Conversation glossary entry, and FR-31 versus "Conversation Participant"; glossary heading "Provider/Model Selection" (capital M) versus "Provider/model selection" everywhere else (6 occurrences).
