# Adversarial Review — Hexalith Agents PRD (2026-09-09, post-course-correction text)

Reviewed: `prd.md` (status `final`, `updated: 2026-09-09`, 879 lines) and `addendum.md`. Consulted `external-dependency-register.md`, `sprint-change-proposal-2026-09-09.md`, and the `ARCH-A-n` table and AD-21/AD-30 rules in `architecture/architecture-agents-2026-06-23-2/ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md` only where a PRD claim depends on them. Findings the current text already fixes are not re-raised.

## Verdict

I would not sign off. The 2026-09-09 update did what the previous gate asked: FR-7 is now one predicate, FR-2 is a three-part step, §8 registers six Conversations seams, cost reservation is ordered and released, SM-3 is decision latency, and unretired assumptions block `RQ-1`. But the course-correction that followed landed `EXT-PROTECTION-1` as an orphan: §0 still counts "seven of eight" dependencies against a nine-entry list, no FR requires a dependency to be `Available` (only `Committed`) before live content-bearing execution, the sentence that disables such execution has no requirement id, no typed blocker, no owner, and no test, and OQ-22's `RQ-1` "evaluates only" list excludes dependency status altogether — so the document now carries three different definitions of what `RQ-1` evaluates. The `ARCH-A-1..7` delegation hands seven `RQ-1` blockers to an unversioned document whose owner retires its own rows, and that document already diverges from the PRD on reservation settlement (`Unreconciled`, period close) and on the role matrix (Platform Operator provisions `hexa`). Below that, the state machines written to satisfy the last gate still have literal holes: the FR-2 step can never re-admit `hexa` after a cleared block because its first branch matches first; FR-7's scheduled re-check turns a transient Conversations outage into an irreversible `Abandoned`; a `PostingFailed` proposal can be abandoned after its post actually landed, leaving audit and Conversation in contradiction; and the kill switch is both a system-abandonment reason (FR-18) and a "humans may still reject or abandon" pause (FR-28), with a trigger a single hostile Participant can trip. Eleven of the thirteen deferred mediums stand untouched. One more pass, focused on wiring the new dependency and closing the state-transition gaps, is needed before this is an executable requirements authority.

## Critical

### C-1. `EXT-PROTECTION-1` is an orphan, and its arrival exposes that `RQ-1` has three incompatible definitions

- **Location:** §0 line 16; §8 lines 718, 720, 731; FR-21 lines 467–469; FR-28 lines 612–613, 621; §3 `RQ-1` line 114; OQ-22 line 878; §9 line 774; FR-30 line 555.
- **Quoted:** §0: "seven of eight critical external dependencies are `Uncommitted`". §8: "The initial register scope includes `EXT-CONV-AI-1`, `EXT-CONV-UI-1`, `EXT-HOST-1`, `EXT-PROVIDER-1`, `EXT-SAFETY-1`, `EXT-TOKEN-1`, `EXT-SECRETS-1`, `EXT-PROTECTION-1`, and `EXT-TOPOLOGY-1`" — nine. §8 line 731: "Live content-bearing execution is disabled while its register entry is not `Available`; the shipped no-op protection implementation cannot satisfy production-like evidence." OQ-22: "`RQ-1` evaluates only evidence a qualification cohort can produce before enablement: live Evidence Levels 4 and 5, SM-1, SM-4, SM-5, SM-6, the NFR-9 and NFR-14 latency gates, and audit completeness."
- **Attack:** (a) The status line of a `final` document undercounts its own blockers; the register says "eight records are `Uncommitted`". (b) The only PRD gate that mentions dependency status is FR-21, and it gates `ready-for-dev` on `Committed`. Nothing in FR-21, FR-28, §3, or OQ-22 requires `Available`; FR-28 line 612 says qualification may collect live evidence once "external dependency commitments are active and recorded" — `Committed` is a commitment. The register's rule ("No runtime, test, or qualification path may execute a consumed external seam until the record is `Available`") lives only in the register, and OQ-22's "evaluates only" excludes dependency status from `RQ-1` entirely, so a literal reader of OQ-22 can record READY with the no-op protection engine in place. (c) The "disabled while not `Available`" sentence is the one place the PRD states the runtime bar, and it has no FR number, no typed blocker (compare `ProhibitedCostControlPosture`, `UnretiredAssumption`), no FR-33 owner, no A-index row, and no consequence an engineer can test — is it a runtime check in FR-28's readiness evaluation, or a process rule? (d) §9's "completion requires restrictive confirmation from payload protection", FR-30's legal hold/export/deletion operations, and the `Erased` replay semantics all depend on this engine and none cite it, so a story implementing FR-30 against the no-op default passes FR-21 as written (FR-30 is not among the register's consuming stories by FR id, only by story number). (e) Three `RQ-1` lists: §3 (Levels 4/5 + SM-1/4/5/6 + NFR-9/14), FR-28 (adds audit completeness, `UnretiredAssumption`, postures, caps, "NFR-11 through NFR-14", register readiness records), OQ-22 ("only" the §3 list + audit completeness + assumptions). NFR-11's recovery exercise and NFR-12's numeric limits appear in FR-28's first sentence and nowhere else (M-6).
- **Fix:** Correct §0 to "eight of nine". Add to FR-28 one enumerated `RQ-1` input list that all three locations cite by reference, including: every dependency in the qualification profile `Available` with a passing compatibility command (typed blocker `DependencyNotAvailable` naming the entry), the NFR-11 recovery exercise, and the NFR-12 limit records. Add a FR-21 consequence: "Content-bearing runtime paths (proposal versions, generated content, Conversation Context, Audit Evidence content fields) fail closed with a typed `PayloadProtectionUnavailable` outcome while `EXT-PROTECTION-1` is not `Available`; a no-op protection implementation is a readiness blocker, not a default." Cite `EXT-PROTECTION-1` from §9 and FR-30.

### C-2. The FR-2 membership step cannot re-admit `hexa` after a cleared block: its removal branch matches first, and the mirror's failure mode is undefined

- **Location:** FR-2 lines 148, 152–153; FR-33 row line 417 ("Remove, block, and re-admit"); OQ-16 line 872.
- **Quoted:** "has three parts, in order: read `hexa`'s participant state in that Conversation; if Agents previously established membership there and `hexa` is now absent, treat it as an external removal — set the Agents-owned block ...; only if Agents has never established membership there, or the block was cleared by the named authority, add `hexa` as a participant." And: "Removal is an Agents-owned per-Conversation block that also removes `hexa` from the Conversation's participant list through the Conversations participant-removal seam".
- **Attack:** Removal mirrors to Conversations, so after any removal `hexa` is absent there. The authority clears the block. Next call: part two evaluates first — "Agents previously established membership" (true) "and `hexa` is now absent" (true, the mirror removed it) — so the step treats the clear as a fresh external removal, re-sets the block, abandons the proposals, and rejects. Part three's "or the block was cleared" is unreachable because the parts run "in order" and part two never consults the cleared flag. Re-admission is impossible under the literal text, and the FR-33 "re-admit" operation has no defined effect (does it clear the block, re-join immediately, or both?). Second hole: the block "also removes `hexa` ... through the ... seam". If the seam call fails (Conversations unavailable), either the block is set without the mirror — `hexa` remains listed "on the same terms as any other participant" while the PRD says removal "is visible to Participants and not only to Agents" — or removal fails when Conversations is down, which contradicts the block being "Agents-owned". Third: a Facilitator can clear a block the Tenant Agent Administrator set for tenant policy reasons, and vice versa; the row grants both symmetric authority over each other's decisions.
- **Fix:** Replace the prose with a `ConversationAgentState` machine: `NeverJoined → Joined → (ExternallyRemoved | Blocked) → Cleared → Joined`. Clearing the block records `ReadmitPending`; the membership step adds `hexa` when state is `NeverJoined` or `ReadmitPending`, detects external removal only when state is `Joined` and the participant read says absent, and never abandons proposals from `ReadmitPending`. State that the block is authoritative and durable immediately, the mirror is an at-least-once outbox with typed `MirrorPending` status visible under FR-25, and calls are rejected while the mirror is pending. State whether a block set by the Tenant Agent Administrator can be cleared by a Facilitator (recommend: cleared only by the setting authority or a higher one).

### C-3. The proposal lifecycle converts transient faults into irreversible terminal states, and can record `Abandoned` for content that was actually posted

- **Location:** FR-7 line 236; FR-18 lines 392, 396–397; FR-21 line 460; §9 line 771; NFR-11 line 699; §8 seam 2 line 724.
- **Quoted:** FR-7: "When any later action or scheduled re-check finds the Eligible Approver set empty, or finds the Source Conversation gone or inaccessible to the Agents Service Principal, the system moves the proposal to `Abandoned`". FR-18: "When the retry budget is exhausted, the proposal remains `PostingFailed` ... until ... an Eligible Approver abandons it; the Tenant Agent Administrator abandons it ...". §9: "Rejected, abandoned, and expired proposals remain audit records and cannot later be posted."
- **Attack:** (a) "Inaccessible to the Agents Service Principal" and "finds the Eligible Approver set empty" are both what a roster read returns during a Conversations outage or a stale-token window. FR-21 says fail closed on "unavailable" — but `Abandoned` is terminal and irreversible, so one sweep during a five-minute outage abandons every awaiting proposal in the tenant, versions preserved but decisions lost, and SM-3 excludes them from its denominator so the damage is invisible to the primary launch-health metric. FR-18's pre-post check goes to retryable `PostingFailed` for the same condition; FR-7 goes to terminal `Abandoned`. The scheduled re-check itself has no cadence, owner, or load bound. (b) Posting is at-least-once with an idempotency key (seam 2). A post whose acknowledgement is lost is `PostingFailed`; after three attempts over fifteen minutes of Conversations unavailability the proposal sits there, then an Approver or Administrator abandons it. The message is in the Conversation, attributed to `hexa`, while Agents records `Abandoned` and §9 says it "cannot later be posted". SM-5 audit completeness and FR-24 traceability are both broken for that message. Nothing requires a lookup by `MessageId` before abandoning or before retrying. (c) The state list gives no transition table: `Approved` is non-terminal, so FR-16's "blocked after a proposal reaches a terminal state" permits regeneration after approval, and FR-15's "before approval" is a description, not a rejection rule. What state does a proposal enter when regenerated while `Approved`?
- **Fix:** Distinguish *absent* from *unknown*: the scheduled re-check abandons only on an authoritative "Conversation deleted / Party removed" answer and marks `ResolutionUnavailable` (non-terminal, visible under FR-25) on any unavailable answer, with the sweep cadence stated (e.g., hourly) and bounded. Before any exit from `PostingFailed`, the system queries the message by its `MessageId`; if present, the proposal moves to `Posted` with a `LateConfirmed` flag; add that lookup to seam 2 as a required read. Add a transition table to FR-18 that names every allowed (from, to, actor, guard) tuple and states that edit and regeneration are rejected from `Approved`, `PostingPending`, and `PostingFailed`.

### H-1. Delegating `ARCH-A-1..ARCH-A-7` to "the current Architecture Spine" creates a governance hole the PRD's own change control cannot see

- **Location:** §8.1 line 742; §0 lines 14, 16, 18; FR-28 line 614; OQ-22 line 878; Spine ARCH-A table lines 736–742, AD-21 (line 222), AD-30 (line 276).
- **Quoted:** "Architecture assumptions `ARCH-A-1` through `ARCH-A-7` are normatively indexed and governed in the current Architecture Spine rather than duplicated here. Any unretired Product, Architecture, or Governance assumption blocks `RQ-1`". §0: "Any approved proposal listing `prd.md` under `amends_if_approved` is not landed until a PRD Update run applies it".
- **Attack:** (a) The reference is a closed range and an unversioned, unpathed document; the Spine is modified in the working tree today. If the Spine adds `ARCH-A-8` it is outside "1 through 7" and blocks nothing; if it retires a row, `RQ-1`'s blocker set changes with no PRD Update, contradicting §0's landing rule. (b) The Spine's owners retire their own rows: `ARCH-A-4` is retired when Story 5.6 "records a permanent override reason" — retirement by writing a sentence; `ARCH-A-3` (which gates are Platform-scoped) is a Product decision retired inside an Architecture document. (c) The Spine already diverges from the PRD in ways Product has not seen: AD-21 introduces an `Unreconciled` reservation state settled "at period close as `ChargedAtMaximum`" (`ARCH-A-6`) for a reservation "past its deadline with no authoritative outcome" — a case FR-28's outcome list (no-usage, usage, `Indeterminate`, `NotInvoked`) does not contain, so the tenant can be charged under a rule the PRD never states; AD-30 grants the Platform Operator a "create-only `AgentSetupMutation` that provisions `hexa` at tenant enablement", an operation absent from the FR-33 matrix the PRD calls "the authoritative role matrix for every authorization rule"; AD-21 says the `PostingFailed` retry bound "is fixed by AD-5, not configurable" while PRD A-7 says Architecture "confirms or retunes before enablement" — either A-7 is already retired and the index is stale, or the Spine pre-empted it. (d) FR-28's blocker rule reads "Any §8.1 assumption whose owner is ..." — an `ARCH-A` row is not a §8.1 assumption; it is referenced from §8.1. The blocker type `UnretiredAssumption` "names the row" — which table?
- **Fix:** Cite the Spine by path and the ARCH-A table by version; make the range open ("every `ARCH-A-n` row in that table at `RQ-1` evaluation"); require that an `ARCH-A` row whose retirement condition names Product, Governance, or the Release PM be retired only by a recorded confirmation from that party; add a reconciliation rule that any Spine rule granting a role an operation not in FR-33, or defining a reservation/proposal outcome not in FR-28/FR-18, is a PRD amendment pending, not an architecture detail. Bring `Unreconciled`/period-close settlement and the Platform Operator provisioning row into the PRD or reject them.

### H-2. The kill-switch trigger is gameable by one hostile Participant and has no minimum sample

- **Location:** FR-28 line 622; SM-C4 line 846; FR-32 line 635; FR-33 row line 422.
- **Quoted:** "a blocked-call share (SM-C4) above 50% or a posting-failure rate above 10% sustained for seven days, on which the Release Operator pulls it".
- **Attack:** SM-C4 is a share of *Agent Calls*, per tenant, with no minimum sample, no distinct-Party or distinct-Conversation denominator, and no exclusion of calls that were rejected cheaply. Blocked calls are the cheapest outcome in the system: an oversized Conversation rejects at FR-8 step 5 in under two seconds and spends nothing. Rate limits are per Party *and* per Conversation, so one Participant with access to several oversized Conversations (or one Conversation the Participant deliberately inflates past the Safe Context Budget) generates blocked calls at the rate-limit ceiling for seven days; in a low-traffic tenant that is far above 50%. The sentence "on which the Release Operator pulls it" is mandatory, not a review — so the outcome is a tenant-wide denial of `hexa` caused by one user and executed by the operator. The "posting-failure rate" still has no numerator (predecessor L-4), so its 10% is not computable.
- **Fix:** Define the trigger over distinct calling Parties and distinct Conversations with a minimum of N Parties and M calls, exclude rate-limit rejections and repeat rejections of the same (Party, Conversation, reason) tuple within the window, and make the threshold a mandatory *review* with the pull a recorded decision. Define the posting-failure numerator (proposals that entered `PostingFailed` at least once in the window over proposals that entered `Approved`).

### H-3. Tenant isolation stops at the Provider boundary: every tenant's Conversation content goes to one platform-owned Provider account with no data-handling requirement

- **Location:** FR-4 lines 173, 183; FR-19 line 445; FR-33 rows lines 412–413; §9 (no Provider clause); NFR-2 line 690; FR-26 line 576.
- **Quoted:** FR-4: "Each model records its secret reference and whether the Provider is configured". FR-33: secret references are Platform-scoped. NFR-2: "Conversation Context, proposal content, and Audit Evidence must not leak across tenants or unauthorized Parties."
- **Attack:** The Global Providers Aggregate is `system`-scoped and secret references are per model, so all tenants' complete Source Conversations are sent under the same third-party credential. The PRD says nothing about Provider-side retention, training use, sub-processor region, or zero-data-retention terms; "cross-tenant ... data" is a blocked *output* category only. A compliance stakeholder will read §9 as a data-governance section that governs Agents' own store and forgets the one place tenant content leaves the platform in bulk on every call. Tenant enablement (A-10) records nothing the tenant agreed to.
- **Fix:** Add to FR-4 required capability metadata per Provider/model: data-retention term, training-use opt-out status, processing region, and a recorded contractual reference; to FR-33 tenant enablement: the tenant's recorded acceptance of those terms (or a per-tenant secret reference option); to §9: "Conversation Context sent to a Provider is governed by the recorded Provider data-handling terms; a model whose terms are unrecorded cannot be enabled for any tenant." Add the Provider terms record to Audit Evidence for each call.

### H-4. The compliance-inspection control approved for the last gate is the subject of the audit approving its own inspection, and "post-hoc review" makes the second party optional

- **Location:** FR-24 line 531; FR-33 rows lines 424, 428; OQ-21 line 877; §9 line 777.
- **Quoted:** "Compliance inspection ... requires a recorded justification and either second-party approval or post-hoc review (both by the Tenant Agent Administrator)". FR-33: "Apply or release legal hold; request authorized export — Compliance Inspector — Tenant".
- **Attack:** The Tenant Agent Administrator configures `hexa`, its instructions, its Approver Policy, and the caps; the most likely compliance case is whether that Administrator misconfigured or abused the Agent. The inspection's second party is the Administrator. And the Inspector chooses "post-hoc review", so the inspection happens unilaterally at access time and the Administrator learns of it afterward — exactly the unilateral read the last gate's H-2 objected to, with a review bolted on. Export has no second-party control at all: one tenant-scoped role can request an encrypted, time-limited export of every retained Agent-derived record in the tenant. Legal hold and its release are the same single role.
- **Fix:** Second party must be a Party that is not the subject of the case and not the Inspector; where the case names the Tenant Agent Administrator, the approver is the Platform Operator or a second Compliance Inspector. Post-hoc review is permitted only for inspections scoped to one proposal or one Conversation and must be completed within a stated window or the access is recorded as unreviewed on the audit surface. Export requires prior second-party approval; hold release is audited with a separate approver.

### H-5. FR-18 and FR-28 disagree about what the kill switch does to proposals, and nobody can release it (M-1 re-rated)

- **Location:** FR-18 line 392; FR-28 line 622; FR-33 row line 422; FR-3 lines 161–163.
- **Quoted:** FR-18: "A proposal abandoned by the system carries its typed reason (`NoEligibleApprover`, `SourceConversationUnavailable`, `RemovedInConversations`, or kill switch)". FR-28: "Pulling it ... lets proposals awaiting a decision be rejected or abandoned but not approved, lets `Approved` and `PostingPending` proposals complete or fail on their own terms".
- **Attack:** Under FR-18 the system abandons awaiting proposals when the switch is pulled; under FR-28 they remain for humans to reject or abandon. An engineer must pick one; a tester cannot write the test. `PostingFailed` is not mentioned: does its retry budget run down while the switch is on? Nothing says who releases the switch or what happens to proposals that were awaiting decision when it was pulled if it is released (are they still approvable — their `ExpiresAt` kept running). No lifecycle state distinguishes "suspended by kill switch" from "disabled" (FR-3), so the FR-25 status surface cannot tell an operator which one it is looking at.
- **Fix:** Pick the FR-28 semantics, delete "kill switch" from the FR-18 system-abandonment list, add a `Suspended` Agent lifecycle state that FR-25 exposes, state that `PostingFailed` retry windows and `ExpiresAt` are frozen while suspended, and add the FR-33 row "Release the kill switch — Platform Operator — Tenant".

## Medium

### M-1. Segregation of duties is Party-granular, and a Party can be an organization

- **Location:** §3 "Party" line 107, "Eligible Approver" line 102; FR-7 line 235.
- **Quoted:** "**Party** - A stable Hexalith.Parties identity representing a human, organization, or AI participant." "the approving Party must itself be an Eligible Approver".
- **Attack:** The predicate compares Party identities, not authenticated principals. A human who can act as their personal Party and as an organization Party (or holds two Parties in the tenant) calls as one and approves as the other; every FR-7 check passes. Nothing says who may act for an organization Party or that organization Parties cannot be Approvers.
- **Fix:** "Parties of type organization or `AiAgent` are never resolved as Approvers or callers in V1 (closes predecessor M-7 too); the predicate additionally excludes any Party whose acting principal is the caller's principal or the last editor's principal."

### M-2. Unsafe human edits are stored unscanned, making the proposal store a durable dump for always-blocked content

- **Location:** FR-15 line 359; FR-27 lines 601–602; FR-26 line 576; §9 line 773.
- **Quoted:** "The exact version being approved passes the then-current active policy at approval time. A human-edited version is scanned on the same terms as generated output".
- **Attack:** The scan is at approval time. An edit is recorded as an immutable version first (FR-14), retained 365 days, and visible to every Eligible Approver and Compliance Inspector. An Approver can therefore persist content the platform policy "always blocks" inside Agents' audit store without ever approving it.
- **Fix:** Scan at edit time; a failing edit is a typed rejection and is not recorded as a version (only as a content-free safety outcome in Audit Evidence).

### M-3. A-14 can be retired by postponement, and OQ-18/OQ-23's "before enablement" conditions have no gate hook

- **Location:** §8.1 A-14 line 759; OQ-18 line 874; OQ-23 line 879; FR-28 line 614.
- **Quoted:** A-14 "Retired when: The decision lands or Product moves the date". OQ-23: "revisit ... before the first Automatic-mode tenant is enabled".
- **Attack:** A-14 is a Product-owned `RQ-1` blocker whose retirement condition includes "Product moves the date" — Product unblocks `RQ-1` by editing a date, with OQ-18 undecided. OQ-18's "in any case before production enablement" and OQ-23's "before the first Automatic-mode tenant is enabled" are not A-rows and not `RQ-1` inputs, so nothing enforces them; an Automatic-mode tenant can be enabled with no launch-health primary metric at all.
- **Fix:** Make A-14's condition "the OQ-18 decision lands"; add `OpenDecision` as an `RQ-1` blocker type for any Deferred OQ row whose text says "before enablement"; make OQ-23 a blocker for enabling an Automatic-mode tenant specifically.

### M-4. OQ-18's "no exclusion path" is false if Conversations lets a Participant delete a message, and undefined if it does not

- **Location:** FR-27 lines 602–603; OQ-18 line 874; §8 seam 5 line 727 (A-15).
- **Quoted:** "an edited or deleted message simply no longer matches its hash ... V1 provides no redaction or exclusion path for individual historical messages".
- **Attack:** If Conversations supports deletion, deleting the offending message is an exclusion path (and a tenant-actionable one the PRD says does not exist); if it supports soft deletion, A-15's content read may still return the deleted content and the block persists; if it forbids deletion, the Conversation is permanently unavailable to `hexa`. The seam does not say what the content read returns for edited or deleted messages.
- **Fix:** Add to seam 5: "the content read returns only messages a Participant would currently see, with edited content in its current form". Rewrite OQ-18 to say what the tenant can do (delete the message in Conversations, where permitted) rather than that nothing can be done.

### M-5. The A-4 fallback depends on a Conversations event feed that no seam registers

- **Location:** §8.1 A-4 line 749; §8 seam 4 line 726.
- **Quoted:** "An Agents-owned substitute is acceptable only if it counts Conversations from a Conversations-side event feed".
- **Attack:** The six seams contain no event feed. The fallback that retires A-4 therefore depends on an unregistered dependency, which is precisely what the FR-21 gate cannot see (the predecessor's C-4 pattern).
- **Fix:** Either register the feed as seam 7 or delete the fallback.

### M-6. FR-8 runs the classifier before the free checks, and the latency gate then cannot see the checks that follow it

- **Location:** FR-8 lines 254–263; NFR-9 line 697; FR-27 line 604.
- **Quoted:** "7. Pre-Provider Content Safety Policy (FR-27). 8. Eligible Approver resolution ... 9. Agent membership". NFR-9: "fast pre-Provider rejection latency is p95 ≤ 2 seconds for ... rejections that require no content safety classifier call".
- **Attack:** A Conversation with no Eligible Approver pays a classifier scan of its whole unscanned history on every first call before being rejected `NoEligibleApprover`; the same for a blocked Conversation. Because a classifier call occurred, that rejection is excluded from the 2-second gate, so steps 8 and 9 have no latency bound at all.
- **Fix:** Order: approver resolution and membership read before the safety scan (membership *add* stays last); state that the fast-rejection gate covers every rejection reason other than a safety verdict, measured excluding classifier time.

### M-7. Disabling the Agent, or disabling its Provider/model, leaves awaiting proposals in an undefined limbo

- **Location:** FR-3 lines 161–163; FR-18 line 397; FR-4 line 178.
- **Quoted:** "Disabling an Agent does not delete existing Audit Evidence, Proposed Agent Replies, or Conversation Messages." FR-18 pre-post: "the Agent is active".
- **Attack:** Nothing blocks approving a proposal under a disabled Agent; the approval then fails pre-post re-validation, burns the retry budget in fifteen minutes, and lands in `PostingFailed` until an administrative retry after re-enable — content that was approved while the tenant had switched the Agent off. Regeneration under a disabled Agent is not blocked either (FR-16 re-runs FR-9/FR-27 only).
- **Fix:** "While the Agent is disabled or suspended, awaiting proposals accept reject and abandon only; approval and regeneration are rejected with a typed reason; `ExpiresAt` continues to run."

### M-8. Legal hold and cross-system erasure have no precedence rule

- **Location:** §9 lines 773–774, 778; §8 seam 6 line 728; FR-30 line 555; FR-33 rows lines 428–429.
- **Quoted:** "An approved deletion of a Conversation in Hexalith.Conversations triggers FR-30 approved deletion of the derived Agent content".
- **Attack:** A Conversations-side data-subject erasure arrives while a legal hold pins the interaction's DEK (the register says hold pinning "rejects DEK destruction"). The PRD does not say which wins, whether the erasure is queued, or who is told. The reverse case — Agents' 365-day erasure of a proposal while the posted message and its "human-edited by which Party" metadata persist in Conversations — is also unaddressed.
- **Fix:** "A hold takes precedence over any deletion signal; the signal is recorded as `DeletionDeferredByHold`, visible to the Compliance Inspector, and executes on hold release." State that provenance metadata persisted into Conversations is governed by Conversations retention and contains no Agents-erasable content beyond Party identifiers.

### M-9. SM-4 has no `InsufficientEvidence` condition, so an empty telemetry set passes the security gate

- **Location:** SM-4 line 829; FR-28 line 623; §11 line 818.
- **Quoted:** "Authorization tests and qualification telemetry show zero successful cross-tenant or unauthorized Agent Calls".
- **Attack:** Zero successes out of zero attempts is zero. The metric passes on a cohort that never exercised a denial path. §11 promises a measurement contract elsewhere; the PRD gives no minimum count of attempted-and-denied operations per FR-33 row.
- **Fix:** "SM-4 is `InsufficientEvidence` unless every FR-33 row has at least one recorded denial in qualification telemetry and the forged-extension test named in the Spine has run."

### M-10. Compliance and administrative inspectors can see who called and what was generated, but the FR-25 status surface tells a caller nothing about why their own call was rejected `NoEligibleApprover` in a way they can act on

- **Location:** FR-7 line 237; FR-25 line 546; FR-33 row line 427.
- **Quoted:** "a caller sees the outcome of their own Agent Calls only".
- **Attack:** The trade-off in FR-7 is "visible rather than silent" — to the operator. The Participant who was rejected gets a typed reason but no indication of what would make an Approver eligible (whose absence, which source), and the FR-7 disclosure category defaults to operator-only, so the caller cannot know whether to add a Facilitator or ask for a policy change. Adoption failure with no user-side recovery path.
- **Fix:** Define a user-visible remediation hint for `NoEligibleApprover` that discloses only the source *kind* (not the members) at the configured disclosure category.

### M-11. `PostingFailed` administrative retry does not state that it re-runs pre-post re-validation, and a safety-failed post is retried three times pointlessly

- **Location:** FR-18 lines 396–397; FR-33 row line 426.
- **Quoted:** "a failed post moves the proposal to `PostingFailed` with a typed reason and a bounded audited retry of at most 3 attempts over 15 minutes".
- **Attack:** A pre-post content-safety failure (policy tightened after approval) is not transient; three automatic retries cannot change it, and an administrative retry is not stated to re-run the FR-18 checks, so it reads as a bypass of the safety re-check.
- **Fix:** "Retry, automatic or administrative, always re-runs the full pre-post re-validation; a `PostingFailed` proposal whose reason is a safety verdict is not retryable and accepts abandon only."
