## Verdict

I would not sign off, but the gap is narrower than last time. The second update did what was asked: `RQ-1` has one enumerated input list, FR-34 gives payload protection an id, a blocker, owners, and a test, FR-2 is a five-state machine that can re-admit `hexa`, FR-7 separates authoritative from unavailable answers, FR-18 has a transition table and a `MessageId` lookup, and the kill switch has one semantics, a release row, and a minimum sample. Fifteen of the thirty prior findings are resolved, two are resolved differently in ways I accept, one is partial, and twelve deferred mediums and lows remain open by decision.

What stops sign-off is that the new machinery has its own edges, and two of them reproduce the exact failure the last gate was written to prevent. A proposal in `PostingPending` can still be system-abandoned while its post is in flight, because the `MessageId` lookup that guards the transition is consulted before the attempt has concluded — and the Spine says that transition must not exist at all. FR-1, FR-2, and the Spine give three different answers to who creates `hexa`'s Party identity, and the one that survives ("Agent Administrators can provision or link") lets a tenant role point `hexa` at any Party, including a human's. Below those: the pre-post re-validation and the kill-switch clause contradict each other about `Approved` proposals under suspension; the trigger review can now be defeated in the opposite direction (a Provider outage or a same-tuple systemic block never reaches its numerator); FR-7's authoritative/unavailable distinction is decidable only for Conversations reads while the predicate also depends on a Tenants role projection and per-Party access checks; the FR-34 blocker clears on a host's say-so; and the ARCH-A citation names a table version that does not match the Spine on disk today. One more pass, scoped to the twelve findings rated High or above below, would make this an executable requirements authority.

## Critical

### C-1. A `PostingPending` proposal can still be abandoned while its post is in flight, and the Spine says the transition must not exist

- **Location:** FR-18 table row line 414; FR-18 line 424; FR-2 lines 153, 161; §8 seam 2 line 793; Spine AD-5 (line 127).
- **Quoted:** Row: "`Approved`, `PostingPending`, `PostingFailed` → `Abandoned` (`RemovedInConversations`) — System — Removal or block detected (FR-2), and the `MessageId` lookup finds no posted message". FR-2: "Setting the block, or detecting an external removal, moves every non-terminal proposal for that Conversation to `Abandoned`". Spine AD-5: "Abandon is legal from every awaiting-decision state and from `PostingFailed`, never from `Approved` or `PostingPending`, which complete or fail on their own terms".
- **Attack:** `PostingPending` means "posting is in progress" (line 397). A Facilitator sets the block, or the hourly membership sweep finds `hexa` absent, while the posting activity has sent the append and is waiting for the acknowledgement. The guard runs the `MessageId` existence read; the message is not yet visible (or the read is served from a replica behind the write), so "finds no posted message" is true; the proposal is recorded `Abandoned`; the append then lands. Result: a message attributed to `hexa` in the Conversation whose proposal says `Abandoned` and which §9 line 843 says "cannot later be posted" — the identical outcome the last C-3(b) was raised for, now reachable through the table that was written to close it. The lookup cannot make this safe, because a negative existence answer is only meaningful after the attempt has concluded. Second, the Spine rule for the same aggregate states the opposite, and the memlog records the divergence as noted, not resolved; the PRD's own §8.1 rule (a Spine outcome absent from FR-18 is "a PRD amendment pending") does not cover a PRD outcome absent from the Spine, so the contradiction is invisible to both documents' change control. An implementer following AD-5 will reject the command the PRD requires.
- **Fix:** Make `PostingPending` uninterruptible: no transition leaves it except the posting attempt's own conclusion (acknowledged → `Posted`; typed failure or attempt timeout at least equal to the seam's posting timeout → `PostingFailed`). Apply block/removal abandonment to `Approved` and `PostingFailed` only, and to `PostingPending` deferred until it exits; state that the FR-2 abandonment "moves every non-terminal proposal" *except* one in `PostingPending`, which is abandoned on exit if it did not post. Then either amend the Spine AD-5 to allow system abandonment from `Approved` and (post-exit) `PostingFailed` on removal, or drop the `Approved` row and let the pre-post re-validation catch it — and record which in OQ-26. Extend the §8.1 reconciliation rule to both directions.

### C-2. Three authorities for `hexa`'s Party identity, and the one that survives lets a tenant role point `hexa` at a human

- **Location:** FR-1 line 132; FR-2 line 143; FR-2 line 147; UJ-1 line 50; FR-33 line 443; §6.1 line 727; §10 line 860; Spine AD-7 (line 139: "the shipped link and replace commands"); §8 seam 1 line 792.
- **Quoted:** FR-1: "The Platform Operator provisions `hexa` once per tenant ... as a create-only operation that establishes the stable Agent identity and tenant scope". FR-2: "Agent Administrators can provision or link the Agent's Party identity so `hexa` appears as a known AI participant". UJ-1: Nora "confirms the linked Party identity". FR-33: "Configure `hexa`: identity, Agent Instructions, ..." — Tenant Agent Administrator. §10: "Agent administration: configure `hexa`, lifecycle, Party identity link".
- **Attack:** (a) Which operation creates the Party in Hexalith.Parties — the Platform provision (FR-1, Spine AD-2 "with ... Party identity link") or the Administrator's "provision or link" (FR-2, §10)? A story author cannot tell whether the provision leaves `hexa` with no Party (so FR-2 line 147 "exactly one Party identity" is a *goal*) or with one (so FR-2's "provision" is dead). (b) "Link" is not constrained. Nothing requires the linked Party to be of AI type, to have been created by Agents, or to be unlinked from anything else. The Spine ships "link and replace" commands to the Administrator. A Tenant Agent Administrator can therefore replace `hexa`'s Party with an existing human Party in the tenant; FR-2 line 149 is satisfied ("attributable to the Agent's Party identity"), FR-11's "does not appear as authored by the caller" is satisfied, and every `hexa` message is now attributed to that human. Seam 1 limits *membership* to "`ParticipantType.AiAgent`", but whether Conversations verifies the Party's type against Parties is an assumption the PRD does not record; the posting seam says "as the `AiAgent` participant", which is the participant already admitted. (c) "Identity" appears in the FR-33 configure row as something the Administrator configures, contradicting FR-1's "establishes the stable Agent identity".
- **Fix:** One sentence in FR-1: "Provisioning creates `hexa`'s Party identity in Hexalith.Parties as an AI-type Party owned by the Agents Service Principal; the identity is immutable for the life of the tenant." Rewrite FR-2's first sentence to "Tenant Agent Administrators can inspect, but not change, the Agent's Party identity"; remove "identity" from the FR-33 configure row and "Party identity link" from §10's Administrator bullet, or scope them to read. Add an FR-2 consequence: "A Party of type human or organization can never be an Agent's Party identity; a link or replace naming one is a typed rejection." Record in §8 seam 1 whether Conversations verifies Party type, as an assumption row.

## High

### H-1. `Approved` proposals "complete on their own terms" under suspension, but the pre-post re-validation requires the tenant not to be `Suspended`

- **Location:** FR-28 line 673; FR-3 line 165; FR-18 line 426; FR-18 table row line 412; Spine AD-12 (line 169: a disabled Agent "has kill-switch semantics ... `Approved` and `PostingPending` complete or fail on their own terms").
- **Quoted:** FR-28: "lets `Approved` and `PostingPending` proposals complete or fail on their own terms". FR-18: "Before posting, the system re-validates that ... the Agent is `Active`, its tenant not `Suspended`, ... a failure moves the proposal to `PostingFailed`". Table: "`Approved` → `PostingPending` — System — Pre-post re-validation passed".
- **Attack:** An `Approved` proposal's "own terms" include the re-validation, and the re-validation fails on `Suspended` and on `Disabled`. So under the text every `Approved` proposal at pull time goes to `PostingFailed` (consuming one of three retry attempts), the retry clock is paused, and it resumes after release — which is a defensible design, but it is not what FR-28, FR-3, and the Spine say, and an implementer who follows those three will post approved content tenant-wide *after* a confirmed cross-tenant security event (the SM-4 trigger), which is the one moment a kill switch exists for. A tester cannot write the test: does an `Approved` proposal post during suspension or not?
- **Fix:** Decide for the re-validation (safety wins): "While the tenant is `Suspended` or the Agent `Disabled`, an `Approved` proposal does not start posting; it waits in `Approved` with the retry clock not running, and re-validates when the condition lifts. A `PostingPending` attempt already in flight completes or fails on its own terms." Delete "complete or fail on their own terms" for `Approved` from FR-28 and FR-3, and amend Spine AD-12 to match. State whether the wait consumes a retry attempt (recommend: no).

### H-2. The trigger review now hides real outages: a Provider outage is in neither trigger, a same-tuple systemic block never reaches the numerator, and the review has no owner or deadline

- **Location:** FR-28 lines 675–677; SM-C4 line 920; FR-12 line 328; NFR-10; FR-33 line 453.
- **Quoted:** "either the blocked-call share (SM-C4) exceeds 50% — counted over distinct (Party, Conversation, reason) tuples, with rate-limit rejections and repeat rejections of the same tuple within the window excluded — or the posting-failure rate exceeds 10% ... the share of proposals that entered `PostingFailed` at least once in the window over proposals that entered `Approved` in it ... Below the minimum sample the trigger evaluates to `InsufficientEvidence` and is reported, not acted on." SM-C4: "Provider and generation failures are failed calls, tracked separately, not blocked calls."
- **Attack:** (a) The numerator is distinct tuples; the denominator is not stated, and SM-C4's is Agent Calls. A systemic condition that blocks everyone for one reason (a mis-published Content Safety Policy that fails every history; a Conversation Context Policy misconfiguration) produces one tuple per (Party, Conversation) however many times users retry: 10 Parties × 5 Conversations = 50 tuples over 500 calls = 10%. A 100% outage reads as 10%. (b) A Provider that fails every generation for seven days is a "failed call", not a blocked call, and never enters `Approved`, so it is in neither numerator. The launch-health primary for Automatic-mode tenants is "SM-C4 and the posting-failure rate" (line 909) — neither sees it. (c) Rate-limit rejections are excluded wholesale, so a rate limit lowered to one call per day by a Tenant Agent Administrator (FR-33 line 449 lets them lower without bound) is an invisible tenant-wide outage. (d) A tenant with four users never reaches five distinct Parties; its outages are permanently `InsufficientEvidence`. (e) "A trigger review, mandatory when ..." names no reviewer, no convening deadline, and no record when it does not convene; "the Release Operator pulls the switch only as the recorded decision of that review" means a review that never happens never pulls. "Confirmed SM-4 event" names no confirmer.
- **Fix:** State the denominator as distinct (Party, Conversation) tuples that attempted a call in the window. Add a third trigger: Provider/generation failure rate above X% over the same window and sample. Exclude only rate-limit rejections *at the Platform/Release-configured limit*; a Tenant-lowered limit counts. For tenants below the sample, evaluate the trigger over the platform cohort or lower the sample for tenants with fewer than N Parties. Name the reviewer (Release Operator) and a convening bound (within one business day of the condition), and record a non-convened review as `TriggerReviewOverdue` on the FR-30 surface. Name who confirms an SM-4 event (Platform Operator, recorded).

### H-3. Two `PostingFailed` exits can never be satisfied as written, so "always has an exit" is false and the retention clock never starts

- **Location:** FR-18 lines 423–424; table rows lines 414–417; FR-33 line 458; FR-7 line 246; §8 seam 2 line 793; register EXT-CONV-AI-1 `RequiredArtifact` (register line 55).
- **Quoted:** FR-18: "or the system abandons it under FR-7 on an authoritative answer that the Source Conversation is gone. A `PostingFailed` proposal therefore always has an exit". "If the lookup itself is unavailable, the exit is refused with a typed reason and the proposal stays `PostingFailed`." FR-33: "Abandon a `PostingFailed` proposal whose Source Conversation is gone or inaccessible (FR-18) — Tenant Agent Administrator; audited; no Conversation read access required". Register seam 2: "append a Conversation Message as the `AiAgent` participant with an Agents-supplied deterministic `MessageId` and idempotency key ... so restart or replay cannot duplicate a post" — no existence read.
- **Attack:** (a) The FR-33 row exists for the case where the Conversation is gone or inaccessible. Its table guard is "the `MessageId` lookup finds no posted message". When the Conversation is deleted or the Agents Service Principal is denied, what does the existence read return — an authoritative "no such message" (exit permitted) or "unavailable/denied" (exit refused)? The PRD classifies neither, so the row written for exactly this case may be permanently unsatisfiable, and the proposal — approved content — sits outside the §9 365-day clock forever. (b) The fourth exit, system abandonment "under FR-7", has no table row (the system row at line 414 is for removal/block only, and the FR-7 re-check runs "over every awaiting proposal", not over `PostingFailed`). (c) The register's seam 2 does not contain the existence read the PRD says it contains; the PRD says the register owns names, so `EXT-CONV-AI-1` can reach `Available` with a compatibility command that never exercises the read — and then every `PostingFailed` exit is "refused with a typed reason" because the lookup is unavailable by construction.
- **Fix:** Classify the lookup's answers: "an authoritative Conversation-deleted or Principal-denied answer counts as 'finds no posted message' for the abandon exits (the message, if any, is governed by Conversations retention) and as unavailable for the retry exits." Add the table row "`PostingFailed` → `Abandoned` (`SourceConversationUnavailable`) — System — authoritative Conversation-deleted answer" and extend the FR-7 sweep to `PostingFailed`. Update the register's seam 2 and compatibility contract to include the existence read (a correct-course item, but the PRD should not claim the register says something it does not).

### H-4. The authoritative/unavailable distinction is decidable only for Conversations reads; the predicate also depends on a role projection, per-Party access checks, and a credential that can be "denied" transiently

- **Location:** FR-7 lines 239, 243, 246; §3 Eligible Approver line 103; Spine AD-30 (line 277: roles "re-read from the Tenants-projection role assignment at every command ingress"); AD-12 (line 169: "Tenant access comes from Agents' local Tenants projection").
- **Quoted:** "It moves the proposal to `Abandoned` ... only on an authoritative answer: Conversations confirms that the Conversation is deleted or that the Agents Service Principal is denied, or returns the roster and the Eligible Approver set resolved from it is empty."
- **Attack:** (a) The roster is one input. A tenant-role source resolves "holders" from the Tenants projection, and every candidate must "hold current read access". If the Tenants projection is stale or unavailable, or the per-Party access check fails closed for every Party, the roster read is authoritative and the resolved set is empty — the sweep abandons on evidence that is missing, not absent. The seams in §8 offer no read whose answer distinguishes "no holders" from "holders unknown". (b) "The Agents Service Principal is denied" is an authoritative answer under this text. A denial is also what a rotated or misconfigured Service Principal credential produces on every read for the duration of the incident; the hourly sweep abandons every awaiting proposal in every tenant, irreversibly, versions preserved and decisions lost. The prior C-3 fix asked for "an authoritative Conversation deleted / Party removed answer"; "denied" is broader than either. (c) Authoritative-but-momentary: the sole Facilitator of a Conversation leaves and rejoins within an hour (or the only other Approver of an `Edited` proposal steps out); a sweep in that window abandons. The design gives no grace and no second confirmation before an irreversible transition.
- **Fix:** Define "authoritative" as a typed Conversations answer of exactly `ConversationDeleted` or `PrincipalRemovedFromConversation` (not a generic 401/403), with generic denial treated as unavailable; require every secondary input (Tenants role projection, access check) to report fresh-and-available or the pass records `ResolutionUnavailable`; require two consecutive authoritative-empty passes at least one cadence apart before `NoEligibleApprover` abandonment, and record the first as `ResolutionEmptyPending` on the FR-25 surface.

### H-5. The FR-34 blocker clears on a host-asserted signal, so a host can lie and the runtime enables production-like generation on unprotected content

- **Location:** FR-34 lines 701–705; FR-28 item 6 line 660; FR-30 line 596; register EXT-PROTECTION-1 (line 174: "Command: `TBD`").
- **Quoted:** "Launch readiness emits the additive blocker `PayloadProtectionUnavailable` ... until the host reports a production protection engine through a host-supplied availability signal, and production-like generation cannot be enabled while that blocker stands." Test: "the same run with a production engine bound must clear the blocker with no other change."
- **Attack:** The blocker evaluates a signal, not behaviour. A host that wraps the no-op default in an adapter that reports "production" clears the blocker; the test in line 705 passes, because it only checks that a bound engine clears it. `RQ-1` item 6 would catch this through the compatibility command — but the command is `TBD` in the register, and production-like generation enablement (real tenant Conversations) is gated by the runtime blocker on the FR-30 surface, not by `RQ-1`. The Platform Operator, who binds the engine, is the same role that would benefit from a quick "available". The Release Operator "cannot clear the blocker by recording" but can enable production-like generation the moment the signal flips. Nothing records what engine identity and version the signal named.
- **Fix:** Replace the signal with an attestation plus a self-test: at startup and on every readiness evaluation, Agents seals a canary payload, verifies the persisted bytes contain no plaintext, unseals it, destroys the DEK, and verifies the field replays as `Erased`; the blocker clears only when the self-test passes and the engine reports an identity and version equal to the register's committed target; the attestation is recorded as Audit Evidence on the readiness record. Make the FR-34 test assert that a wrapper around the no-op default that reports "production" still leaves the blocker standing.

### H-6. The ARCH-A citation is already wrong on disk, the owner filter drops Security-owned rows, Architecture can block `RQ-1` indefinitely, and the Spine still contradicts three FRs

- **Location:** §8.1 line 811; FR-28 item 9 line 663; §0 line 16; Spine ARCH-A table (lines 737–746); Spine AD-5 (line 127), AD-12 (line 169), AD-22 (line 229).
- **Quoted:** §8.1: "the Architecture Spine at ... (frontmatter `updated: 2026-09-09`, at which version the table holds `ARCH-A-1` through `ARCH-A-7`)". FR-28: "every `ARCH-A-n` row in the Architecture Spine table, whose owner includes Product, Architecture, or Governance is retired". Spine AD-12: the Release Operator pulls "only on the recorded triggers (blocked-call share above 50 percent or posting failures above 10 percent sustained seven days)".
- **Attack:** (a) The Spine with frontmatter `updated: 2026-09-09` holds ten rows, not seven; the PRD's version anchor is false the day it was written, so a reader cannot tell which Spine the PRD approved. (b) The owner filter is "Product, Architecture, or Governance". `ARCH-A-9` — "no numeric rate bound is set" on the security event log, a burst-volume risk — is owned by "Security" and therefore blocks nothing; `ARCH-A-4` is owned by "Stack"/Architecture and is retired when Story 5.6 "records a permanent override reason". The Spine is `status: final` and will be read by implementers as binding. (e) §0 says "every unretired ... assumption in §8.1, including the `ARCH-A` rows" while §8.1 says the ARCH-A rows are *not* in §8.1's table — a small thing that makes the `UnretiredAssumption` "naming the row and its table" vocabulary ambiguous.

Make the blocker filter "every unretired `ARCH-A` row regardless of owner", or say why a Security-owned row is not a launch blocker. Add: "An `ARCH-A` row added after `RQ-1` evaluation is scheduled requires a recorded Release PM acknowledgement to block; every Architecture-owned row carries a target retirement date." Make the reconciliation rule bidirectional: "a Spine rule that narrows or contradicts an FR-18, FR-24, FR-28, or FR-33 rule is a Spine defect to be corrected before the consuming story is `ready-for-dev`", and list the three known divergences as open items with owners.

## Medium

### M-1. `ExternallyRemoved` has no clearing authority, no re-admission path when `hexa` is re-added directly in Conversations, and the state set does not cover pre-emptive blocks

- **Location:** FR-2 lines 153, 160–161; §3 line 97; FR-33 line 448; OQ-16 line 946 (`Joined → (ExternallyRemoved | Blocked) → ReadmitPending`).
- **Quoted:** "record `ExternallyRemoved`, set the Agents-owned block"; "A block is cleared only by the authority that set it or by the Tenant Agent Administrator"; "exactly five states".
- **Attack:** (a) The authority that "set" an `ExternallyRemoved` block is the system; only the Tenant Agent Administrator can clear it. A Facilitator who removed `hexa` in Conversations and re-adds it there directly (seam 1 is the ordinary participant add) sees `hexa` present in the roster while every call is rejected `RemovedInConversations`, with no self-service recovery — the machine never re-reads presence from `ExternallyRemoved`. (b) "Record `ExternallyRemoved`, set the ... block" is two facts against "exactly five states": is the state `ExternallyRemoved` or `Blocked`, and does the outbox mirror a removal of a participant that is already absent (seam 1 returns a "typed conflict"; is that a confirmed mirror or a permanent `MirrorPending`)? (c) OQ-16's machine has no edge into `Blocked` from `NeverJoined` or `ReadmitPending`; FR-33's block row does not say the Conversation must be one `hexa` joined. A tester cannot decide whether a pre-emptive block is a typed rejection or a valid state.
- **Fix:** Add the edges `NeverJoined → Blocked`, `ReadmitPending → Blocked`; state that `ExternallyRemoved` is cleared by the Tenant Agent Administrator or by any Facilitator of that Conversation (the removal authority), and that an `ExternallyRemoved` Conversation whose participant read shows `hexa` present again at the next call moves to `ReadmitPending` automatically (a Facilitator re-adding `hexa` is the clearest possible re-admission signal). State that the mirror for `ExternallyRemoved` is a no-op recorded as confirmed, and that a seam-1 "already absent" conflict confirms a mirror.

### M-2. Mirror/clear race and Automatic-mode block race

- **Location:** FR-2 line 160 ("the mirror is retried until it succeeds or the block is cleared"); FR-12 line 321; FR-11; Spine AD-12.
- **Attack:** (a) Block set, outbox removal in flight (Conversations slow), block cleared (`ReadmitPending`), call accepted and `hexa` re-joined (`Joined`), then the delayed removal lands. Next call: `Joined` and absent → `ExternallyRemoved`, every proposal for the Conversation abandoned. A stale mirror has produced a wrongful removal. (b) FR-12's list of conditions that prevent automatic posting does not include the Agents-owned block; in Automatic mode a block set between acceptance and posting has no proposal to abandon, and only the Spine (AD-12 "every side-effecting step re-reads ... the per-Conversation block") prevents the post. The PRD's fail-closed claim for the block is complete only for Confirmation mode.
- **Fix:** Give each block a monotonically increasing `BlockVersion` carried on the mirror; a clear cancels outbox entries of lower version and is refused while an entry is in the "sent, unacknowledged" state; a Conversations removal that lands after a clear is treated as the mirror's late effect (state stays `ReadmitPending`), not as external removal — detect external removal only when the last mirror is confirmed and no clear is newer. Add "the Agents-owned block (FR-2)" to FR-12's list and to FR-11's posting precondition.

### M-3. The data-handling record is self-declared, "tenant acceptance" is recorded by the platform on the tenant's behalf, and the record's version key changes on every catalog write

- **Location:** FR-4 line 193; FR-33 line 442; A-10 line 824; FR-4 line 194; §9 line 848.
- **Quoted:** "a data-handling record: the Provider's data-retention term ..., its training-use status (opted out or not), its processing region, and a reference to the contractual terms ... versioned with the model's `CapabilityVersion`". FR-33: "Enable a Provider/model for a tenant; enablement records the tenant's acceptance ... — Platform Operator".
- **Attack:** (a) Every field is typed by the Platform Operator with no evidence; "a reference to the contractual terms" is a string. There is no requirement that the reference resolve, be retained, or be inspectable by the tenant. (b) The tenant's "acceptance" is a side effect of a Platform Operator action; no tenant role acts, sees the record, or can decline. A compliance stakeholder will read "records the tenant's acceptance" as consent and find that the tenant was never asked. (c) `CapabilityVersion` "acts as the optimistic-concurrency token for catalog writes" — it increments on a pricing update or limit change. Acceptance "versioned with" it therefore either expires on every unrelated write (re-acceptance storm) or silently survives a change to the retention term (acceptance of terms never seen). The PRD does not say which.
- **Fix:** Version the data-handling record separately (`DataHandlingVersion`), bumped only when one of its four fields changes. Make acceptance a Tenant Agent Administrator action against a named `DataHandlingVersion`, required before the first activation that selects the model and again when the version changes (existing calls continue under the accepted version until a stated grace expires). Require the contractual reference to be a retained document the Compliance Inspector can inspect. Keep the Platform Operator's enablement as the gate that *permits* selection, not as acceptance.

### M-4. "A subject of the case" is undefined, the Inspector decides whether the case "concerns the Administrator", and two Inspectors can approve each other

- **Location:** FR-24 line 568; FR-33 line 457; OQ-30 line 960.
- **Quoted:** "the approval of a second party who is neither the Inspector nor a subject of the case: the Tenant Agent Administrator when the case does not concern that Administrator's configuration or conduct, and otherwise the Platform Operator or a second Compliance Inspector".
- **Attack:** (a) "Subject" is not enumerable from the scope, so the system cannot enforce the row; it becomes a checkbox in the justification. Is the caller a subject? The Approver? The Facilitator? A Participant whose message is in the Conversation? (b) Whether "the case concerns the Administrator" is asserted by the Inspector in the justification; asserting it is precisely how the Inspector routes around the Administrator to a colleague. (c) Two tenant-scoped Compliance Inspectors can approve each other's inspections reciprocally with no rule against it; post-hoc review within 7 days by the colleague makes the access unilateral in practice. The Platform Operator sees only "the inspection rate and every unreviewed inspection".
- **Fix:** Define the subject set structurally and compute it: every Party recorded as caller, editor, Approver, decision actor, or Facilitator on any proposal in scope, plus the Tenant Agent Administrator whose configuration version was in force for any call in scope. Reject a second party in that set with a typed reason. Make "concerns the Administrator" a computed fact (the Administrator is in the subject set) rather than a declaration. Forbid reciprocal approvals between the same two Inspectors within a rolling 30-day window; require Platform Operator approval for any inspection wider than one Conversation.

### M-5. `MembershipUnavailable` and call-time resolution failures are outside the "exactly" list of blocked-call reasons, so a Conversations outage is invisible to launch health and to the trigger review

- **Location:** FR-2 line 155; FR-7 lines 243, 246; SM-C4 line 920; FR-25 line 584.
- **Quoted:** SM-C4: "The blocked-call reasons are exactly: Conversation Context Policy, Content Safety Policy, cost caps, rate limits, `NoEligibleApprover`, and `RemovedInConversations` or the Agents-owned block; Provider and generation failures are failed calls". FR-2: "reject the call with the typed reason `MembershipUnavailable`". FR-7: "an unavailable answer fails that action closed with a typed reason" — for "every later proposal action"; call time is not a later action.
- **Attack:** A call rejected `MembershipUnavailable` is neither a blocked call (not on the list) nor a failed call (not Provider/generation). It is counted nowhere; FR-25's per-reason count cannot show it; the trigger review cannot see it. At call time, an unavailable roster read has no named typed reason: an implementer will return `NoEligibleApprover` (false, and it inflates the structural-unavailability signal) or a generic error (uncounted).
- **Fix:** Add a third SM-C4 class, "unavailable calls" (`MembershipUnavailable`, `ApproverResolutionUnavailable`, `ContextUnavailable`), tracked per reason under FR-25 and included in the H-2 trigger as its own rate. Name `ApproverResolutionUnavailable` as the call-time typed reason.

### M-6. Kill switch: `ExpiresAt` keeps running, so a suspension longer than the expiry silently converts every awaiting proposal to `Expired` and the launch-health metric records it as a tenant that "is not approving"

- **Location:** FR-28 line 673; FR-18 line 425; SM-3 line 911; SM-C5 line 921; addendum "Freezing `ExpiresAt`".
- **Attack:** The addendum's rationale is that a proposal that waits out a suspension is stale. Accepted — but the metric consequences are not handled: SM-3's denominator keeps those proposals (only *system abandonment* leaves it), so a 48-hour suspension at the default 24-hour expiry produces a 0% SM-3 window and an SM-C5 spike, which is itself a launch-health miss that feeds "two consecutive ... misses: Product records a disable-or-continue decision". The switch manufactures the evidence for its own continuation.
- **Fix:** Exclude from the SM-3 and SM-C5 denominators any proposal whose awaiting window overlapped a `Suspended` period, or record `ExpiredWhileSuspended` as a distinct terminal reason that both metrics exclude.

### M-7. The register does not carry what the PRD now says it carries: seam 4's event-feed alternative and the FR-13 "Conversation status entry" are absent

- **Location:** §8 seam 4 line 795; A-4 line 818; FR-13 line 346; §8 line 799; register EXT-CONV-AI-1 line 55 ("(4) Active-Conversation count per tenant and window"), EXT-CONV-UI-1.
- **Attack:** The prior M-5 was resolved by making the feed "an alternative form of seam 4"; the register's seam 4 has no such alternative, so an `Available` `EXT-CONV-AI-1` still cannot retire A-4 by the fallback route. The prior M-13 (status entry not in `EXT-CONV-UI-1`) remains. Both are gate holes of the kind FR-21 cannot see, and the PRD's text implies they are closed.
- **Fix:** Either state in §8 that these register extensions are pending (a correct-course item with a date) or delete the claims until the register carries them.

### M-8. "Qualification profile" has no definition or owner, so `RQ-1` item 6 is evaluated against a list nobody in the PRD controls

- **Location:** FR-28 item 6 line 660; §8 line 787; register line 183.
- **Attack:** "Every external dependency in the qualification profile `Available`" — the profile is not defined in the PRD, the register, or FR-33. Whoever writes the profile decides which dependencies `RQ-1` checks; omitting `EXT-SAFETY-1` or `EXT-TOKEN-1` from it lets a run pass with an in-process classifier or tokenizer approximation as long as §11's Level 4 is argued for the "areas" it names.
- **Fix:** "The qualification profile is the full §8 register scope; a dependency may be excluded only by a recorded Release PM decision naming the FR consequences that will not be exercised, and that exclusion is itself an `RQ-1` blocker until Product accepts it."

### M-9. The launch readiness register still gates `RQ-1` on SM-1 through SM-6, contradicting "nothing outside it is an `RQ-1` input"

- **Location:** FR-28 line 654; §0 line 14 ("build readiness is owned by ... the launch readiness register (gate records)"); launch-readiness-register.md `LR-PRODUCT-METRICS` (lines 79, 239, 266: "Versioned SM-1 through SM-6").
- **Attack:** The PRD hands gate authority to a register whose product-metrics gate still requires SM-2 and SM-3 — metrics the PRD says "only real usage can produce". Read together, `RQ-1` can never be READY before enablement, and enablement requires READY. The consistency reviewer raised this last run; it is still open, and the PRD has no rule (as §8.1 now has for the Spine) that says what happens when the register's gate set disagrees with FR-28.
- **Fix:** Add to FR-28: "A launch readiness gate whose evidence requirement is not derivable from this input list is void for `RQ-1` purposes until reconciled; the Release Operator records the discrepancy as `GateOutOfScope`." And correct-course the register.

### M-10. Agent Instructions are now protected content with no interaction key, no terminal state, and no retention rule; configuration audit becomes content-bearing; per-message content hashes are a plaintext oracle

- **Location:** FR-34 line 706; FR-1 line 139; §9 line 845; FR-27 line 643; Spine AD-22 (per-`AgentInteraction` DEK).
- **Attack:** (a) FR-34 lists Agent Instructions as protected content. The Spine's DEK is per interaction; instructions live on the Agent aggregate, which has no interaction, no terminal state, and no 365-day clock. FR-1 requires configuration audit to record "prior value ... and new value" — instructions are therefore content in Audit Evidence, so FR-21 line 507 fails configuration closed under `PayloadProtectionUnavailable`: no tenant can configure `hexa` until the engine is `Available`. That may be intended, but it is not stated and UJ-1 implies otherwise. (b) Content hashes "are never inside the protected envelope" and survive erasure. An unsalted per-message hash of a short message ("yes", "approve the merger") lets anyone with audit access confirm whether a guessed text was in an erased Conversation.
- **Fix:** Define an Agent-level DEK with its own retention (erased when the tenant is offboarded, or 365 days after the Agent is disabled); state explicitly that configuration paths are content-bearing under FR-34. Key the FR-27 hash with a per-tenant secret (an HMAC) so it is a cache key, not an oracle, and say so in §9.

### M-11. `Unreconciled` settlement at "period close" and a 72-hour hold do not compose across a month boundary; "period close" is undefined in the PRD

- **Location:** FR-28 line 667; A-8 line 822; Spine AD-21.
- **Attack:** A reservation made on the 30th with a 72-hour `Indeterminate` hold is still within its hold when the UTC month closes. Is it settled at maximum at close (contradicting the hold), carried into the next period (contradicting "the UTC calendar month of the reserving command"), or left `Unreconciled` in a closed period? The Tenant Agent Administrator reads the PRD, and it does not say which cap the spend lands on.
- **Fix:** "Period close occurs at the end of the reserving month plus the maximum configured hold; a reservation still held at that instant is settled at the estimated maximum against the reserving month."
