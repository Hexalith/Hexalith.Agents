# PRD Quality Review — Hexalith Agents (post-update walk)

Review date: 2026-09-08 (after the same-day revision). Rubric: `.claude/skills/bmad-prd/assets/prd-validation-checklist.md`.
Calibration: launch-level, chain-top (UX, architecture, epics and stories exist downstream), brownfield extension of Hexalith.Conversations, high stakes. Graded on the current text only; items recorded as deliberate decisions in §13 (OQ-15, OQ-18, OQ-19) and the non-ascending FR ordering are excluded by instruction.

## Overall verdict

The revision landed. Every structural hole that made the previous pass unusable is now closed with normative text rather than intent: Safe Context Budget is defined and audited (§3, FR-9), cost caps have an owning requirement and surfaces (FR-32, FR-25, §10), the fail-closed context trade-off is costed and instrumented (OQ-10, SM-C4), retention/hold/export/deletion became callable operations (FR-30), and the approve→post path is no longer a single state (FR-18 `Approved`/`PostFailed`/`Posted`). What remains is second-order but real: the same normative rules now live in three places and have already drifted once (FR-28 vs OQ-6 on unknown-outcome reservations), two consumption controls introduced as hard bounds — per-Party/per-Conversation rate limits and the per-proposal regeneration ceiling — repeat exactly the gap that FR-32 was written to fix, and the new proposal states were added without reconciling "terminal state," which is the denominator SM-3 is measured on. This is a PRD an engineer can now build from; it is not yet one whose launch gate can be evaluated without interpretation.

## Decision-readiness — strong

The document decides. §13 now carries 19 rows with owner and status, and the three deferred rows state a revisit condition rather than trailing off — OQ-18 in particular states its own cost out loud ("a single historical message that fails the active policy blocks generation in that Conversation permanently, with no redaction or exclusion path"), which is the opposite of smoothing to neutral. OQ-10 is the clearest improvement over the prior pass: it names what is given up ("`hexa` is unavailable in Conversations that outgrow the budget"), why the alternative was rejected, and points at SM-C4 as the instrument that makes the loss visible. OQ-19 refuses to quietly re-score a metric it disagrees with and escalates instead — a real decision recorded as a decision.

The one place where decision-readiness still costs the reader is §13's role: rows restate normative text in full rather than pointing at the FR that holds it, so the register reads as a fourth copy of the requirements instead of a decision log with alternatives-rejected. The drift below is the predictable consequence.

### Findings
- **high** Normative text is triplicated and has already diverged (§4.10 FR-28 vs §7 NFR-10 vs §13 OQ-6) — OQ-6 resolves the unknown-outcome reservation case ("held for a bounded period and then conservatively settled at the estimated maximum ... with an audited administrative override"). FR-28, the location a story is cut from, still stops at "releases any unused reservation only after confirming that no usage occurred" and NFR-10 says only "atomic reservation plus reconciliation." An implementer reading FR-28 alone builds an indefinitely-held reservation, which is exactly the behaviour OQ-6 rejected. The same triplication exists for latency (FR-28 / NFR-9 / OQ-5) and safety (FR-26 / FR-27 / OQ-9) and will drift next. *Fix:* Add the bounded-settle sentence to FR-28 now; then convert §13 rows to pointers — decision, alternative rejected, owner, and the FR/NFR ID that holds the normative text.
- **low** UJ-1 declares `hexa` "active and callable" without the cost-cap precondition (§2.3 UJ-1 climax; §4.10 FR-28) — FR-28 makes an unconfigured cap a blocker for production-like generation, but the journey that defines "configured and ready" never mentions caps, and its edge case covers only the missing-provider path. *Fix:* Add caps to UJ-1's climax conditions and add a second edge case for the unconfigured-cap block.

## Substance over theater — adequate

Content is earned. The four UJs each drive requirements that exist (UJ-1→FR-1..FR-7, UJ-3→FR-13..FR-18, UJ-4→FR-23), the NFRs carry product-specific numbers rather than adjectives (NFR-9's four distinct latency gates with a 30-execution floor, NFR-11's RPO 0 and 15-minute recovery, NFR-14's 500 ms acknowledgement), and the addendum's competitive notes visibly shaped the boundary — the Rovo/Zoom observations map onto Non-Goals and onto FR-31's "Conversation Context is untrusted input," which is a genuine differentiator rather than a differentiation section.

Two residues remain. §0 still spends its first paragraph listing the document's own structural devices ("uses glossary-anchored terms, grouped features, globally stable ... IDs"), and §11's Evidence Level 3, "Fail-closed deferred seam," is a normative table row whose meaning appears nowhere in the PRD.

### Findings
- **low** §0 self-description and an undefined normative Evidence Level (§0; §11 Level 3) — Level 3 gates story evidence claims but is four words of jargon; the other four levels are self-explanatory and it is not. *Fix:* Define Level 3 in one clause (what a deferred seam is, what proves it fails closed); trim §0 to audience, source brief, and the downstream-authority paragraph.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis is stated and load-bearing: "AI assistance must become part of the conversation model without weakening the model's existing identity and governance guarantees" (§1), and the feature set follows from it rather than from convenience — governance-first sequencing (§6.2 defers tools, memory, and multi-Agent "until governed conversation participation is proven"), fail-closed everywhere, and a counter-metric set that now includes SM-C4 with an actual threshold. The MVP scope kind is coherent: a platform/capability bet whose scope logic matches.

The gap is in the measurement layer, and one part of it is new. The `Eligible Conversation` glossary entry added in this revision fixed the right problem — blocked calls can no longer be dropped from the denominator — but it defines the denominator as Conversations "in which a Conversation Participant *attempted* an Agent Call." SM-2 is therefore no longer "Conversation adoption": it is the share of already-attempted Conversations whose calls were accepted, and a tenant where `hexa` is invisible to everyone but a handful of users passes it as readily as one where it is broadly used. This is not the frozen-threshold complaint of OQ-19 — it is a definition added on 2026-09-08 that changed what the frozen ≥20% is a threshold *on*, without OQ-11 being amended to say so.

### Findings
- **high** The new `Eligible Conversation` definition silently changes what SM-2 measures (§3 Eligible Conversation; §12 SM-2; §13 OQ-11) — SM-2 is titled "Conversation adoption" and OQ-11 calls it "≥ 20% adoption," but with an attempt-gated denominator it measures call acceptance rate among Conversations that already adopted. Reach — the share of the cohort's Conversations where anyone tried at all — is now unmeasured by any SM, while the 50-Conversation floor is also a floor on *attempts*, so a low-reach launch cannot even be evaluated. *Fix:* Keep the attempted-but-blocked inclusion (it is correct), but split the measure: denominator = Conversations in the enabled cohort meeting a stated eligibility rule (participants authorized to call `hexa`), and record attempt rate and acceptance rate as reported sub-measures. Then amend OQ-11 to say the ≥20% threshold applies to the restated denominator, so the frozen number keeps its meaning.
- **medium** SM-C1, SM-C2, and SM-C3 remain unmeasurable (§12) — SM-C4 was given a source, a window, a per-tenant scope, and a 10% signal threshold; the other three are still single sentences of intent with no quantity or source event, so `RQ-1` cannot evaluate them and they cannot "counterbalance" anything. The contrast with SM-C4 in the same list shows the fix is understood. *Fix:* Restate each on SM-C4's pattern, e.g. SM-C2: SM-5 audit completeness must remain 100% in any window where SM-3's terminal-within-26h figure improves.
- **low** SM validation mappings overstate and under-cover (§12 SM-1, SM-2) — SM-1 still claims to validate "FR-1 through FR-12 and FR-22 through FR-28"; one tenant recording successful calls exercises none of FR-10, FR-12, or FR-27, whose whole content is the failure path. Meanwhile FR-29 through FR-32 are validated by no SM at all. *Fix:* Narrow SM-1 to the FRs its evidence exercises and add FR-32 (caps enforced) and FR-30 (governance operations callable) to SM-1 or SM-4.

## Done-ness clarity — thin

Most FRs now carry consequences an engineer can write a test against, and several are unusually good: FR-4's pricing rules ("a pricing version that starts at 1 on create and strictly increases ... a malformed, negative, decreased, or reused pricing version is a typed rejection"), FR-9's per-call budget derivation, FR-18's `Approved`/`PostFailed`/`Posted` separation, FR-29's three-stage write acknowledgement. This is the dimension that improved most.

It is still thin because three controls that the PRD treats as hard boundaries have no bound. NFR-10 states that "Per-Party and per-Conversation rate limits and a per-proposal regeneration ceiling bound the consumption a single participant can cause" — this is the sole mention of rate limits in the document. No FR defines who configures them, at what scope, with what default or range; §10 lists no surface for them; FR-25 exposes no consumption against them; no audit consequence attaches. FR-16's regeneration ceiling is "a configured per-proposal regeneration ceiling" with no default and no range, in a document that gets this exactly right one requirement later ("The default expiry duration is 24 hours. An Agent Administrator may configure a duration from 1 hour through 30 days"). This is the same class of hole that FR-32 was written this revision to close for cost caps, left open twice more.

Two smaller ones persist unchanged from the prior pass. FR-7 names four disclosure categories — "user-visible, operator-only, redacted, or omitted" — and never assigns a policy source to one, which leaves its own parity consequence ("API/client contracts and admin UI use the same disclosure category") with nothing to compare. FR-4 still contains "A documented migration state *may* allow temporary read-only inspection," a permission inside a testable-consequences list.

### Findings
- **high** Rate limits and the regeneration ceiling are enforced bounds with no bound, owner, or surface (§7 NFR-10; §4.6 FR-16; §10; §4.9 FR-25) — NFR-10 makes per-Party and per-Conversation rate limits part of the launch cost-control posture, yet the term appears once in the whole PRD. FR-16's ceiling has no default and no configurable range. Downstream cannot cut a story for either, and FR-28's readiness gate cannot tell a configured limit from an absent one — the precise failure mode FR-32 was added to prevent for caps. *Fix:* Extend FR-32 (or add one requirement beside it) covering rate limits and the regeneration ceiling: actor, scope, default and permitted range on the FR-18 expiry pattern, no-implicit-default or an explicit stated default, audit of changes, exposure through FR-25 status and §10, and a typed rejection on breach.
- **medium** The proposal state machine is never enumerated, and "terminal state" is now ambiguous (§4.6 FR-18; §12 SM-3; §3) — This revision added `Approved`, `PostFailed`, and `Posted` and stated that "Approval does not imply posting," but "terminal state" is used unqualified in FR-16, FR-18, §9, and — decisively — in SM-3, whose gate is "at least 95% ... reach a terminal state within 26 hours" with a separate posting-failure sub-limit. Whether `Approved`, `PostFailed`, and `Posted` count as terminal changes the SM-3 numerator, and `PostFailed` explicitly carries "bounded audited retry," so it is not obviously terminal at all. *Fix:* Add a glossary entry enumerating the states and marking which are terminal (`Posted`, `Rejected`, `Abandoned`, `Expired`; `Approved` and `PostFailed` non-terminal), and reference it from SM-3.
- **medium** FR-7's disclosure categories are declared but never assigned (§4.3 FR-7) — Four categories, four policy sources (Conversation Facilitator, caller, predefined Parties, tenant roles), and no mapping between them; the parity consequence and the "records the policy basis" consequence are both untestable as written. Unchanged from the prior pass. *Fix:* Add a source→category table to FR-7 (e.g. Facilitator and predefined Parties user-visible; tenant role operator-only; caller-as-approver user-visible), and state the default for an unmapped source.
- **medium** "Explicitly permitted tenant use case" still has no configuration path (§4.10 FR-26; §13 OQ-9) — Restricted categories are unblocked only by a permitted tenant use case, but neither FR-26, FR-30's policy publication operation, nor §10 provides a field to record one, so the restricted branch is unreachable and untestable. *Fix:* Make permitted use cases an explicit versioned field of the Content Safety Policy with actor and audit consequences, or state in FR-26 that V1 configures none and restricted categories are blocked outright.
- **medium** A "may" inside a testable consequence (§4.2 FR-4) — "A documented migration state may allow temporary read-only inspection" leaves downstream unable to tell whether the state exists. Unchanged from the prior pass. *Fix:* Name the state, what is readable in it and to whom — or delete the sentence.
- **low** `Rejected` and `Abandoned` are still undifferentiated (§4.6 FR-18; §4.1 FR-2; §3) — FR-18 grants both to "Authorized Approvers or system policy" without saying which actor produces which; FR-2 now adds a third producer (Agent removal abandons non-terminal proposals) and SM-3 counts both as human resolution. *Fix:* Glossary entries stating Rejected is an Approver's content judgment, Abandoned is withdrawal without one (by caller, Approver, or Agent removal), and system policy alone produces Expired.

## Scope honesty — adequate

Omissions are explicit and load-bearing: §5's eleven Non-Goals include the two that matter most ("will not make unapproved generated content a Conversation Message," "will not silently truncate Conversation Context"), §2.2 names non-users, §6.2 gives a reason with each deferral rather than a bare list, and §8's admission that "only `EXT-HOST-1` is `Committed`; the remaining entries are `Uncommitted`" — with the consequence that "every consuming story therefore remains blocked from `ready-for-dev`" — is a genuinely uncomfortable fact stated plainly. Open-items density is appropriate: three deferred rows out of nineteen on a launch-level PRD, each with an owner and a revisit trigger.

The weakness is that §6.1 was not updated alongside the new requirements. It lists neither governance operations (FR-30: legal hold, export, approved deletion), nor cost-cap configuration as a capability (FR-32), nor the acceptance/confirmation staging of FR-29 — the scope list a reader uses to size V1 now understates V1.

### Findings
- **medium** §6.1 In Scope is stale against the requirement set (§6.1; FR-29, FR-30, FR-32) — Three requirements added this revision, each with admin UI and API surface, appear in no scope line; §6.1's nearest bullet is "cost-control posture required for launch readiness," which reads as a gate rather than a build. *Fix:* Add scope lines for governance operations, cost-cap and consumption-limit configuration, and write-acceptance staging.
- **low** No journey covers the compliance operator or the governance operations (§2.1; §2.3; FR-30) — §2.1 names a "Tenant or Compliance Operator" job and FR-30 makes legal hold, export, and deletion first-class operations, but the four UJs stop at UJ-4's integrator. Nothing describes what a hold or an export looks like to the person doing it. *Fix:* Either add UJ-5 for the compliance operator, or state in §2.3 that governance operations are API-first with no dedicated V1 journey.

## Downstream usability — adequate

This dimension carries real weight here (UX, architecture, epics, and stories all source from this PRD), and it mostly holds. §0's new "Downstream authority" paragraph resolves the backlog-precedence question that previously had no answer. FR IDs 1–32 are unique with no gaps; NFR-1–14 and SM/OQ IDs are contiguous; cross-references resolve (FR-27 from FR-17, FR-32 from FR-28, NFR-13/14 from FR-22, OQ-15 from the Facilitator glossary entry, OQ-6 from FR-28). The glossary grew the entries downstream most needed — Safe Context Budget, Approved Bounded Context Behavior, Eligible Conversation, Evidence Level, Release Qualification Gate — and each section still reads standalone.

Two things still cost a downstream reader. Companion authorities remain named without paths or precedence: "readiness registry" (NFR-12), "final UX spines" (NFR-13), "external dependency register" (§8), "the epic and story set named by the current sprint status" (§0). And several terms introduced this revision — "platform Agents service principal" most importantly, since FR-2 and FR-21 both make it the acting identity — never reach the glossary.

### Findings
- **medium** Companion authorities are named but not located or ranked (§0; §7 NFR-12, NFR-13; §8) — Four external artifacts are binding on launch readiness with no canonical path, owner, required status, or conflict rule. Unresolved across three passes. *Fix:* Add a companion-authorities table to §8: artifact, path, owner, required status, and what wins on conflict with this PRD.
- **low** New terms did not reach the glossary (§3; FR-2, FR-21, OQ-16 "platform Agents service principal"; FR-29 `AuthoritativePending`/`ProjectionConfirmed`) — The service principal is the posting identity that FR-21's access check is performed against; leaving it undefined invites it being conflated with the Agent Party identity. *Fix:* Glossary entry for the service principal distinguishing it from the Agent's Party identity; the FR-29 stage names are adequately defined in place.

## Shape fit — adequate

The shape matches the product. This is a multi-stakeholder B2B governance capability, so named-protagonist UJs are load-bearing and present (Nora, Milan, Anika, Omar — each carries entry state, path, climax, resolution, and an edge case), four personas is under the theater threshold, and the brownfield references check out: `ParticipantRole.Facilitator`, `IConversationClient.AddParticipantAsync`, and `POST /api/v1/conversations/{conversationId}/participants` are named concretely, and §8 distinguishes what exists from what Conversations must still publish. Chain-top obligations are taken seriously — evidence levels, measurement contracts, and a dependency-commitment gate are all present because downstream needs them.

The one shape strain is FR-28, which has become a fourteen-bullet omnibus mixing runtime behaviour the system enforces (cost-cap reservation, latency gates) with release-process sequencing ("Qualification access does not authorize production enablement," evidence-level requirements, register reconciliation). A story cut from FR-28 cannot be scoped. The comparable FR-21 bullets are excluded here as OQ-17 explicitly reaffirmed them.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-28 mixes enforced system behaviour with release process (§4.10 FR-28; §8; §11) — Roughly half its bullets are gate procedure that no code implements and no acceptance test can execute, sitting alongside the cost-cap and latency behaviours that stories genuinely need. *Fix:* Keep in FR-28 only what the system enforces at runtime (caps, reservation, latency, `InsufficientEvidence` handling); move qualification sequencing, evidence-level requirements, and register reconciliation into §11 and §8, which already hold most of that material, and have FR-28 reference them.

Defensible for a `status: final` PRD whose inferences have been converted into the §13 Decision Register with owners and dates — the register does the work the index would. Worth a one-line note in §0 saying so, since a downstream reader looking for the index will not find one.
- **Glossary drift:** lowercase forms of glossary terms persist where the defined term is meant — "approver policy" and "response policy" (§2.1, §2.3 UJ-1, §3 Agent Administrator entry, §4.1 description, FR-22, FR-31), "automatic response mode" (§2.3 UJ-2 entry state), "confirmation response mode" (§2.3 UJ-3 entry state). Unchanged from the prior pass.
- **UJ protagonists:** all four named with inline context; no floating UJs. UJ-4 alone lacks an edge case, where the other three have one.
- **Required sections:** all present for a launch-level chain-top PRD, including the evidence authority (§11) and decision register (§13) this document's stakes demand.
