# Adversarial Review — Hexalith Agents PRD

Reviewed: `prd.md` (status `final`, updated 2026-09-08, 763 lines) and `addendum.md`. Judged as they stand; no other review or reconcile files consulted.

## Verdict

I would not sign off. The document is unusually precise about *what must never happen* (silent truncation, unapproved posting, secret leakage) and unusually vague about the things an implementer must actually decide on day one: who holds "Agent call permission", who owns cost caps, whether `hexa` is one Agent per tenant, what "accepted" means relative to membership and fail-closed checks, and how a proposal moves through its states when the Agent is disabled or the Conversation removes it. The single biggest way this PRD gets misbuilt is FR-7: read literally, its three simultaneous constraints — configuration-time rejection of any policy that "cannot satisfy" segregation of duties, rejection of any source "whose holders are not guaranteed Conversation access", and "the caller may not be the sole Approver" — make every realistic Approver Policy (Facilitator-only, a single named Party, a tenant role) either unconfigurable or capable of producing proposals no one is permitted to approve. A fully compliant implementation of §4.3 leaves Confirmation Response Mode, the product's central governance thesis (UJ-3), without a valid configuration. Close behind it: the release gate (FR-28 → `RQ-1`) is wired to adoption metrics that can only be produced after the enablement they gate.

## Findings

- **[critical]** Segregation-of-duties and access-guarantee rules make Confirmation Response Mode unconfigurable or produce orphan proposals (FR-7, UJ-3, OQ-14) — FR-7 says "The caller may not be the sole Approver of their own Agent Call, and the approving Party must differ from the Party that last edited the version being approved. A policy configuration that cannot satisfy this segregation of duties is rejected at configuration time." Whether the caller is the sole Approver is only knowable at *call* time (it depends on who calls). A Facilitator-only policy is fine for Milan's call and violated for the Facilitator's own call. Two literal readings, both wrong: (a) reject Facilitator-only at configuration time because a Facilitator *could* call — then UJ-3's default configuration is illegal and, because the same FR also rejects "a bare tenant role whose holders are not guaranteed Conversation access" (predefined Parties are equally not guaranteed access), no single-source policy survives validation; (b) accept it and at call time either reject the Facilitator's call with no stated reason code, or create a `Pending` proposal that no Party may approve, which sits until `Expired` and pollutes SM-3's expiry rate. The edit rule has the same hole: with a single Approver, the moment she edits, the proposal becomes unapprovable ("a second authorized Approver would have to approve") and nothing says edit must be refused when no second Approver exists. *Fix:* Split the rule into a configuration-time check (policy must be able to yield ≥2 distinct Parties *or* must include a fallback source) and explicit call-time and edit-time behaviors: if the resolved Approver set for this proposal is {caller} → typed rejection of the call before Provider work; if an edit would leave zero eligible approvers → typed rejection of the edit. Define "bare tenant role" or delete the phrase; decide whether predefined Parties are permitted at all under the access-guarantee rule.

- **[critical]** Adoption metrics gate production enablement but can only be produced after it (FR-28, SM-1, SM-2, §11, §12) — FR-28: "Production enablement remains blocked until `RQ-1` records READY from the required live evidence and launch metrics" and "When a primary metric evaluates to `InsufficientEvidence`, `RQ-1` records NOT READY". SM-2 is a primary metric requiring "At least 20% of eligible Conversations ... record one or more accepted Agent Calls during a rolling 30-day window, measured only when the cohort contains at least 50 eligible Conversations." Before production enablement the only source is "production-like launch validation", where the population of Conversations and the callers are the test team. Either the qualification team scripts 50 conversations with 20% calls (metric is gamed and measures nothing) or a real single-tenant launch (SM-1 needs one tenant) with fewer than 50 eligible Conversations returns `InsufficientEvidence` → NOT READY indefinitely. SM-1 is also circular: it requires the tenant "satisfies launch-readiness gates", and readiness requires SM-1. *Fix:* Move SM-2 and SM-3 out of the `RQ-1` gate into post-enablement launch-health review (they are outcome metrics, not readiness evidence); keep in the gate only what can be produced pre-enablement (latency, isolation, safety, audit completeness on the qualification cohort). Strip "satisfies launch-readiness gates" from SM-1.

- **[critical]** Audit content access rule defeats the Compliance Operator job and orphans evidence when the Conversation goes away (FR-24, §9, §2.1, FR-30) — FR-24: "Inspecting Audit Evidence that contains Conversation-derived content additionally requires current read access to that Source Conversation, and fails closed otherwise." §2.1 JTBD: "As a Tenant or Compliance Operator, prove who called the Agent, what it generated, what changed during approval, who approved it, and what was finally posted." A compliance operator is not a participant of every Conversation, so "what it generated" and "what changed" are unreadable unless the operator is injected into every Conversation, which is itself a governance violation. FR-30/§9 require an operator to "request authorized export" and apply legal hold to content the same rule forbids them to read. When Hexalith.Conversations deletes the Conversation under its own retention ("Posted Conversation Messages remain governed by Hexalith.Conversations retention"), "current read access" is impossible for everyone, so 365 days of retained Agent content becomes permanently uninspectable while still being retained. *Fix:* Define a distinct compliance-inspection authorization (tenant-scoped role, audited, justification-required) that bypasses the participant check; define the behavior when the Source Conversation no longer exists (evidence remains inspectable under compliance authorization; participant-based access is simply unavailable).

- **[high]** The stated Conversations dependency surface cannot deliver several normative guarantees (§8, FR-2, FR-11, FR-17, NFR-11, `EXT-CONV-AI-1`, `EXT-CONV-UI-1`) — §8 limits `EXT-CONV-AI-1` to "`IConversationClient.AddParticipantAsync` and `POST /api/v1/conversations/{conversationId}/participants`" and `EXT-CONV-UI-1` to the **Call hexa** action contribution. Nothing in the register scope covers: (1) posting a Conversation Message *as* an `AiAgent` participant with a trace reference ("The posted message references the Agent Call or equivalent trace identifier"); (2) idempotent posting so that "Restart or replay cannot duplicate ... Conversation posts" (NFR-11) — exactly-once across a process boundary requires an idempotency key on the Conversations side; (3) rendering "a participant-visible marker identifying it as AI-generated" (FR-11) and "whether the posted version was human-edited and by which Party ... exposed wherever the message's provenance is disclosed" (FR-17) — these are Conversations UI concerns; (4) notification of participant removal so that "removal ... moves every non-terminal proposal for that Conversation to `Abandoned`" (FR-2) can fire when removal happens in the Conversations UI rather than through Agents; (5) enumerating participants of every tenant Conversation to compute the "Eligible Conversation" denominator. *Fix:* Either add these to the named dependency contracts in §8 with the same nine-field commitment record, or downgrade the affected consequences to what Agents alone can guarantee (e.g., marker lives in message metadata Agents controls; removal abandons proposals only when performed through the Agents surface).

- **[high]** Direct contradiction on proposal visibility after access loss (FR-13 vs FR-7 and UJ-3) — FR-13: "Proposal discovery returns only proposals whose Source Conversation the requesting Approver can currently read; proposals for other Conversations are not listed, counted, or otherwise disclosed." FR-7: "An Approver who has lost Conversation read access may see that a proposal exists and its state." UJ-3: "she can still see that it exists, but not its content." A queue that both lists and does not list the same proposal cannot be built. *Fix:* Pick one. If existence-visibility is intended, FR-13 must say "returns full content only for readable Conversations; returns existence and state for proposals the Approver was previously authorized on" and define what "previously authorized" means (was she ever resolved as an Approver for it?).

- **[high]** Response mode is per Agent, but the journeys and counter-metrics assume per-Conversation sensitivity (FR-6, UJ-3, SM-C1, §6.2, FR-1) — FR-6 configures "`hexa` for Automatic Response Mode or Confirmation Response Mode", FR-16/FR-32 configure ceilings "per Agent", and §6.2 says only `hexa` exists. UJ-3 opens with "Anika is an Approver for a sensitive Conversation where Agent responses require confirmation", and SM-C1 says "if launch tenants choose confirmation for safety-sensitive contexts." Those sentences imply a per-Conversation or per-context mode, which no FR provides. UX will design a per-Conversation toggle; architecture will build a per-Agent flag; both comply. Compounding this, whether `hexa` is one Agent instance per tenant (FR-1 lists "tenant scope" as a field; FR-2 says "exactly one Party identity"; Parties are tenant-scoped) or one platform Agent is never stated, so "per Agent" may or may not mean "per tenant". *Fix:* State explicitly: `hexa` is instantiated per tenant; response mode is tenant-wide in V1; rewrite UJ-3 and SM-C1 so they do not imply per-Conversation choice, or add an FR for a Conversation-level override.

- **[high]** The permission model that every authorization FR depends on is never defined (FR-8, FR-20, glossary "Conversation Participant", "Eligible Conversation", UJ-1 edge case, FR-4/FR-19, FR-32) — FR-8 requires "Agent call permission"; FR-20 enforces "Agent calling"; the SM-2 denominator is Conversations where "at least one Participant was authorized to call `hexa`". No FR says where this permission lives (tenant role? per-Agent allow-list? every Conversation Participant by default?). Likewise UJ-1's edge case "If no Provider/model is available or enabled for the tenant" implies per-tenant Provider enablement, but FR-4 describes a single "Global Providers Aggregate" and FR-19 only allows leakage when "explicitly platform-scoped and authorized" — there is no per-tenant enablement FR, so either every tenant sees and can select every configured Provider (and its "secret reference" and configured flag), or an undocumented feature is required. And FR-32 lets "Authorized administrators or release operators" configure the tenant's own cost cap and the override that lifts it; if the tenant's Agent Administrator is such an administrator, the "hard enforcement boundary" is self-set and self-lifted. *Fix:* Add a short role matrix (platform operator vs tenant Agent Administrator vs Approver vs Participant) and state for each of: Agent call permission, Provider enablement per tenant, cap configuration, cap override, Content Safety Policy publication — who can do it and at what scope.

- **[high]** Safe Context Budget formula omits the caller's prompt (glossary, FR-9) — "the selected model's input limit ... minus the reserved output allowance, the Agent Instructions, and a fixed configured safety margin." FR-8 says the call carries "a user prompt or request"; FR-27 says the prompt is scanned; nothing subtracts it from the budget. An engineer who implements exactly the stated formula — and the PRD insists it is recorded as evidence "for every call" — admits a Conversation that fits the budget and then overflows the model once the prompt and any framing tokens are appended, producing a Provider rejection *after* reservation and after the fail-closed point the PRD promised. The "fixed configured safety margin" has no default or range, so it cannot be relied upon to absorb this. *Fix:* Add the caller prompt and any system framing to the subtraction; give the safety margin a default and range (e.g., 5% of input limit, 1–25%).

- **[high]** Per-Conversation safety-verdict cache is unversioned and conflicts with "future calls use the new policy" (FR-27, FR-26, OQ-9, OQ-18) — FR-27: the pre-Provider scan reuses "a cached per-Conversation verdict for already-scanned history". FR-26: "Content Safety Policy changes are auditable and affect future Agent Calls only." A new call after a policy tightening is a future call and must use the new policy, but a cache keyed only on Conversation returns the old policy's verdict for the history. Nothing keys the cache on policy version, and nothing invalidates it when a Conversation message is edited or deleted in Hexalith.Conversations. Both are silent under-scanning, which is exactly what the safety section claims can never happen. OQ-18 also relies on this cache: "a single historical message that fails the active policy blocks generation in that Conversation permanently" — permanence depends on the cache never being re-evaluated, so a fix to OQ-18 and a fix to this finding pull in opposite directions unless the cache contract is written down. *Fix:* Cache key = (Conversation, message content version, policy version); state that a policy publication invalidates cached verdicts tenant-wide; record the policy version the cached verdict came from in Audit Evidence.

- **[high]** Known-defective and deferred items sit on the release path of a `status: final` PRD (OQ-18, OQ-19, SM-3, FR-28) — OQ-19 concedes "SM-3's 26-hour terminal threshold is unattainable for any Agent configured with an expiry beyond 24 hours under OQ-3, and SM-3 measures the expiry setting rather than approval behavior", then leaves SM-3 as a primary metric that `RQ-1` must evaluate (FR-28). A tenant that sets a 7-day expiry will see undecided proposals sit `Pending` past 26 hours and fail SM-3; a tenant with the 24-hour default passes SM-3 mostly through `Expired`, which the ≤20% expiry-rate sub-threshold then fails. OQ-18 defers per-category handling of unsafe history "before production enablement" — a launch-level PRD with a launch-blocking open decision is not final. *Fix:* Either resolve OQ-19 now (define SM-3 relative to the Agent's configured expiry: "reaches a human decision within min(expiry, 26h)" and drop the fixed 26h) or mark the PRD `status: draft` until the escalation returns. Give OQ-18 an owner date, not a condition.

- **[high]** Removal of `hexa` from a Conversation is underspecified and self-defeating (FR-2, OQ-16) — "An authorized Party may remove `hexa` from a Conversation; removal blocks further Agent Calls there." Also: "The Agent joins a Conversation as a participant at first accepted Agent Call in that Conversation." After removal, the next call is either accepted (then the join rule re-adds `hexa`, defeating the removal) or rejected (then the block is permanent with no stated way to lift it). "Authorized Party" is undefined (Facilitator? any Member? Agent Administrator?). Whether the block is a Conversations-side participant state or an Agents-side flag determines whether Agents can even observe it (see the dependency finding). *Fix:* Define removal as an Agents-owned per-Conversation block with a named authority to set and clear it; state that a call to a blocked Conversation is a typed pre-Provider rejection; state whether re-adding requires the same authority.

- **[medium]** Unbounded bounds: "bounded", "configured", and "monthly" without defaults or ranges (FR-28, FR-32, FR-18, FR-16, glossary) — "a reservation whose outcome is `Unknown` is held for a bounded period"; the override "is itself bounded"; `PostFailed` has "bounded retry available"; "a fixed configured safety margin"; the regeneration ceiling has "a documented default and a valid range" documented nowhere; rate limits over "a stated rolling window" stated nowhere; "per-tenant monthly" caps with no definition of month (calendar vs rolling, timezone, treatment of a reservation that straddles the boundary). Every one of these is a knob an implementer will set arbitrarily and a QA lead cannot write a boundary test for. *Fix:* Give each a default and a range in the FR text, or move them all into a single "V1 Bounds" table referenced by ID.

- **[medium]** p99 over 30 executions is statistically vacuous (NFR-9, NFR-14, FR-28, OQ-5) — "p99 ≤ 120 seconds ... Each gate requires at least 30 production-like executions." With n=30 the 99th percentile is the maximum sample (or an interpolation between the top two). The gate is therefore either "no single run exceeds 120 s" (far stricter than intended and sensitive to one Provider hiccup) or meaningless depending on the percentile method the measurement contract picks. *Fix:* Either raise the minimum to a sample size that supports p99 (≥ 300) or drop p99 from the gate and keep p95 with n ≥ 30, stating the percentile method.

- **[medium]** `Unknown` means two different things on the public surface (FR-12, FR-23, NFR-11) — FR-23: "Public enums define `Unknown = 0`" as the sentinel for unrecognized/absent values. FR-12/NFR-11: a Provider attempt "resolves to a typed `Unknown` terminal outcome" — a real, meaningful terminal state. An integration consumer receiving `Unknown` for an Agent Call outcome cannot tell "Provider outcome undetermined, do not retry" from "server sent a newer enum value my client does not know" — and the correct client behavior for those two cases is opposite. *Fix:* Rename the Provider outcome (e.g., `Indeterminate`) and reserve `Unknown = 0` for the sentinel.

- **[medium]** `CapabilityVersion` concurrency semantics are inverted relative to convention and "not regressed" has no referent (FR-4, FR-5) — "a caller-supplied expected version below the stored value is rejected as regressed, and one above it is rejected as stale." In every optimistic-concurrency convention, a caller whose expected version is *below* the stored value is the *stale* one. Whichever labels are intended, an engineer will implement whichever reading matches their habit, and the typed error codes will disagree with the PRD or with the other team's client. FR-5 then requires at activation that "its `CapabilityVersion` is not regressed" — regressed relative to what stored value? The version the Agent was configured against? If the catalog advanced (say, input limit reduced), is the Agent's selection stale, blocked, or silently rebound? *Fix:* State the comparison in one sentence with conventional terms ("expected must equal stored; lower → stale; higher → invalid") and define what an Agent's selection pins (model id only, or model id + `CapabilityVersion`) and what happens on catalog advance.

- **[medium]** "More restrictive of two policies" and "stricter mode-specific policy" are not decidable (FR-26) — "each retry, approval-time check, and pre-post check uses the more restrictive of the initial attempt's policy and the then-current active policy" and "unless a stricter mode-specific policy is configured." Two policy documents with different category sets, thresholds, and handling rules are not totally ordered; "stricter" is not computable. The only implementable reading is "content must pass both policies", which the text does not say. *Fix:* Replace with "must pass every applicable policy version (initial attempt's and current active); a mode-specific policy is applied in addition to, never instead of, the tenant policy."

- **[medium]** FR-31's impersonation/assertion category and "attempts to redirect" are unfalsifiable and will block legitimate use (FR-31, OQ-9, OQ-18) — "blocks generated content that impersonates a named Party, or that asserts a decision, commitment, or approval on a Party's behalf." UJ-2's entire premise is `hexa` "interpreting the prior discussion"; a summary like "Milan agreed to ship Friday" asserts a commitment on a Party's behalf. No classifier separates reporting from asserting reliably, so the requirement is either a no-op or blocks summaries. Approver edits are scanned "on the same terms" (FR-17), so an Approver writing "the team approved this plan" is blocked at approval time. "Attempts to redirect the Agent through Conversation content are recorded ... as a safety outcome" — "attempt" is undefined; and if a pasted "ignore previous instructions" in history is a policy *failure*, OQ-18 makes that Conversation permanently unavailable. *Fix:* Narrow to first-person impersonation ("content written in the voice of a named Party") and state explicitly that reporting a Party's stated position is permitted; classify injection attempts as an audited *observation*, not a blocking failure, or say which.

- **[medium]** A disabled Agent's pending proposals can still be approved and posted (FR-3, FR-12, FR-18, FR-2) — FR-3 only says disabling "does not delete existing ... Proposed Agent Replies". FR-18's pre-post re-validation checks "that the Source Conversation still exists and is accessible to the posting principal, and that the approved version still passes the then-current Content Safety Policy" — not Agent lifecycle, not Party identity state, not Provider/model state. FR-12's list of blocking conditions applies to *automatic* posting only. So an administrator disables `hexa` for an incident and approvals keep posting as `hexa` for up to 30 days. *Fix:* Add Agent lifecycle and Party identity to the FR-18 pre-post checks and state what disabling does to `Pending`/`Approved` proposals (freeze vs abandon).

- **[medium]** Regeneration context and pre-checks are ambiguous (FR-16, glossary "Agent Call", SM-2) — "Regeneration uses the same Source Conversation and Agent configuration" — same Conversation *identity* (so the current, longer content) or the original context snapshot? The two give different Safe Context Budget outcomes, different safety-scan cache behavior, and different audit stories. FR-16 makes regeneration "a chargeable Agent Call for cost-cap, rate-limit, and audit purposes" but does not say it re-runs the FR-9 budget check and FR-27 pre-Provider scan; and because it is an "Agent Call", SM-2's "accepted Agent Calls" may count Approver regenerations as Conversation adoption. *Fix:* State that regeneration re-builds context from the current Conversation, re-runs every pre-Provider gate, and is excluded from SM-2's numerator.

- **[medium]** Readiness authority is a register document while FR-30 requires a callable runtime operation (FR-28, FR-30, §8, §10) — "The external dependency register's launch-readiness gate records are the readiness authority. Any per-Agent readiness record is transitional and must reconcile to that authority." FR-30: "Authorized operators can inspect launch-readiness state and its blockers" via a public, versioned contract. The register is a planning-artifact markdown file; the runtime cannot read it as an authority, so there will be two sources of truth with a hand-wavy "reconcile" between them, and the public API will report whatever someone last typed in. *Fix:* Decide whether readiness state is a governed runtime aggregate (register is an input, API is authority) or a document (API exposes a read-only mirror with a "last synced" stamp and is explicitly non-authoritative).

- **[medium]** "Accepted Agent Call" and the position of membership in the fail-closed sequence are undefined (FR-2, FR-9, FR-27, FR-28, OQ-16) — Membership happens "at first accepted Agent Call ... before generation." FR-9's oversized case "fails closed before Provider invocation and creates neither Provider work, a Proposed Agent Reply, nor a Conversation Message" — membership is not in that list. If "accepted" precedes the budget, cost-reservation, and safety pre-scan, `hexa` joins (visibly, per FR-2) Conversations it can never answer in, permanently for oversized ones (OQ-10). The clause "never as a side effect of an unapproved proposal" is vacuous: in Confirmation mode the join always precedes and outlives a rejected proposal. *Fix:* Define "accepted" as "passed authorization, lifecycle, rate-limit, cost reservation, context budget and pre-Provider safety gates"; make membership the last step before Provider invocation; delete or rewrite the vacuous clause.

- **[medium]** Agent Call has no enumerated state machine while FR-25 requires distinguishing its outcomes (FR-8, FR-10, FR-12, FR-25) — FR-18 enumerates proposal states with terminality rules; nothing does the same for the Agent Call (accepted, blocked-by-{authz, context, safety, cost, rate}, generating, `Unknown`, generated, posted, post-failed). FR-25 nevertheless requires status to "distinguish configuration errors, authorization failures, context policy failures, content safety failures, Provider failures, generation failures, pending approvals, and posting failures" and FR-23 requires stable public enums. Each team will invent its own. *Fix:* Add an FR-8 consequence enumerating Agent Call states and which are terminal, mirroring FR-18.

- **[medium]** 365-day cryptographic erasure breaks the traceability guarantee and diverges from Conversations retention (§9, OQ-8, FR-24, FR-3) — FR-24: "Every posted Agent Response can be traced back to caller, Agent, Source Conversation, Provider/model, generated content." §9: "Sensitive Agent content is retained for 365 days after the interaction reaches a terminal state" then "cryptographically erases or redacts protected sensitive payloads", while "Posted Conversation Messages remain governed by Hexalith.Conversations retention." On day 366 the posted message is still in the Conversation, the generated content and edited versions that explain it are gone, and the audit trail the compliance JTBD depends on is a tombstone. FR-3's "Disabling an Agent does not delete existing Audit Evidence" reads as a durability promise that §9 quietly caps. *Fix:* State which fields are "sensitive payload" (content) and which are structural (identities, versions, decisions, hashes) and that structural evidence survives erasure so the trace remains provable via content hash.

- **[low]** Glossary "Agent Response ... becomes a Conversation Message only after automatic posting or approval" contradicts FR-18's `Approved` ≠ `Posted`. *Fix:* "after posting".

- **[low]** "Expiry" is overloaded: proposal `ExpiresAt` (FR-18) vs retention expiry that "legal hold suspends" (§9). An engineer can read legal hold as freezing proposal expiry. *Fix:* Use "retention period" in §9.

- **[low]** FR-7 authorizes "expiry-resolution actions" that do not exist: FR-18 makes `Expired` automatic "on every read and every command". *Fix:* Delete the phrase or define the action.

- **[low]** FR-29 `Submitted` stage cannot be reported by a synchronous API response, yet "Admin UI and API/client contracts report the same stages ... for the same write." NFR-14's 500 ms "authoritative pending acknowledgement" requires a durable EventStore append inside 500 ms p95; state that explicitly or relax it. *Fix:* Say `Submitted` is a client-observed state and that the API's first response is `AuthoritativePending` or a typed rejection.

- **[low]** FR-7 re-checks Conversation access "at discovery, edit, regeneration, approval, and audit-content inspection" but not rejection or abandonment; whether an access-lost Approver can reject is unspecified. *Fix:* Add them to the list or state the exception.

- **[low]** FR-1 "prior value where safe to expose" — "safe" undefined; the only unsafe field is a secret reference, which FR-4 already excludes. *Fix:* Name the excluded fields.

- **[low]** NFR-13 makes "The final UX spines" "binding launch authority" without naming or locating them, and "fail closed for high-impact actions when the viewport cannot present required context" has no definition of required context (is approval on a phone refused?). *Fix:* Reference the UX artifact path and define the minimum context set for approval.

- **[low]** SM-1 claims to validate "FR-1 through FR-12 and FR-22 through FR-32" — one tenant making successful calls says nothing about FR-30 governance operations, FR-31 injection resistance, or FR-29 write staging. *Fix:* Trim to FR-1..FR-6, FR-8, FR-11/13.

- **[low]** Glossary "Conversation Context — The authorized Source Conversation content" — authorized for whom? The service principal loads the whole Conversation; if Conversations ever supports partial visibility, the caller receives an answer derived from content they cannot see. *Fix:* State "content visible to the caller at call time" or "the entire Conversation, which V1 assumes is uniformly visible to all Participants".

- **[low]** FR-26's always-block category "cross-tenant or unauthorized personal/Conversation data" applied to the pre-Provider input scan is incoherent: the Conversation's own content is by definition authorized in its own Conversation, and a classifier cannot know authorization. *Fix:* Scope the category to generated output only.

## Notes

- `EXT-*` status: §8 says only `EXT-HOST-1` is `Committed`; every consuming story is blocked from `ready-for-dev` under FR-21/OQ-17. The PRD is therefore `final` for a product no story can currently start. That is a process fact, not a defect in the text, but it makes the many "production-like" evidence requirements untestable for the foreseeable future.
- Evidence Level 4 requires "an actual ... Provider ... process". Every Level 4 run costs real Provider money and counts against whatever cap the qualification tenant has; nothing exempts qualification from FR-32's "unconfigured cap blocks production-like generation", so qualification needs its own configured caps. Worth a sentence.
- NFR-9's 2 s pre-Provider rejection includes the context-budget check, which requires loading and tokenizing the entire Conversation. For Conversations near the budget on large models this is the slowest pre-Provider step; the gate is testable but may be unrealistic for the exact cases it matters most.
- NFR-11 "Provider attempts are at-most-once" is tautological per attempt; the intended invariant is "at most one Provider attempt with possible usage per Agent Call". FR-28 already states this correctly; NFR-11 should mirror it.
- `Approved` → `Expired` is possible when `ExpiresAt` passes between approval and post; SM-3 then counts a human-decided proposal as `Expired`. Small, but it argues for freezing `ExpiresAt` semantics at `Approved`.
- The addendum is explicitly non-normative and consistent with the PRD; nothing to report.
