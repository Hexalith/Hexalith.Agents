# PRD Quality Review — Hexalith Agents

Reviewed as of the 2026-09-08 revision (frontmatter `status: final`, `updated: 2026-09-08`). Judged against the PRD quality rubric as the document stands now; `addendum.md` was read as context.

## Overall verdict

This is a rigorous, chain-top PRD: the thesis in §1 is specific, every FR carries testable consequences, the Decision Register names what was given up (OQ-10, OQ-17, OQ-18), and the glossary and ID scheme are clean enough for UX, architecture, and story extraction to source from directly. What is at risk is the `final` label rather than the content: the PRD's own register records that a primary success metric is unattainable under a permitted configuration and that the metric set does not validate the thesis (OQ-19), §8 records seven of eight critical external dependencies as `Uncommitted`, and the document carries zero `[ASSUMPTION]` tags while naming external contract members that do not yet exist. The PRD is decision-ready as a build specification and not yet decision-ready as a launch instrument; the fixes are concentrated in §12 and §13, not spread across the FRs.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

The PRD states decisions as decisions. §13 is a binding register with owners, dates, and status, and the deferred rows are honestly marked ("must not be read as settled"). Trade-offs are named with the cost, not just the choice: OQ-10 says outright that "`hexa` is unavailable in Conversations that outgrow the budget, in exchange for never sending a silently reduced context to a Provider" and points at SM-C4 as the visibility mechanism; OQ-17 refuses to retroactively legitimize work done against `Uncommitted` dependencies; OQ-18 admits that "a single historical message that fails the active policy blocks generation in that Conversation permanently." A reader pushing back would find the objection already on the table.

The weakness is that one recorded tension is left standing inside a `final` document where it disables a primary gate. OQ-19 records that "SM-3's 26-hour terminal threshold is unattainable for any Agent configured with an expiry beyond 24 hours under OQ-3" and that "SM-3 measures the expiry setting rather than approval behavior" — and then defers it. FR-28 makes `RQ-1` depend on live SM attainment, and FR-28's last consequence says an `InsufficientEvidence` primary metric forces NOT READY. So for any tenant that uses the 1-hour-to-30-day expiry range OQ-3 explicitly permits, the launch gate is either mechanically failing or measuring the wrong thing, and the PRD knows it. Escalation is the right process; leaving the metric in force as written is not a decision a release PM can act on.

A second, smaller tension is unresolved between configuration-time and call-time approval authority in FR-7. The PRD rejects at configuration time "a policy configuration that cannot satisfy this segregation of duties" and "a configured source that cannot satisfy the Conversation-access requirement at call time," but both properties depend on the membership of a Conversation that does not exist at configuration time. What the system does when the resolved Approver set at call time is empty, or consists only of the caller, is never stated.

### Findings

- **[critical]** Primary metric SM-3 is known-unattainable under permitted configuration and left in force (§12 SM-3; §13 OQ-3, OQ-19; FR-28) — OQ-3 permits expiry "from 1 hour through 30 days"; SM-3 requires "95% of Proposed Agent Replies ... reach a terminal state within 26 hours" and counts `Expired` as terminal, so the metric measures expiry configuration, not approval behavior, and cannot be met by a 30-day-expiry Agent unless 95% are decided by humans within 26 hours regardless. OQ-19 records both defects and defers them. A `final` PRD whose launch gate depends on a metric it declares broken cannot support a READY/NOT READY decision. *Fix:* Either (a) re-express SM-3 in terms of human-decision latency (e.g. "95% of proposals receive an Approver decision — `Approved`, `Rejected`, or `Abandoned` — within N hours of creation, measured on proposals whose expiry is ≥ N hours") with expiry rate as a separate counter-metric, or (b) constrain OQ-3's range to ≤ 24 hours for launch tenants and re-open the range post-launch. Whichever is chosen, mark SM-3 as provisional in §12 until the OQ-19 escalation lands, so `RQ-1` is not evaluated against a metric the PRD disowns.
- **[medium]** Call-time Approver resolution has no stated outcome for empty or caller-only sets (FR-7 consequences 9–11; FR-21) — FR-7 says segregation-of-duties and Conversation-access failures are "rejected at configuration time," but a policy source such as "the Conversation Facilitator" resolves to zero Parties in a Conversation with no Facilitator, and to the caller alone when the caller is the Facilitator. FR-21's list ("missing, stale, ambiguous, disabled, or unavailable") does not obviously cover "resolves to an empty or disqualified set." *Fix:* Add a FR-7 or FR-13 consequence: "If the Approver Policy resolves to no Party eligible under the segregation and Conversation-access rules at the moment of the Agent Call, the call fails closed before Provider invocation with a typed reason, and the failure is recorded under FR-12's authorization category."

## Substance over theater — strong

The four UJs each carry real decisions: UJ-3's "no Party may approve a version it last edited" is the origin of FR-7's segregation rule and FR-17's human-edited provenance; UJ-4's "typed result before any side effect" drives FR-20, FR-23, and FR-29; UJ-1's edge case drives FR-5's eligibility set. No persona is decorative and none exceeds the four-persona ceiling. The Vision is not swappable — it names `hexa`, Party identity, the Conversation model, and the specific things V1 refuses to do. The addendum's differentiation (governed participation versus summarization) is grounded in named competitors and is reflected in the actual design of Proposed Agent Replies and Approver Policy, not left as a positioning paragraph.

NFRs are mostly earned. NFR-9, NFR-11, NFR-12, and NFR-14 carry numbers and sample sizes; NFR-8 and NFR-10 carry hard behavioral rules. NFR-1 through NFR-7 are closer to category statements, but each is concretely instantiated by FRs (NFR-6 by FR-4 and FR-22, NFR-3 by FR-10/FR-12/FR-18), so they read as headings over real content rather than boilerplate.

### Findings

- **[low]** SM-6 is a restated requirement, not a metric (§12 SM-6) — "Core administration and workflow operations are available through both admin UI and API/client contracts with the same authorization outcomes" is FR-22 consequence 1 and FR-20 consequence 2 restated as a success metric; it has no threshold, cohort, or window, unlike every other SM. *Fix:* Either drop SM-6 (parity is already gated by FR-20/FR-22 evidence) or restate it as a measurable parity check, e.g. "100% of operations in §10 have a passing parity test in the Level 4 evidence manifest."

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The PRD has a thesis and bets on it: §1 says "AI assistance must become part of the conversation model without weakening the model's existing identity and governance guarantees," and the feature set follows — Party identity (FR-2), governed Provider catalog (FR-4/5), approval workflow (FR-13–18), fail-closed context (FR-9), untrusted-content boundary (FR-31). The MVP scope kind is problem-solving/platform ("prove governed participation first," §2.2 and addendum), and §6.2's deferrals match it. Counter-metrics are present and well chosen; SM-C4 in particular is the honest cost of the OQ-10 decision made measurable.

The gap is between the thesis and the primary metrics, and the PRD says so itself. OQ-19 records "the finding that they measure adoption and throughput rather than whether governed AI participation is valued." SM-1 is a one-tenant existence proof, SM-2 is call frequency, SM-3 is workflow throughput. None measures whether attribution, approval, or audit changed anything for a Conversation — whether approvers reject or edit at a rate that shows governance is doing work, whether posted replies are acted on, whether tenants that could choose Automatic mode choose Confirmation for the reasons the thesis predicts. The rubric names exactly this pattern (activity metrics under an engagement-quality thesis) as a tell.

### Findings

- **[high]** Primary metrics measure activity, not the thesis, and the PRD concedes it (§12 SM-1–SM-3; §13 OQ-19) — the launch gate `RQ-1` will pass or fail on adoption and throughput while the bet in §1 is about governance not weakening the Conversation model. The counter-metrics are closer to the thesis than the primaries are. *Fix:* Add at least one primary metric that a governance-value hypothesis would falsify — e.g. edit-or-reject rate on Proposed Agent Replies within a band (too low means approval is rubber-stamping; too high means generation is unusable), or share of Conversations where an Agent Response is followed by a human reply within the window — and demote SM-2 to secondary. The measurement-contract machinery in §11 already exists to carry it. If Product declines, record the accepted risk in OQ-19 as a decision, not a deferral.

## Done-ness clarity — strong

This is the PRD's best dimension. Every FR has a consequences block and almost all consequences are verifiable: FR-4's pricing rules ("a pricing version that starts at 1 on create and strictly increases"), FR-9's budget derivation, FR-18's explicit state machine with `Approved`/`PostFailed`/`Posted` distinguished, FR-26's enumerated always-block categories, FR-29's three write stages, NFR-9 and NFR-14 with percentiles and minimum sample counts. Adjective-only requirements are rare and mostly confined to admin-visibility statements. An engineer can write acceptance tests from most of this directly.

The residual gaps are of two kinds. First, the PRD uses the word "bounded" or "documented" in places where its own standard elsewhere is a number: the `Unknown`-reservation hold period (FR-28, OQ-6), the `PostFailed` retry bound (FR-18), the regeneration ceiling's "documented default and a valid range" (FR-32) — contrast expiry, which gets "24 hours ... 1 hour through 30 days." Second, one term that anchors both a primary metric and every latency gate — "accepted" Agent Call — is not defined. SM-2 counts "accepted Agent Calls"; NFR-9 and OQ-5 time from "accepted-call"; nothing in §3 or FR-8 says which check marks acceptance (authorization? membership join? reservation? context-policy pass?). The choice moves the SM-2 numerator and the NFR-9 start timestamp.

### Findings

- **[medium]** "Accepted Agent Call" is undefined but anchors SM-2 and every NFR-9 gate (§3; FR-8; NFR-9; §12 SM-2; §13 OQ-5) — the PRD uses "accepted" six times as a measurement boundary without saying which of authorization (FR-8), membership join (FR-2), cost reservation (FR-28), or context-policy pass (FR-9) constitutes acceptance. Each is a different timestamp and a different denominator. *Fix:* Add a glossary entry "Accepted Agent Call — an Agent Call that has passed authorization, Agent lifecycle, Party identity, cost reservation, and Conversation Context Policy checks and has been durably recorded; the acceptance timestamp is the start of NFR-9 measurement and the SM-2 counting event." Adjust if the intended boundary differs.
- **[medium]** Cost- and context-critical bounds are named but not numbered (FR-9 consequence 3; FR-28 consequence 3; §13 OQ-6) — the "fixed configured safety margin" that decides when `hexa` fails closed has no default or range; the `Unknown` reservation is "held for a bounded period" with no period. Both directly drive SM-C4 and tenant spend. *Fix:* State a default and permitted range for the safety margin (e.g. "default 10% of the recorded input limit, configurable 5–25%") and a maximum hold for `Unknown` reservations (e.g. "≤ 24 hours, after which the reservation settles at estimated maximum"), or explicitly delegate each to a named §13 row with an owner.
- **[medium]** Regeneration ceiling and `PostFailed` retry have no numbers (FR-16 consequence 4; FR-18 consequence 5; FR-32 consequence 6) — FR-32 says "a documented default and a valid range" where FR-18 gives expiry "24 hours ... 1 hour through 30 days"; FR-18's "bounded audited retry" has no count or duration. *Fix:* Give the regeneration ceiling a default and range (e.g. "default 3, range 1–10") and the `PostFailed` retry a bound (e.g. "at most 3 attempts over 15 minutes, then the proposal remains `PostFailed` until an Approver re-approves or it expires").
- **[medium]** Expiry of `Approved` and `PostFailed` proposals is implied but not stated (FR-18; §12 SM-3) — `Expired` is defined as "its `ExpiresAt` passed while non-terminal," and `Approved`/`PostFailed` are non-terminal, so an approved-but-unposted reply silently becomes `Expired` and is never posted. That is a plausible rule but a surprising one for an Approver who already approved, and SM-3 counts these as "in-flight" while they are expiring. *Fix:* Add an explicit FR-18 consequence stating whether `Approved`/`PostFailed` proposals expire (and, if so, that the Approver is notified in-product per FR-13), or exempt them from expiry once approved.
- **[low]** Approved posts have no stated participant-visible marker (FR-11 consequence 4; FR-17) — automatic posts carry a marker "identifying it as AI-generated from Conversation Context and not human-verified"; FR-17 records human-edited provenance in the trace but says nothing about what Participants see on an approved post. *Fix:* Add a FR-17 consequence defining the marker for approved posts (e.g. "AI-generated, approved by a human Approver" and, when edited, "human-edited"), consistent with the FR-7 disclosure category.
- **[low]** Residual adjective-only phrasing (FR-1 consequence 3; FR-4 consequence 2; FR-20 consequence 3; FR-22 consequence 3; FR-25 consequence 3) — "prior value where safe to expose," "a documented migration state may allow," "at a level sufficient to explain," "clearly distinguishes," "support launch monitoring." Each is bounded elsewhere or low-stakes, but they are the only unverifiable lines in an otherwise testable FR set. *Fix:* For FR-1, name who decides safety (the FR-7 disclosure category is a natural anchor); for FR-4, either specify the migration state or drop the sentence; for FR-25, replace with "exposes the SM-2 and SM-3 inputs defined in §12."

## Scope honesty — adequate

Omissions are explicit and specific. §5 Non-Goals does real work (each item is a thing a reader could otherwise assume — memory, tools, ambient triggers, external channels, silent truncation). §6.2 states the deferral reason for each out-of-scope item. §8 is blunt: "only `EXT-HOST-1` is `Committed`; the remaining entries are `Uncommitted`. Every consuming story therefore remains blocked from `ready-for-dev`." De-scoping in OQ-18 is honest to the point of discomfort.

Two things undercut this. First, the PRD contains zero `[ASSUMPTION]` tags and no Assumptions Index, yet it asserts specific members of an external contract that §8 says does not yet exist: `ParticipantRole.Facilitator` (FR-7, OQ-14), `ParticipantType.AiAgent`/`AIAgent` and `IConversationClient.AddParticipantAsync` and `POST /api/v1/conversations/{conversationId}/participants` (§8). The slash in "`AiAgent`/`AIAgent`" is an untagged assumption written inline. Second, the frontmatter says `final` while the document's own content describes a state that is not final by its own gates: seven Uncommitted critical dependencies, a primary metric declared defective (OQ-19), a launch-blocking deferred decision (OQ-18, "in any case before production enablement"). The open-items density is modest in count but high in weight for a green-light PRD.

### Findings

- **[high]** `status: final` is contradicted by the document's own gate state (frontmatter; §8 paragraph 3; §13 OQ-18, OQ-19) — the PRD records that every consuming story is blocked from `ready-for-dev`, that SM-3 is defective, and that OQ-18 must be resolved before production enablement. Downstream skills and readers treat `final` as license to build and gate; here it would license building against a gate the PRD disowns. *Fix:* Set status to `approved-pending` (or the repository's equivalent) with a one-line frontmatter note listing the three blockers, or resolve OQ-19 and re-issue as `final`. Keep §8's honesty either way.
- **[high]** External contract members asserted without `[ASSUMPTION]` tags (FR-7; §8 Hexalith.Conversations bullet; §13 OQ-14, OQ-16) — the PRD names types, methods, and routes on a dependency it marks `Uncommitted`, and writes "`ParticipantType.AiAgent`/`AIAgent`" as though both spellings are live. For a brownfield chain-top PRD these are the highest-consequence inferences in the document and are the ones architecture will code against. *Fix:* Tag each as `[ASSUMPTION: Conversations will publish <member> under EXT-CONV-AI-1; unconfirmed as of 2026-09-08]`, add an Assumptions Index at the end of §8 or §13, and pick one spelling of the participant type or state that the register owns the final name.
- **[medium]** OQ-18's permanent-block behavior is absent from the FRs it governs (FR-9; FR-27; §13 OQ-18) — a reader of FR-27 learns that "Prompt and Conversation Context pass the active policy before Provider invocation" but not that a single failing historical message makes the Conversation permanently uncallable with "no redaction or exclusion path." That consequence lives only in the register. *Fix:* Add a FR-27 consequence stating the behavior explicitly and marking it `[NON-GOAL for MVP: per-message redaction or exclusion of unsafe historical content — see OQ-18]`, so story creation does not have to discover it from §13.

## Downstream usability — strong

Every FR, NFR, OQ, SM, and UJ ID referenced anywhere in the document resolves to a definition; there are no gaps, duplicates, or dangling cross-references. The glossary covers the domain nouns that matter and is used consistently in the load-bearing places (FR bodies, §9, §13). UJs have named protagonists carrying context inline (Nora, Milan, Anika, Omar). Sections stand alone — §9 and §10 are extractable without reading §4. The PRD's explicit "Downstream authority" paragraph in §0 is a useful anchor for sprint tooling.

Two things will cost downstream time. FR-21's consequences 4–6 are process rules about story states ("blocks every consuming story from `ready-for-dev`," "recorded as non-conformant") embedded in a functional requirement whose header is "Fail Closed On Dependency Uncertainty"; a story-creation pass will either try to implement them as system behavior or have to special-case them. And "platform Agents service principal" — the identity that actually posts to Conversations (FR-2, FR-21) — appears three times without a glossary entry, while the glossary does define the less operationally critical "Launch Readiness Gate."

### Findings

- **[medium]** Process gates embedded as FR consequences (FR-21 consequences 4–6) — "A story completed while a dependency it consumes was `Uncommitted` is recorded as non-conformant" is a sprint-governance rule, not a system behavior; it has no implementing story and will confuse extraction. *Fix:* Move consequences 4–6 into §8 (where the nine-field rule already lives) and leave FR-21 with its three behavioral consequences plus the posting-principal rule; keep the cross-reference from FR-21 to §8.
- **[low]** "Platform Agents service principal" and "posting principal" lack glossary entries (FR-2 consequence 5; FR-21 consequence 7; FR-18 consequence 6; §3) — the principal that performs membership joins and posts is the most authorization-sensitive actor in the design and is named informally. *Fix:* Add a glossary entry "Agents Service Principal — the platform identity under which Hexalith Agents joins Conversations and posts Agent Responses on behalf of an Agent's Party identity; distinct from the caller and any Approver," and use it in FR-2, FR-18, and FR-21.

## Shape fit — adequate

The shape matches the product on the axes that matter most: a multi-stakeholder B2B capability with a governance workflow, so UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing and present; chain-top, so the traceability investment (§3, stable IDs, §0 downstream-authority paragraph) is justified; brownfield, so the PRD is careful to say that Conversations must *publish* the contract members it names rather than implying they exist. All UJs are new, which is correct for a new capability, and no existing UJs are misrepresented as new.

Where the shape strains is weight distribution. §0 says the PRD serves "release-readiness workflows," and it does — but §11 Evidence Levels, FR-28, FR-30, FR-21 consequences 4–6, NFR-11, and NFR-12 together form a release-qualification specification interleaved with product requirements. A reader looking for "what does `hexa` do" has to step around "what proves it" repeatedly. This is a deliberate choice, and for a governed-AI launch it is defensible, but it is heavier than a "prove one thing well" V1 (§1) would predict, and it is where the process-in-FR leakage above comes from.

### Findings

- **[medium]** Release-qualification content is interleaved with product requirements (§4.10 FR-28, FR-30; FR-21 consequences 4–6; §11; NFR-11, NFR-12) — the PRD carries an evidence taxonomy, a readiness gate, story-state rules, and recovery-drill requirements alongside feature FRs. Each is legitimate; together they make §4 read partly as a release spec and blur which consequences are system behavior versus release process. *Fix:* Keep FR-28/FR-30 as the product-visible surface of readiness (they define callable operations and hard behaviors), but relocate purely procedural rules (story states, non-conformance recording, "cites the harness and environment") into §8/§11, and add a one-line note in §0 that §11 and §8 are release authority while §4 is product behavior.

## Mechanical notes

- **ID continuity.** FR-1 through FR-32, NFR-1 through NFR-14, OQ-1 through OQ-19, SM-1 through SM-6 and SM-C1 through SM-C4, UJ-1 through UJ-4: all unique, all referenced IDs resolve. FR headings are not in numeric order in the document — FR-29 sits between FR-23 and FR-24, FR-30 between FR-25 and FR-26, FR-31 between FR-26 and FR-27, FR-32 after FR-28. This is the correct outcome of the stated "globally stable" ID policy (§0) and should not be renumbered; downstream tooling that assumes document order equals ID order will need to key on IDs.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip.** Vacuous: zero inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags and no index. See the Scope honesty high finding — the roundtrip passes only because nothing was tagged.
- **Callout tags.** Zero `[NOTE FOR PM]` and zero `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` inline callouts. The §13 register's *Deferred* rows and §5/§6.2 do the equivalent work for a `final`-stage document; acceptable, noting that OQ-18's consequence is not surfaced where it applies (FR-27).
- **UJ protagonists.** All four named with inline context (Nora, Milan, Anika, Omar). UJ-3 carries the segregation-of-duties rule inline, which matches FR-7.
- **Glossary drift (low).** "Agent response(s)" lowercase in §2.2, UJ-2, UJ-3, FR-19, §6.2 (5 occurrences) versus glossary "Agent Response"; "Agent call" lowercase in UJ-3 and FR-8 consequence 1 (and "Agent calling" in FR-20) versus "Agent Call"; bare "Participant(s)" in UJ-2, the Eligible Conversation glossary entry, and FR-31 versus "Conversation Participant"; glossary heading "Provider/Model Selection" (capital M) versus "Provider/model selection" everywhere else (6 occurrences). "`ParticipantType.AiAgent`/`AIAgent`" in §8 gives two spellings for one contract member.
- **Two names for one dependency.** §8 uses both `CONV-AI-1` ("External prerequisite `CONV-AI-1`, tracked as `EXT-CONV-AI-1`"); harmless but downstream should key on the `EXT-` form used in the register list.
- **Register naming.** §13 is titled "V1 Decision Register" but its rows are `OQ-` (Open Question) IDs and 16 of 19 are resolved decisions. Keep the IDs (they are referenced throughout) but the mismatch is worth a one-line note in §13's preamble so readers do not treat every `OQ-` reference as an open item.
- **Glossary carrying metric logic.** The "Eligible Conversation" entry contains SM-2/SM-C4 rationale ("Eligibility measures reach, so SM-2 remains an adoption metric..."). Definition is fine; the rationale belongs in §12 so the glossary stays extractable as pure vocabulary.
- **Required sections.** Present for a launch-level chain-top PRD: purpose, vision, users and journeys, glossary, features with FRs, non-goals, MVP scope, NFRs, dependencies, governance, API surface, evidence, success metrics with counter-metrics, decision register. No Assumptions Index (see above).
