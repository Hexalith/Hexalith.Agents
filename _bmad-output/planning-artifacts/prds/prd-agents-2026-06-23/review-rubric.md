## Overall verdict

This PRD now does what a launch-level, chain-top, brownfield PRD should: the §1 thesis is specific, the §13 register states decisions with what was given up (OQ-10 in particular), `RQ-1` is wired only to evidence a qualification cohort can produce (OQ-22), SM-7 gives the governance thesis a metric that could falsify it, FR-33 finally names who may do what, and the 14 inferences about contracts no owner has confirmed are tagged and indexed with owners. Every critical and high finding from the 2026-09-08 run is closed on the page. What remains at risk is narrower and mostly in the seams: FR-2's account of a removal performed inside Hexalith.Conversations contradicts its own idempotent-join rule, a handful of decidability gaps that the earlier adversarial pass raised (FR-26 "more restrictive", FR-31 "asserts a commitment", the absent Agent Call state enumeration, p99 on n=30) were carried forward unchanged, and the frontmatter's `updated:` field — which §0 designates as the record of when amendments land — has not been advanced to the revision it describes. None of these blocks UX, architecture, or story extraction; they are the items an implementer will otherwise decide alone.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions read as decisions. §13 is a register of 22 binding rows with owners, dates, and amendment history; the two rows marked *Deferred* (OQ-15, OQ-18) carry a revisit condition, and OQ-18 now has a due date (2026-10-15, A-14). The trade-offs are named with what was given up: OQ-10 says outright "`hexa` is unavailable in Conversations that outgrow the budget, in exchange for never sending a silently reduced context to a Provider", and SM-C4 makes that cost visible. FR-6 and OQ-20 resolve the per-tenant/per-Conversation ambiguity with a sentence a pushback would recognise: "a tenant that wants confirmation for sensitive Conversations runs Confirmation Response Mode for all of them." §0 confronts the `status: final` objection directly — final "does not state that the build is implementation-ready" — and §8 reports "only `EXT-HOST-1` is `Committed`" without softening.

The gate logic is now decidable. `RQ-1` (§3, FR-28, OQ-22) takes only SM-1, SM-4, SM-5, SM-6, the NFR-9/NFR-14 latency gates, and audit completeness; SM-2/SM-3/SM-7 are explicitly post-enablement, and SM-1 no longer references the gate it feeds. A Release Operator can act on this.

There are no `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts; the register and the Assumptions Index do that work here, and for a document at this stage that is the right shape.

### Findings

- **medium** Readiness authority is still a document while FR-30 exposes a callable readiness surface (§ FR-28 consequence 9; FR-30 consequence 2; §10 "Launch readiness") — FR-28: "The external dependency register's launch-readiness gate records are the readiness authority. Any per-Agent readiness record is transitional and must reconcile to that authority." FR-30 and §10 require a public, versioned contract to "inspect readiness state, recorded postures, evidence levels, and outstanding blockers." A markdown register cannot be read by the runtime, so two sources of truth persist with "reconcile" as the only bridge, and the API reports whatever was last typed. This was raised on 2026-09-08 and is still open. *Fix:* Decide one way: either readiness is a governed runtime record (register is an input; the API is authority and the register cites it), or the API is a read-only mirror stamped with a "last synced from register" marker and explicitly non-authoritative.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis (§1: "AI assistance must become part of the conversation model without weakening the model's existing identity and governance guarantees") is what the feature set serves: identity (§4.1), governed Provider selection (§4.2), approval (§4.3, §4.6), fail-closed authorization (§4.7), safety before side effects (§4.10). The V1 bet "does less than a broad autonomous agent platform" and the non-goals (§5) and §6.2 defer exactly the things the addendum says competitors lead with (tools, memory, ambient triggers). MVP scope kind is problem-solving with a platform seam (the Approved Bounded Context Behavior and `ProposedAgentReplyState` contracts exist so later behaviors need no contract change), and the scope logic matches.

The metric set now validates the thesis rather than activity. SM-7 ("between 10% and 60% are edited, regenerated, or rejected before that decision ... This is the metric the §1 thesis would falsify") is the right kind of metric, and SM-2 is demoted to secondary. Counter-metrics exist for every primary except SM-7, whose two-sided band is self-counterbalancing.

### Findings

- **low** SM-7 is unmeasurable for an all-Automatic launch cohort (§ §12 SM-7; FR-6; SM-C1) — SM-7 counts "proposals that reach a human decision", which only Confirmation Response Mode produces, and FR-6 makes mode tenant-wide. If every launch tenant runs Automatic Response Mode, the thesis metric returns `InsufficientEvidence` and the governance bet is tested only by SM-4/SM-5. The PRD does not say whether that is acceptable. *Fix:* Add one line to §12 or OQ-22: either the launch cohort must include at least one Confirmation-mode tenant, or SM-7 `InsufficientEvidence` is an accepted launch-health outcome that does not trigger review.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

The FR set is unusually testable. Each FR has consequences with a verifiable condition; the hard cases have numbers (FR-16 ceiling "default 3 ... 1 through 10"; FR-18 expiry "24 hours ... 1 hour through 30 days" and retry "at most 3 attempts over 15 minutes"; FR-28 hold "default 24 hours, configurable from 1 through 72 hours"; FR-9 margin "10% ... 5% through 25%"); typed reasons are named where an engineer would otherwise invent one (`NoEligibleApprover`, `MembershipUnavailable`, `MembershipRejected`, `ProhibitedCostControlPosture`, `Indeterminate`, `Blocked`); the proposal state machine (FR-18) is enumerated with terminality and expiry semantics; the Accepted Agent Call glossary entry fixes the check order and the latency clock. The prior run's "unbounded bounds" list is closed.

What keeps this from *strong* is a cluster of rules that read as testable but are not decidable as written, most of them flagged on 2026-09-08 and carried forward unchanged.

### Findings

- **medium** "More restrictive of two policies" and "stricter mode-specific policy" are not computable (§ FR-26 consequences 4 and 7; FR-33 row "Publish or version the Content Safety Policy") — "each retry, approval-time check, and pre-post check uses the more restrictive of the initial attempt's policy and the then-current active policy" and "unless a stricter mode-specific policy is configured." Two policy documents with different category sets, thresholds, and handling rules are not totally ordered, so "more restrictive" has no implementation other than "must pass both", which the text does not say. Still open from 2026-09-08. *Fix:* Replace with "the content must pass every applicable policy version (the initial attempt's and the then-current active policy); a mode-specific policy is applied in addition to, never instead of, the tenant policy." Apply the same wording in OQ-9.
- **medium** FR-31's assertion category blocks the summaries UJ-2 exists for (§ FR-31 consequence 2; OQ-9; UJ-2; FR-17 consequence 4) — "blocks generated content that impersonates a named Party, or that asserts a decision, commitment, or approval on a Party's behalf." Milan's job is "interpreting the prior discussion"; "Milan agreed to ship Friday" asserts a commitment on Milan's behalf. Because FR-17 scans human-edited versions "on the same terms", an Approver who writes "the team approved this plan" is blocked at approval time. No classifier separates reporting from asserting, so the rule is either a no-op or blocks the core use. Still open. *Fix:* Narrow to first-person impersonation ("content written in the voice of a named Party or presented as that Party's own statement") and state that reporting a Party's stated position from Conversation content is permitted.
- **medium** The Agent Call has no enumerated state contract while FR-25 requires distinguishing its outcomes (§ FR-8; FR-10; FR-12; FR-25 consequence 2; FR-23) — FR-18 enumerates `ProposedAgentReplyState`; nothing does the same for the call. FR-25 nevertheless requires status to "distinguish configuration errors, authorization failures, context policy failures, content safety failures, Provider failures, generation failures, pending approvals, and posting failures", and FR-23 requires stable public enums. FR-9 (`Blocked`) and FR-12 (`Indeterminate`) now name two values but not the set they belong to. Still open. *Fix:* Add an FR-8 consequence listing the public Agent Call states (accepted; blocked-by authorization / context / safety / cost / rate limit / membership; generating; generated; `Indeterminate`; posted; posting failed) and which are terminal, mirroring FR-18.
- **medium** The configuration-time segregation predicate is not defined per source kind (§ FR-7 consequence 12) — "the system rejects a policy whose sources can never resolve two distinct Parties, such as a caller-only policy or a single predefined Party with no other source." Whether a Facilitator-only or tenant-role-only policy "can never" resolve two Parties depends on Conversation membership, which does not exist at configuration time, so the check must be structural — and the PRD gives only two examples, not the rule. An implementer deciding whether Facilitator-only is legal will decide differently from UX. The call-time `NoEligibleApprover` rule makes either answer safe, but the admin-facing outcome is undefined. *Fix:* State the predicate: a policy passes configuration when it contains at least two predefined Parties, or any tenant-role source, or any Facilitator source combined with at least one other source; caller-only, single-predefined-Party-only, and Facilitator-only fail. If Facilitator-only is meant to pass, say so and note that the Facilitator's own calls will return `NoEligibleApprover`.
- **medium** p99 over 30 executions is the sample maximum (§ NFR-9; NFR-14; FR-28 consequence 5; OQ-5) — "p99 ≤ 120 seconds ... Each gate requires at least 30 production-like executions." With n = 30 the 99th percentile is the largest sample or an interpolation between the top two, so the gate is either "no single run exceeds 120 s" or method-dependent. §11 delegates the percentile method to the measurement contract, which makes the gate's meaning depend on a downstream choice. Still open. *Fix:* Either raise the minimum for the p99 gates to a size that supports them (≥ 300) or keep only p95 in the `RQ-1` gate with n ≥ 30, and name the percentile method in NFR-9.
- **low** Residual adjective-only phrasing (§ FR-1 consequence 3 "prior value where safe to expose"; FR-4 consequence 2 "A documented migration state may allow temporary read-only inspection"; FR-20 consequence 3 "at a level sufficient to explain"; FR-22 consequence 3 "clearly distinguishes"; FR-25 consequence 3 "support launch monitoring") — each is bounded elsewhere or low-stakes, but they are the only unverifiable lines in the FR set. *Fix:* FR-1: name the excluded fields (secret references, per NFR-6). FR-4: specify the migration state or delete the sentence. FR-25: "exposes the SM-2, SM-3, and SM-7 inputs defined in §12."

### Findings

- **high** A removal performed inside Hexalith.Conversations is silently undone by the idempotent join (§ FR-2 consequences 5, 8, 9; §8 Hexalith.Conversations bullet; OQ-16) — FR-2: membership "is idempotent: a repeated join for the same Agent and Conversation is a no-op that returns the existing membership", and the join is the last pre-Provider step of every Accepted Agent Call. FR-2 then says a removal "performed directly in Hexalith.Conversations rather than through Agents is not observed by Agents until the next membership check at acceptance or before posting, at which point the call or post fails closed under the same rules." The "same rules" are the Agents-owned block, which nothing has set; so at the next call the membership step is an `AddParticipantAsync` that succeeds and re-admits `hexa`. §8 builds on this ("Removal of `hexa` needs no Conversations notification ... a removal performed directly in Conversations is caught at the next membership check"), so the decision to omit a removal seam from `EXT-CONV-AI-1` rests on a step the PRD's own text does not perform. An implementer who builds exactly what is written ships a governance control that reverts itself. *Fix:* Choose one: (a) Agents records that it has joined a Conversation and, on a later call, *verifies* membership rather than re-joining; an observed absence sets the Agents block and fails the call closed, with re-admission requiring the FR-33 authority; or (b) accept re-join as the intended behavior and delete the "fails closed" sentence, stating that removal is only honored through Agents; or (c) add a removal-notification seam to `EXT-CONV-AI-1` with its own assumption tag. Update OQ-16 and §8 to match.
- **low** Process gates remain inside FR-21 (§ FR-21 consequences 4–6) — "A story completed while a dependency it consumes was `Uncommitted` is recorded as non-conformant" is a sprint-governance rule, not a system behavior; it has no implementing story and story extraction will stumble on it. §8 already states the rule. *Fix:* Move consequences 4–6 into §8 and keep a one-line cross-reference in FR-21.
- **low** Code names for the two central aggregates are absent from the glossary (§ §3 "Agent Call", "Global Providers Aggregate") — the code's established names are `AgentInteraction` and `ProviderCatalog` (domain `provider-catalog`); every route and event uses them. For a brownfield PRD that architecture and stories will map onto existing code, the alias saves a lookup every time. *Fix:* Add "(code: `AgentInteraction`)" and "(code: `ProviderCatalogAggregate`, domain `provider-catalog`)" to the two entries.
- **low** `Draft` lifecycle state is never named (§ FR-3; FR-22 consequence 3) — the public `AgentLifecycleStatus` is `Draft | Active | Disabled`; FR-22 asks the UI to distinguish "active, disabled, invalid", where "invalid" is a readiness classification, not a lifecycle state. *Fix:* Name `Draft` in FR-3 and say "invalid configuration (readiness)" in FR-22.

## Shape fit — adequate

The shape fits a multi-stakeholder B2B governance capability with meaningful UX: UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing (Anika's UJ-3 is where FR-7's rules become legible), SMs are user-facing where the thesis needs them (SM-7) and operational where the gate needs them (SM-4, SM-5), and the brownfield references are accurate and distinguished from new requirements (FR-23's deprecate-and-reject register; FR-18's explicit adoption of the shipped enum). The chain-top obligations are met.

What still strains the shape is release-process content interleaved with product behavior: FR-21 consequences 4–6 (story-state rules), FR-28 (readiness gate mechanics, evidence levels, kill switch), §11 (evidence taxonomy), NFR-11/NFR-12 (recovery drills, environment limits). Each is legitimate, and §0 now says the PRD carries "normative evidence authority", but §4 reads partly as a release specification and a story writer must sort behavior from procedure. This is a shape cost the team has chosen; it is recorded here rather than counted as a defect.

### Findings

- **low** Evidence retention and traceability diverge at day 366 (§ §9 retention bullets; FR-24 consequence 1; FR-3 consequence 2; OQ-8) — §9 erases "protected sensitive payloads" 365 days after terminal state while "Posted Conversation Messages remain governed by Hexalith.Conversations retention"; FR-24 promises every posted Agent Response "can be traced back to ... generated content". After erasure the posted message stands and the generated and edited versions that explain it are a tombstone. The PRD never says which evidence fields are "sensitive payload" and which are structural. Still open from 2026-09-08. *Fix:* State that identities, versions, decisions, timestamps, policy versions, and content hashes survive erasure so the trace remains provable without content.
