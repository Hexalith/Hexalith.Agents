Hexalith Agents — UX Design Validation Report

_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/ux-designs/ux-agents-2026-06-23/ · DESIGN.md + EXPERIENCE.md · four lenses with regression duty

The FR-33 Update landed the authorization model and left the plumbing describing the world before it. That sentence is the governance lens's, and the other three arrived at it independently. What the Update set out to do, it did: § Authorization roles binds all six PRD roles to the five AD-30 policy constants; all 15 IA RequiredPolicy cells are re-derived from FR-33 rows; the caller can no longer approve their own call; the Caller source is retired; cost caps are a two-tier lower-only editor with a Platform-Operator-only override; content safety splits into platform publication and a stricter-only tenant delta; deletion carries the A-12 two-person rule. 58 of the baseline's 96 findings are verifiably closed, and the pair is mechanically the cleanest it has been.

The answer to this run's question is nonetheless no, it did not land cleanly, and it failed in the same shape as the Update before it: a set, rule, or name was changed and the counts, rosters, cross-references and downstream statements that described the old state were left standing. The pattern is confirmed at least a dozen times — “the four FR-18 exits” against six, “the six-part eligibility set” against seven, “this four-name list” against two, and “the ten families” against a roster the Update itself grew past: it declared four operations to be high-impact-confirmation families and added a contents row for none, so four justification-gated dialogs have no specified body and, since the rendered set is the audit record, no specified audit record either.

Three consequences are severe. The platform tier has no reachable surface — no route is gated on Agents.PlatformOperator, and in shipped code the constant is unregistered, so platform operations fall back to Agents.Administrator, recorded two lines after § Authorization roles forbids falling back to a more permissive row. The emergency stop misdescribes itself — the kill-switch confirmation promises that Approved proposals “complete or fail on their own terms” where FR-28 and AD-12 hold them without posting, and the Update then listed the kill switch as an Abandoned reason FR-18 states it can never be. Segregation of duties reached deletion and stopped — one Compliance Inspector still releases the hold protecting the evidence and exports it, and the new compliance-inspection rule lets the Tenant Agent Administrator post-hoc-review an inspection of which they are the subject.

Two causes sit underneath. The Update reconciled the FR-33 table and treated it as the whole 2026-09-09 PRD revision, which also rewrote FR-3, FR-4, FR-18, FR-28 and FR-30 and added OQ-24 through OQ-30 — all seven return zero hits in both spines, and 16 of the rubric lens's 20 new findings trace to it. The mirror image sits in the epics: epics.md carries 33 stories and the pair names 28, and three of the five it never names own precisely what the Update added. The invented-constant failure the Agents.AuditOperator revert corrected before this run was not the only one of its kind — it was the visible one. The Update also invented KillSwitchActive, a token in no source, where Architecture's token is Suspended, which has no row anywhere. Both spines say status: final; on authorization, lifecycle vocabulary and story ownership, they are not.

Three baseline findings regressed, all the same failure: a rendering that was missing became a rendering that is wrong. The Abandoned enumeration landed with a fourth value FR-18 forbids; the Audit evidence IA cell landed granting posted provenance to three role constants without membership; and the version-radio fix, having correctly established that DisabledFocusable is absent on FluentRadio, replaced it with a rule that opens a modal on every arrow keypress in a native radiogroup.
