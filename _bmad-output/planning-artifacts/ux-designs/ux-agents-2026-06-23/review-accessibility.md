# Accessibility Review — Hexalith Agents

Reviewer lens: accessibility as a buildable contract (WCAG 2.2 AA binding; whole-string EN/FR parity binding). Run: 2026-09-12, round 5, against `DESIGN.md` and `EXPERIENCE.md` as edited by the 2026-09-10 Update. Inputs read: both spines (§ Accessibility Floor, § State Patterns, § Interaction Primitives, § Component Patterns, § Key Flows, § Responsive & Platform, § Information Architecture, § Conversation Integration Seam, § Voice and Tone, § Known gaps; `DESIGN.md` § Colors, § Layout & Spacing, § Components, § Do's and Don'ts), `reconcile-validation-2026-09-10.md`, the round-4 `.working/validation-2026-09-10-round4/review-accessibility.md`, and `architecture/architecture-agents-2026-06-23-2/reviews/reconcile-ux-2026-09-12.md`.

This run did **not** re-verify Fluent v5 parameter surfaces or the `src/Hexalith.Agents.UI` census. Round 4 verified both against source, the 2026-09-10 reconciliation re-verified the two contested inventories against the tree (29 live-region sites / 20 files; 15 `Disabled` sites / 5 files; the `aria-describedby` splat resolved empirically through the eight shipped `data-testid` sites), and nothing in this round's edits reopens them. Findings below are read against the spines' own rules.

## Overall verdict

This is the first round in which the accessibility contract holds together as one document rather than as a set of individually correct rules that contradict each other three sections apart. **All twenty-two round-4 findings are closed**, including both criticals and the one regression — the `conversation-agent-call` row and `DESIGN.md § conversation-agent-call` now agree that the panel owns no live-region node at all, and the version radios now edit a draft selection applied by an explicit **View version** control, with the unsaved-changes dialog given a named `EditorDraft` contract instead of being left family-less. The politeness table absorbed every event the previous run found missing and gained the generic `ProjectionConfirmed` row that finally announces the terminal success of the product's highest-consequence writes; the confirmation-family table is now cited by `OperationGateMatrixVersion = 3` rather than by a count that kept going stale; the A-7 retry window has its own argued 2.2.1 essential claim and its own nearing-window flag and announced expiry; § Voice and Tone carries strings for the nine previously unkeyed announcements plus the predicate that makes any future omission a defect. The four source contradictions `reconcile-ux-2026-09-12.md` asked UX to fix — the six-conjunct Eligible Approver predicate, the withdrawal of "queue position where safe", the retry actor, and the `CurrencyMismatch` / `EXT-CONV-UI-1` known-gap rows — are all closed in the spines.

What remains is a different and narrower class of defect: the spine is now excellent at the states it enumerates and blind to the mechanisms that carry them. Three of them matter. First, **the grid has no ARIA contract for virtualization** — the envelope rule mandates virtualization with a density-bound `ItemSize` and never mentions `aria-rowcount`/`aria-rowindex`, so a conforming build tells a screen-reader user "row 4 of 12" on a 25-row page and passes every check this spine names. Second, **countdowns multiplied and their live-region discipline did not**: the queue's expiry label carries an explicit "refreshed at most once per minute, outside any live region", and the four countdowns added since — the tightening grace, the `PausedDuration`-adjusted retry window on proposal detail, the A-19 7-day inspection window, the trigger-review validity — carry nothing, on surfaces that already own a polite node. Third, **2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide is never named**, in a § Other criteria that disposes of 2.5.8, 2.4.11, 2.5.7, 3.2.6, 3.3.8, 3.1.2 and twice of 2.2.1 individually, while the product polls every 250 ms on every write surface and re-sorts a 25-row queue on a poll cycle.

The fourth material gap is at the seam, and it is the one a story-dev cannot discover. `EXT-CONV-UI-1` now correctly makes the persistent contributed region the panel's sole speaker. It says nothing about what the *host* announces. The Conversation view is a chat surface; a posted `hexa` reply arriving as a Conversation Message is exactly the thing chat surfaces announce. On the product's primary path the spine has carefully guaranteed one Agents-owned pair and left the most likely duplicate announcement in the product entirely unaddressed — and that same silence is where streaming or partial generated output would enter if anyone ever built it, since the spine neither specifies nor forbids it.

No criticals. Six high, ten medium, four low.

## Findings

### Keyboard operability

- **[high]** The virtualized grid has no ARIA row-count contract, so a conforming build misreports the size of every list in the product. § Grid rules carries the FC-TBL envelope rules over to the hand-authored `FluentDataGrid` — "virtualization with a density-bound `ItemSize`, stable item keys, the reserved column keys" — and neither § Grid rules nor § Other criteria ever names `aria-rowcount` or `aria-rowindex`. A virtualized grid renders only the visible window into the DOM; without those attributes a screen reader announces the DOM row count and DOM row positions, so a 25-row proposal queue reads as "row 3 of 9" and an Approver cannot tell how many proposals they are responsible for or where they are in the list. Every other row-level rule in the section — `ItemKey` so a poll cannot move the focused row, the status-cell focus target, `FcExpandInRowDetail` — presupposes a coherent row identity that this omission destroys. It reaches all three grids, including the read-only Launch readiness gate grid. *Fix:* add a row to § Grid rules: the grid sets `aria-rowcount` to the authoritative total (not the rendered window) and each rendered row sets `aria-rowindex` to its absolute position; make both an `LR-UI-CONFORMANCE` assertion alongside the 320 px evidence, since axe does not flag a virtualized grid that simply lies.

- **[high]** The approver-policy reorder controls are the spine's only substitute for a drag operation and they have no name, focus, or announcement contract. § Approver policy rules ends "Row order is edited with buttons or a menu, never drag-only", and `DESIGN.md § approver-policy-builder` renders it as "move up/down `FluentButton`s" per row — which is the right answer to 2.5.7 and produces, on a six-source policy, twelve buttons whose accessible names are all "Move up" or "Move down". § Forms names the *row* ("Policy-source rows have an accessible name including kind and basis") and not the buttons. Worse, the operation is stateful and silent: nothing says focus follows the moved row's button to its new position (the default is that focus stays put and the row the user was moving jumps away under them), and the politeness table — which asserts "any event this spine names and this table omits is a defect in the table" — has no row-reorder event at all. Row order is load-bearing here: it is the order in which Approver sources are resolved. A keyboard user can operate this control and cannot tell what it did. *Fix:* require each reorder button's accessible name to carry the row identity (`Move {kind}: {basis} up`), require focus to follow the moved row's activated button, and add a politeness-table row announcing `{source} moved to position {n} of {total}` in the polite node. While there, note that the builder specifies ordering and validation but no add or remove control — if rows can be added or deleted, they need the same three contracts, and row deletion is the one focus case § High-risk pending commands' ladder does not cover.

- **[medium]** The grid's keyboard navigation model is unspecified, and the row action pattern multiplies tab stops with no bypass. § Interaction Primitives says "Hover-revealed row actions also render on row focus-within", § Grid rules gives every row a status cell plus an open action, and the deterministic focus rule places `tabindex="-1"` on a status `gridcell` — which implies grid navigation with a roving tabindex, but the spine never says so. If the implementation leaves cells and row actions naturally tabbable instead, a 25-row queue is 50 to 75 tab stops with no skip mechanism, and the shell's skip links land on `main`, not past the grid. The two readings produce completely different keyboards from the same document. *Fix:* state which model the grids use — `FluentDataGrid`'s own grid-navigation mode with a single tab stop into the grid is the reading the `tabindex="-1"` rule already assumes — and say what the arrow keys do relative to the row action and the expand control.

- **[medium]** `FcExpandInRowDetail` names every expanded row's detail region identically, and its expand control has no stated ARIA. `DetailPanelAriaLabel` is `[EditorRequired]` and specified as "a per-grid localized whole string" with one entry per view key. The envelope rule requires "a detail panel outside the virtualized grid with an always-present `role="region"`". With more than one row expanded — which nothing forbids — a screen-reader user's landmark or region list shows N regions all called the same thing, with no way to tell which proposal each belongs to. The expand control itself is never given `aria-expanded`, `aria-controls`, or a row-identifying name, and nothing says where focus goes on expand or returns on collapse. *Fix:* make the detail region's accessible name composite — the per-grid label plus the row identity — and specify the toggle's `aria-expanded`/`aria-controls` plus focus behaviour on expand and collapse.

- **[medium]** The inline confirm strip's inertness is unspecified, leaving two live commit paths inside one dialog. § Interaction Primitives correctly replaces the forbidden nested dialog with "an inline confirm strip rendered in the same `FluentDialogBody`, with focus moved to it and a second `Esc` cancelling the strip". It does not say whether the rest of the dialog goes inert while the strip is live. If it does not, a user can `Tab` from the strip straight back to Confirm and commit the governed write while an unresolved discard prompt is on screen — and on a justification-gated confirmation that Confirm is the operation the strip exists to protect. This reaches all nineteen confirmation rows, since each contains a required justification `FluentTextArea`. *Fix:* state that the strip is the only operable region while open (the other controls `aria-disabled` or the body marked inert), and say what Confirm and Cancel do while it is live.

### Focus management

- **[high]** `version-history` announces a version that is not on screen, once per arrow key. The redesign is correct — the radio group edits a draft selection and only **View version** applies it — but the sentence written for the old immediate-apply model survived intact: "On a selection change the textarea's accessible name becomes the version identity and the status node announces `Showing version {n}.`" In a native radiogroup the selection *is* the arrow key, which the same row states two sentences earlier. So arrowing from version 1 to version 4 now fires three polite announcements of `Showing version 2`, `3`, `4` while the textarea still holds version 1, and renames the textarea three times to versions it does not contain. That is worse than the defect the redesign fixed: the old rule at least announced something true. It is also the one announcement in the product a screen-reader user would reasonably trust to tell them which version they are about to approve. *Fix:* rescope both clauses to **View version** activation — the accessible name and the announcement change when the applied version changes, never when the draft moves. The draft movement is already carried by the radio's own selection semantics and needs no announcement of its own.

- **[medium]** Nothing says where focus goes after **View version** applies, or what the control becomes once it has. The row gives **View version** a `DisabledFocusable` state while the editor is dirty and a focus-return target on Cancel and on Save/Discard resolution, and is silent on the ordinary success path: the user activates it, the textarea content changes under them, and the spine's own § Accessibility Floor rule for "after async completion" does not reach it because this is not a route change or a state-slot swap. Nor does it say whether the control is now a no-op that still looks activatable (draft equals applied) or becomes `DisabledFocusable` again, and with what reason. *Fix:* focus moves to the textarea (whose accessible name is now the newly applied version identity, per the fix above), and **View version** becomes `DisabledFocusable` with a "this version is already shown" reason while the draft matches the applied selection — consistent with how `response-mode-toggle` treats its Apply.

### Live regions and announcements

- **[high]** The seam guarantees Agents owns one pair on the Conversation surface and says nothing about what the host announces, on the product's only V1 invocation path. `EXT-CONV-UI-1`'s fourth artifact kind is now correct and the panel owns no node — but § Live regions' governing rule is scoped to Agents routes and to FrontComposer components ("the permitted FrontComposer speakers are a closed list"; "four shell-owned speakers render on every Agents route"). The Conversation view is neither. It is a chat surface owned by a separate module, and the single most likely thing a chat surface announces is the arrival of a new message — which is precisely the event Agents' persistent region announces as `Posted`. `hexa`'s reply therefore plausibly announces twice, once as the domain outcome and once as Conversation content, and `agent-response-marker` is contributed into that same message. Nothing in the seam table, the ownership split, or the four artifact kinds constrains it, and NFR-14's `LiveRegionAnnouncedTick` observes the Agents node only, so the duplicate corrupts nothing measurable and ships invisible. *Fix:* add a fifth clause to `EXT-CONV-UI-1` — the host declares its own announcement behaviour for contributed content, and Agents suppresses whichever of `Posted` / new-message the host already speaks — and record it as an open seam item the way the focus-return contract was, since Agents cannot decide it alone.

- **[high]** Four countdowns were added with no update cadence and no live-region exclusion, on surfaces that own a polite node. The queue's expiry label is explicitly disciplined — "an absolute culture-formatted timestamp plus a static relative label refreshed at most once per minute, **outside any live region**" — and every countdown added since carries nothing: the tightening-grace deadline "rendered as a countdown" in § Provider data handling; the `PausedDuration`-adjusted retry-window remaining time, which § Other criteria deliberately moves onto proposal detail "rather than only inside the retry confirmation"; the A-19 7-day post-hoc countdown in § Audit evidence rules; and the trigger-review 7-day decision validity. A per-second countdown inside or adjacent to a `role="status"` node is the canonical screen-reader flood, and proposal detail is exactly where § Announcement volume's discipline was applied to a different hazard and stops. The retry-window case is the sharpest: the same paragraph adds a once-only nearing-window announcement *and* a rendered remaining time, and only the first is bounded. *Fix:* extend the queue's sentence into a general rule — every rendered countdown or relative-time label updates at most once per minute and lives outside every live region; time-sensitive changes announce through the named once-per-item warnings in the politeness table and nowhere else.

- **[high]** WCAG 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide is never claimed, dispatched, or excepted, in a section that disposes of every other applicable criterion individually. § Other criteria argues 2.2.1 twice at length, and names 2.5.8, 2.4.11, 2.5.7, 3.2.6 (N/A), 3.3.8 (N/A), 3.1.2 and 2.5.2 — and never 2.2.2, while the product polls every 250 ms for 8 s on every write surface, polls the queue on a cycle that re-sorts 25 rows nearest-expiry-first, auto-refreshes readiness on poll and window focus, and (per the finding above) renders live countdowns. Some of that is defensible under the criterion's own exceptions, and the deferred-re-sort rule is a partial mitigation already — but the spine's whole method is to argue an exception rather than assert one, and a reviewer signing `LR-UI-CONFORMANCE` has no row to sign here. A story-dev can ship an auto-refreshing operational status page with no pause and nothing in the spine is violated. *Fix:* add a 2.2.2 bullet on the same pattern as the two 2.2.1 ones: name the auto-updating surfaces, claim the essential exception where the update *is* the information (pending-command catch-up, readiness freshness), and where it is not — the queue's periodic re-sort — state the pause or the manual-refresh mechanism that satisfies it. The `{count} proposals changed. Refresh list.` deferral is most of that mechanism already; it just needs to be claimed as one and extended past "focus inside the grid body".

- **[medium]** The re-sort deferral is scoped to focus, which is not where a screen-reader user is reading. § Proposal queue rules: "while focus is inside the grid body a re-sort or page shift is deferred while the status node announces `{count} proposals changed. Refresh list.`" A screen-reader user in browse mode reads with a virtual cursor and holds no DOM focus inside the grid; for them the guard never arms and rows re-sort silently under the reading position, which is the exact defect the rule exists to prevent, on the exact user it most affects. *Fix:* arm the deferral whenever the grid is the user's current reading context, not only when it holds focus — in practice, defer whenever the grid has been interacted with or focus is anywhere within the page's grid region, and announce the deferral regardless.

- **[medium]** Filtering announces nothing unless the result is empty. Both interactive grids render state and `needs my action` filters as Agents-owned `aria-pressed` toggles; `FcFilterEmptyState` is a permitted speaker "for their own filter and row-expansion events only", and `FcFilterSummary` is explicitly **silenced**. So a toggle that narrows 25 proposals to 3 produces no announcement at all, while a toggle that narrows them to 0 does — and the politeness table, which asserts its own completeness, has no filter-applied row. The `aria-pressed` state change alone tells the user the button toggled, not what happened to the list. The Agents-owned reset control is specified as "announced" and the toggles that create the state are not. *Fix:* add a polite row — `{count} proposals match the current filters` — fired once per filter change on both grids, and say that it, not `FcFilterSummary`, is the speaker.

- **[medium]** Cost, quota and grace threshold crossings are not in the politeness table. `budget blocked` and `rate limited` are (assertive, correctly), and the states that *precede* them are not: the 80% consumption warning, the audited override lapsing at the earlier of its ceiling and expiry, and the tightening grace reaching its deadline and flipping the model to `DataHandlingAcceptanceLapsed`. All three are threshold crossings that occur while an operator is on the surface, all three change an adjacent badge's text beside a control whose label does not change — which is the precise pattern § Live regions bans ("a state change a user is waiting on is never left to a silently mutating badge") — and the table declares any such omission a defect in itself. *Fix:* add the three as polite rows (`Budget consumption reached 80% of the {scope} cap`, `The cost override expired`, `The data handling grace period ended; {model} is blocked`), with strings in § Voice and Tone.

- **[medium]** Streaming or partial generated output is neither specified nor forbidden, so the one thing that would break the live-region design is the one thing nothing rules on. § Agent call renders `generating` as "In flight; no message or proposal", and the contract has no partial-output token — so nothing streams today. But `proposal-editor`'s primary region is a `FluentTextArea` holding generated content, the editor has a preview, § Other criteria already anticipates generated text needing its own `lang` attribute, and a story-dev filling an 8-second generation wait with incrementally rendered tokens is an obvious and unprohibited move. If that content lands anywhere a live region can see it — and Agents owns exactly one polite node per route — the result is a screen reader reading a reply one token at a time, unstoppable. *Fix:* one sentence in § Live regions: generated content is rendered only on completion, never incrementally, and never inside or adjacent to a live-region node; if streaming is ever introduced it renders into a non-live region with a completion announcement. This costs nothing now and closes the hazard permanently.

### Semantics and naming

- **[medium]** Launch-readiness blocker provenance classes are distinguished by appearance for two of three. § Launch readiness rules requires the three classes to "stay visually distinct" and gives only the third — the PRD-declared conditions — "a visible provenance qualifier"; `DESIGN.md § launch-readiness-panel` repeats it as "visually distinguishable provenance classes, with a visible qualifier on the PRD-declared ones". Classes one and two are therefore separated by chip appearance alone, in a document whose own § Colors makes no-color-only mandatory for every status. Provenance is not cosmetic here: it tells the Release Operator whether a blocker is cleared in the launch-readiness register, in the runtime, or by a Product decision — three different recovery owners. The blocker *code* is visible text, so a reader who knows the vocabulary can infer the class; a reader who does not is being asked to read a colour. *Fix:* give all three classes a visible whole-string provenance label, not just the PRD-declared one, and drop "visually distinct" as the mechanism.

- **[medium]** The contrast inheritance the spine leans on hardest is the one it never names. `DESIGN.md § Colors` states the right targets per theme and says "Inherited Fluent role pairs are assumed AA at the pinned package and re-verified when the pin changes" — an acceptable form of the claim. But two lines later it makes `BadgeAppearance.Tint` the calm default for *every* status badge in the product across all seven roles, and tinted badge fills are exactly where Fluent's own AA guarantee is thinnest, because the guarantee covers the foreground/background role pairing rather than any particular appearance variant of a component. The spine's own rule elsewhere is that an inherited binding is named and verified (`DisabledFocusable`, `FixedHeaderFooter`, the `aria-describedby` splat). This one is asserted. *Fix:* name the combination explicitly — `FluentBadge` `Tint` foreground against `Tint` fill, per role, in Light and Dark — as a distinct row in the axe lane's evidence rather than folding it into "inherited role pairs", so a marginal role is caught at the pin rather than at a user.

- **[low]** The deterministic focus target's `aria-label` outlives the moment it was written for. § High-risk pending commands gives the status `gridcell` "an `aria-label` carrying the item identity and the new state" — correct at the instant of resolution, and permanent thereafter, because nothing says it is removed. An `aria-label` on a `gridcell` overrides its content for all subsequent reading, so from then on that column announces "Proposal {id}, Approved" in ordinary grid navigation instead of "Approved", duplicating the row identity in every cell traversal. *Fix:* say the composite label is applied for the focus move and reverts to the cell's own text once focus leaves it, or that the identity portion is carried by the row's `rowheader` instead.

- **[low]** The confirmation's scroll region is required to be accessibly named and no name is specified. `high-impact-confirmation` mandates "a focusable, accessibly named scroll region" so Confirm and Cancel stay reachable at 320 px — and neither spine supplies the string or the key, while § Voice and Tone's "announced events" and "architecture tokens" rows bound the inventory obligation to politeness-table events and contract tokens, so this name falls outside both and the parity gate will never see it. *Fix:* name it per family or generically (`{operation} details`) and add it to the localization inventory.

### Motion, timing, and density

- **[low]** `prefers-reduced-motion` is never bound to anything Agents renders. § Other criteria's motion rule is one sentence about what reduced motion must not hide, plus `MessageBarAnimation` disabled and no `pointerdown` activation — all correct, and none of it a reduced-motion binding. The informative role's glyph is `ArrowSync16`, the conventional rotating sync affordance, carried on `Submitted`, `AuthoritativePending`, `catching up`, `awaiting projection`, `generating` and `capacity queued`; nothing says whether it animates, and if a dev animates it, six of the product's most common states spin continuously with no reduced-motion path. *Fix:* one clause — Agents renders no looping or auto-playing animation, the role glyphs are static, and any progress affordance respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.

- **[low]** Zoom is proven only at 320 px reflow; 1.4.4 and 1.4.12 are unnamed. The reflow claim is strong and per-route, and 400% zoom at a 1280 px viewport is the 320 px case, so 200% is subsumed for layout. It does not cover 1.4.4 Resize Text (text-only enlargement, where a dense grid clips rather than reflows) or 1.4.12 Text Spacing, which is the criterion most likely to break a datagrid-heavy surface with reserved status-badge space and pinned columns — and `DESIGN.md § Layout & Spacing` explicitly reserves fixed space for badges, action slots and expiry labels. *Fix:* add both to § Other criteria with the grid named as the surface to test, and put the text-spacing override in the conformance lane beside the 320 px evidence.
