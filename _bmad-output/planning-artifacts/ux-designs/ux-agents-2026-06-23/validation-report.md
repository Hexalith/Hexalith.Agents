## Overall verdict

The FR-33 Update landed the authorization **model** and left the **plumbing** describing the world before it. That sentence is the governance lens's, and the other three arrived at it independently. What the Update set out to do, it did: § Authorization roles binds all six PRD roles to the five AD-30 policy constants; every one of the 15 IA `RequiredPolicy` cells is re-derived from an FR-33 row; the caller can no longer approve their own call; the `Caller` policy source is retired; cost caps are a two-tier lower-only editor with a Platform-Operator-only override; content safety splits into platform publication and a stricter-only tenant delta; deletion carries the A-12 two-person rule; retry posting moved to `ConversationPosting` with the A-7 bound restored. Fifty-eight of the baseline's 96 findings are verifiably closed, and the pair remains mechanically the cleanest it has been — 22/22/22 three-way component parity, all 19 token references resolving, all 37 source paths resolving, every `§` pointer resolving, no malformed rows.

The answer to this run's question is nonetheless **no, it did not land cleanly**, and it failed in the same shape as the Update before it: *a set, rule, or name was changed and the counts, rosters, cross-references and downstream statements that described the old state were left standing.* The pattern is confirmed at least a dozen times. "The four FR-18 exits" against six. "The six-part Provider eligibility set" against seven. "This four-name list" against two. "The ten families" against a roster the Update itself grew past — it declared the kill switch, the per-Conversation block, administrative abandon and administrative retry to be `high-impact-confirmation` families and added a contents row for none of them, so four justification-gated dialogs have no specified body and, since the rendered set *is* the audit record, no specified audit record either. A seam bullet still asserts the Conversation-owned removal the section above it replaced. A flow still walks one protagonist through three FR-33 roles without naming one. The two shipped-drift inventories the Update rewrote to fix a counting error both still miscount, and each omits the file the original finding named.

Three consequences are severe enough to name here. **First, the platform tier has no reachable surface.** No route in the spine is gated on `Agents.PlatformOperator`; every platform-scoped operation is a control on a route whose `RequiredPolicy` is tenant-scoped, and in shipped code the constant is unregistered, so those operations fall back to `Agents.Administrator` — recorded two lines after § Authorization roles forbids falling back to a more permissive row, with no fail-closed interim, on a page that has already shipped. **Second, the emergency stop misdescribes itself.** The kill-switch confirmation tells an operator that `Approved` proposals "complete or fail on their own terms" where FR-28 and AD-12 hold them without posting; it omits the edit and regeneration prohibition, the paused retry clock, and `ExpiredWhileSuspended`; and the Update then listed the kill switch as an `Abandoned` reason that FR-18 states it can never be. **Third, segregation of duties reached deletion and stopped.** A single Compliance Inspector still releases the legal hold that protects the evidence and exports it, against FR-30, FR-33 and AD-22 — and the compliance-inspection rule the Update wrote lets the Tenant Agent Administrator post-hoc-review an inspection of which they are the recorded subject.

Two further causes sit underneath. The rubric lens found that the Update **reconciled the FR-33 table and treated it as the whole 2026-09-09 PRD revision**, which also rewrote FR-3, FR-4, FR-18, FR-28 and FR-30 and added OQ-24 through OQ-30 — all seven return zero hits in both spines. Sixteen of that lens's twenty new findings trace to it. The implementation-readiness lens found the mirror image in the epics: `epics.md` carries **33 stories and the pair names 28**, and three of the five it never names own precisely what the Update added — Story 6.8 the pre-Provider rejections, Story 7.7 the entire `PostingFailed` recovery lane, Story 5.10 the `Caller` retirement. Two headline additions have no story with an acceptance criterion that would build them at all: the per-tenant kill switch and the Security approval stage, the latter also contradicting the Architecture Spine, where it is a recorded reference on the command rather than a stage.

The invented-constant failure that the `Agents.AuditOperator` revert corrected before this run was not the only one of its kind. It was the visible one. The Update also invented `KillSwitchActive`, a token in no source artifact, where Architecture's token is `Suspended` — which has no row anywhere in either spine. Both spines say `status: final`. On the authorization model, the lifecycle vocabulary, and story ownership, they are not.

**Zero prior findings regressed in the rubric and implementation-readiness lenses; three regressed across accessibility and governance.** All three are the same failure: a rendering that was *missing* became a rendering that is *wrong*. The `Abandoned` enumeration landed with a fourth value FR-18 forbids. The Audit evidence IA cell landed granting posted provenance — Conversation-derived content — to three role constants without membership. And the version-radio fix, having correctly established that `DisabledFocusable` does not exist on `FluentRadio`, replaced it with a rule that opens a modal on every arrow keypress in a native radiogroup, readmitting as a named exception the exact defect the Update's own generalisation had just closed.

## Findings by severity

### Critical (11 filings, 9 distinct)

**[Governance] The Platform Operator has no route it can enter, and the spine records a fallback its own rule forbids** (§ Authorization roles `EXPERIENCE.md:75,79`; § Shipped-code corrections `:733`; § Information Architecture `:98`)
§ Authorization roles states that a missing assignment "fails closed and never falls back to a more permissive row". § Shipped-code corrections then records the live state as `Agents.PlatformOperator` unregistered, so "every platform-scoped row therefore has no constant to gate on and falls back to `Agents.Administrator`" — verified against `AgentsFrontComposerRegistration.cs`, which registers `/agents/providers` at `AgentsAdministratorPolicy`. A whole scope tier failing *open* into the tenant tier, on a shipped page, recorded as a clerical row with no interim — unlike `EXT-SECRETS-1`, where the fail-closed interim is stated. The design side is the same defect: Launch readiness reads "`Agents.Operator`; the per-tenant kill switch `Agents.PlatformOperator`" — a route gated on a tenant constant carrying a control only the platform role may pull, so under the spine's own `[Authorize(Policy = ...)]` rule the Platform Operator is denied the page. Provider catalog, Content safety and Cost controls share the shape.
Fix: state that a surface carrying two tiers is gated on the disjunction with each control on its own constant, and write those routes as disjunctions the way Operational status already is; add the interim to the drift row — until the constant is registered, every platform-scoped mutation control is **absent** and every platform-scoped route renders `not available`, never gated on `Agents.Administrator`.

**[Rubric §4 + Governance] The kill-switch confirmation contradicts FR-28 and OQ-27 in the permissive direction** (§ Launch readiness rules `EXPERIENCE.md:308,317`; `agent-config-form` `:206`)
The confirmation states "`Approved` and `PostingPending` proposals complete or fail on their own terms". `prd.md:698` (FR-28), OQ-27 and AD-12 say an `Approved` proposal "is held in `Approved` without starting a post and without consuming a retry attempt" — only an in-flight `PostingPending` attempt completes. The sentence also drops "edited, or regenerated" from what awaiting proposals may not do, omits the paused `PostingFailed` retry clock (AD-12's `PausedDuration`), and omits `ExpiredWhileSuspended`. OQ-27 records this as a deliberate correction: "safety wins over 'complete on their own terms'". The identical defect sits in the Disable confirmation, which promises "pending proposals stay resolvable" where AD-12 permits reject and abandon only. Built from this spine, pulling the emergency stop still lets an Approver edit and regenerate, still starts posts for approved proposals, and still burns retry attempts.
Fix: restate both confirmation bodies to FR-28/AD-12 verbatim; add `Suspended` as an Agent readiness state distinct from `disabled`; add `ExpiredWhileSuspended` and the paused-window arithmetic to § Proposal lifecycle.

**[Governance] `Abandoned` names the kill switch as a typed reason FR-18 says it can never be** (§ Proposal lifecycle `EXPERIENCE.md:416`)
The row reads "one of four typed values … `NoEligibleApprover`, `SourceConversationUnavailable`, `RemovedInConversations`, or kill switch (FR-18)". FR-18 states the opposite in terms: the kill switch "is never a system-abandonment reason: while it is pulled, proposals awaiting a decision remain for humans to reject or abandon", and AD-12 repeats it. The Update added the requested typed enumeration and invented a fourth value; three exist. A rendered fourth reason is a rendered claim that the emergency stop destroys pending decisions — the one thing FR-28 promises it does not do. **Regression:** a missing rendering became a wrong one.
Fix: three typed reasons; state on the `Abandoned` row that the kill switch is never an abandonment reason and that awaiting proposals survive a pull for human decision.

**[Rubric §7 + Governance] Export and legal-hold release lost the second-party approval FR-30 and FR-33 require, while deletion gained it** (§ Information Architecture `EXPERIENCE.md:100`; § Audit governance rules `:320`)
The IA gates "Legal hold and export" on `Agents.AuditOperator` alone; § Audit governance rules gives a hold release nothing but "a hold release names the records it exposes". `prd.md:622` (FR-30): "Export requires prior second-party approval and hold release a separate approver (FR-33)", and FR-33's rows are explicit — release is "with the audited approval of a second Compliance Inspector or the Platform Operator"; export is "with prior approval by a second party on the FR-24 terms; never post hoc". AD-22 adds the computed-subject-set and anti-collusion terms. One Inspector therefore releases the hold protecting the evidence and exports it in two dialogs — the same single-actor chain the baseline named for deletion.
Fix: give `LegalHoldRelease` and `ExportRequest` the same request/approve/execute staging `DeletionRequest` has, with the approver identity and the "never post hoc" rule in their confirmation contents, and correct the IA cell.

**[Governance] The Audit evidence IA cell grants posted provenance to three role constants, contradicting its own rules section** (§ Information Architecture `EXPERIENCE.md:99` vs § Audit evidence rules `:314`)
The cell reads "Operational and posted-provenance evidence `Agents.Administrator`, `Agents.Operator`, or `Agents.PlatformOperator`". The rules section two hundred lines later states the correct rule: posted provenance by a current Participant with read access. FR-33's row and AD-22 agree — "a current Participant with read access inspects posted provenance only". Posted provenance is Conversation-derived content; the cell hands it to every tenant administrator, Release Operator and Platform Operator without membership, which is exactly the ambient-participation route Story 8.8 exists to prevent. **Regression**, and the FR-33-in-three-places redundancy failing in the direction that matters.
Fix: the cell reads operational evidence to the three roles; posted provenance to a current Participant with read access only; unposted protected content to Eligible Approver authority or the Story 8.8 inspection — matching § Audit evidence rules verbatim.

**[Governance] The compliance-inspection second-party rule permits self-review** (§ Audit evidence rules `EXPERIENCE.md:314`)
The spine allows "either second-party approval or a declared post-hoc review by the Tenant Agent Administrator". OQ-21 requires a second party "who is neither the Inspector nor a subject of the case", post-hoc review "only for single-proposal or single-Conversation scopes" within the A-19 window, and Platform Operator approval for any inspection wider than one Conversation. AD-22 computes the subject set, makes an approver inside it a typed rejection, bars two Inspectors approving each other within 30 days, and flags an unreviewed inspection persistently. The spine's sentence permits a case-wide inspection to proceed on the post-hoc review of the very administrator whose configuration is under examination.
Fix: render the four constraints as mechanics — computed subject set with typed refusal; post-hoc offered only at single-proposal or single-Conversation scope with the A-19 window shown; Platform Operator approval before the first content read at case scope; an `UnreviewedInspection` signal on the surfaces the TAA and Platform Operator read.
