# Governance & Audit Review — Hexalith Agents

Reviewer lens: governance, authorization, audit. Run 2026-09-09 against the spines updated 2026-09-09. Inputs read in full: `DESIGN.md`, `EXPERIENCE.md`, `.memlog.md`, `reconcile-validation-2026-09-09.md`, `reconcile-validation-2026-09-08.md`, the predecessor `.working/validation-2026-09-09-pre-update/review-governance.md`. Sources verified against rather than accepted: `../../prds/prd-agents-2026-06-23/prd.md` and `addendum.md`, `../../architecture/architecture-agents-2026-06-23-2/ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md`, `../../launch-readiness-register.md` (OperationGateMatrix v1), `../../external-dependency-register.md`, `../../epics.md` (Stories 6.5, 6.7, 7.x, 8.1–8.4, 8.7), the PRD's own `reconcile-validation-2026-09-09.md`, and the shipped `src/Hexalith.Agents.UI/Composition/AgentsFrontComposerRegistration.cs`. Adversary assumed throughout: a story-dev who builds exactly what the spines say; a hostile or merely self-interested tenant administrator; a caller who wants their own words in the Conversation; an operator who wants a mistake to disappear.

## Overall verdict

The 2026-09-09 Update did real work at the level it was aimed at. Where the previous review attacked the *mechanics* of a governed act — `Approved` staying editable, retry posting having no surface, deletion having no justification or scope, pricing currency circularity, the expiry clock — the spines now hold. Fourteen of the predecessor's twenty-one findings are genuinely closed and I could not reopen them. What the Update did not do is read the PRD it claims to reconcile against. `EXPERIENCE.md:31` states the spines are "reconciled to the PRD of 2026-09-08"; the current `prd.md` carries the 2026-09-09 amendments, and the largest of them is **FR-33, a thirteen-row authorization matrix over six named roles, which `OQ-21` declares authoritative for every authorization rule in the product**. Neither spine mentions FR-33, Platform Operator, Release Operator, Compliance Inspector, the kill switch, the Agents-owned per-Conversation block, `NoEligibleApprover`, or `SourceConversationUnavailable` — the strings do not occur in `DESIGN.md`, `EXPERIENCE.md`, `.memlog.md`, or either reconciliation. The consequence is not cosmetic. Built strictly from these two files, a tenant administrator holding `Agents.Administrator` publishes the platform Content Safety Policy, raises their own cost caps, and grants themselves an audited override of a reached cap — three acts FR-33 assigns to the Platform Operator and one it names "never the Tenant Agent Administrator". A caller approves the reply to their own Agent Call whenever a second Approver exists, because the spine wrote the segregation predicate as "sole Approver of their own call" where the PRD wrote "the caller is never an Eligible Approver". And `retry posting` — correctly identified as a Conversation write and correctly given a policy, a bound, a confirmation and audit rows — was filed under operation family `ProposalResolution`, which in OperationGateMatrix v1 requires neither `LR-CONVERSATIONS-MEMBERSHIP-POSTING` nor `LR-SAFETY`: the one new Conversation write in the spine is the one write that skips the membership and safety gates. Set against that, the areas the task asked me to attack hardest are in good shape: provider secrets are airtight but for one reason-code leak, the currency circularity is genuinely resolved with no residual loop, the expiry/approval race is server-timed on both sides, terminal proposals cannot be double-posted, and justification gating is specified down to whitespace rejection on both client and server. The spines are not unsafe because they are careless. They are unsafe because they are a version behind on exactly the requirement that says who may do what.

## Findings

### Authorization model and segregation of duties

- **[critical]** The FR-33 role matrix does not exist in either spine. `EXPERIENCE.md:59-80` binds every surface to one of five policy constants (`Agents.Administrator`, `Agents.Approver`, `Agents.Operator`, `Agents.AuditOperator`, `Agents.PlatformProviderAdministrator`); `prd.md:405-435` resolves every authorization rule in the product to one of six roles at a stated scope, and `OQ-21` declares FR-33 authoritative. Four of the six roles — Platform Operator, Release Operator, Compliance Inspector, Conversation Participant — have no representation in the spine at all, and `Agents.Administrator` silently absorbs three of them. `prd.md:433` requires that "Admin UI and API/client contracts apply the same matrix, and an operation not granted to the acting role fails closed with a typed denial before any side effect"; a UI built on five constants cannot apply a six-role matrix, and there is no surface that renders the role basis FR-33's fourth consequence requires every authorized operation to record. *Fix:* add an § Authorization roles section to `EXPERIENCE.md` mapping each of the six FR-33 roles to a policy constant and stating the scope; re-derive the `RequiredPolicy` column of the IA table from the FR-33 matrix row by row, splitting read from write per surface as the Provider catalog row already does; state that every confirmation's authorization-basis line names the FR-33 role and scope, and that the audit record carries it.

- **[critical]** Cost controls put the cap boundary and the cap override in the hands of the party they bound. `EXPERIENCE.md:67` gates `/agents/cost-controls` on tenant-scoped `Agents.Administrator`, and `EXPERIENCE.md:187` gives that surface authorship of monthly and per-call caps, per-Party and per-Conversation rate limits, and "the audited override (actor, justification, scope, expiry) is `TenantBudgetUpdate`". `prd.md:418` reserves cap and rate-limit configuration to "Platform Operator or Release Operator … the Tenant Agent Administrator may lower a cap or limit for their own tenant and never raise it, **so the boundary is not self-set**", and `prd.md:419` reserves the override to "Platform Operator only; never the Tenant Agent Administrator". FR-32 (`prd.md:625`) repeats both. There is no monotonicity rule anywhere in the spine: nothing says a lower-privileged editor may only decrease a cap, and nothing distinguishes who may create the override from who may see it. A tenant at 100% fail-closed simply raises its own cap, or grants itself an override, and both acts render as a normal `TenantBudgetUpdate` with a justification the same actor typed. *Fix:* split the Cost controls surface — Platform/Release Operator sets caps, rate limits, and overrides; `Agents.Administrator` gets a lower-only editor whose `FluentNumberInput` rejects any value above the current one with a typed, localized reason, and whose override item is absent, not disabled; the `TenantBudgetUpdate` confirmation renders the FR-33 role basis and, for a lower-only change, the statement that the boundary cannot be raised from this surface.

- **[critical]** The Content Safety Policy is published from a tenant-scoped surface, and the spine lets a tenant *relax* it. `EXPERIENCE.md:66` gates `/agents/content-safety` on `Agents.Administrator`; `EXPERIENCE.md:186` gives that surface draft validation and future-only publication through `PolicyPublication`, with restricted categories "editable only with an explicitly permitted tenant use case and Confirmation Response Mode". `prd.md:420` assigns policy publication to "Platform Operator with Security approval" at **Platform** scope and permits a tenant only to "add restrictions through a stricter mode-specific policy". Permitting a restricted category is a relaxation, not a restriction, and the Security-approval condition has no surface, no confirmation line, and no pending state anywhere in the spine. Under the spine as written, one tenant administrator publishes a policy version that permits hate or sexual content for the tenant and nothing in the UI requires a second party. *Fix:* the platform policy surface is Platform Operator plus a rendered Security-approval stage (a second acceptance stage in the `PolicyPublication` pending sequence, not a checkbox); the tenant surface publishes only a stricter delta and its editor cannot move a category from blocked to restricted or from restricted to permitted; the `PolicyPublication` confirmation states which direction the change moves and refuses to render a relaxation on the tenant surface.

- **[critical]** The two-person rule on deletion is deferred to a decision the PRD has already taken. `EXPERIENCE.md:189` says "Whether deletion additionally requires a second confirm stage by a different Party is deferred to Product and Security; until that decision lands, single-actor deletion with mandatory justification, scope rendering, and hold interlock is the specified behavior", and `reconcile-validation-2026-09-09.md:39` and `:65` record the deferral with owners. `prd.md:429` already states it: "Request approved deletion (FR-30) | **Platform Operator with Compliance Inspector approval** `[ASSUMPTION A-12]` | Tenant", and `prd.md:428` puts legal hold and export with the Compliance Inspector — a different role from the deletion requester. The spine collapses all three onto one `Agents.AuditOperator` on one route (`EXPERIENCE.md:73`), which is precisely the "release the hold, then request deletion, in two dialogs by one actor" path the predecessor review named. A deferral is a defensible answer to an open question; this question is closed, and V1 as specified ships single-actor evidence deletion against a written requirement. *Fix:* remove the deferral; `LegalHold` and `ExportRequest` require the Compliance Inspector role, `DeletionRequest` requires the Platform Operator role and renders a second, separately authorized Compliance Inspector approval stage before the request is submitted; the requester's own approval is refused with a typed reason; `audit-governance-panel` shows requester, approver, and both justifications on every deletion row. If A-12 is later retired in the other direction, that is a PRD change, not a UX deferral.

- **[high]** A caller can approve the reply to their own Agent Call. `EXPERIENCE.md:181` blocks approval "when the Approver last edited the selected version **or is the sole Approver of their own call**". `prd.md:91` defines Eligible Approver as a Party "that is **not the caller of the Agent Call**, and that is not the Party that last edited the version under decision", `prd.md:234` states "the approving Party must itself be an Eligible Approver … so a caller can never approve their own Agent Call", and `prd.md:501` puts `ApproverPolicySourceKind.Caller` on the deprecate-and-reject register for exactly this reason. The word "sole" inverts the rule: in any tenant with two or more resolvable Approvers — the normal case — the caller is not the sole Approver and the spine's predicate lets her through. This is the authorship-laundering path the task asked about, and it needs no laundering at all: the caller never authors anything, the Provider does, so the last-editor test never fires. `CanCurrentUserApproveSelectedVersion` (`EXPERIENCE.md:181`, `:617`) is specified to carry the spine's predicate, so the server-side rule the spine names is the wrong rule; the client block is backed, but backed by the wrong check. *Fix:* replace the clause with the FR-33/glossary predicate in full — the approving Party must be resolved by the current Approver Policy for this proposal, be a current Participant of the Source Conversation with current read access, not be the caller of the Agent Call, and not be the last editor of the selected version — and state that `CanCurrentUserApproveSelectedVersion` returns that predicate with its typed reason.

- **[high]** `approver-policy-builder` offers a retired policy source. `EXPERIENCE.md:178` builds authority from "`ConversationOwner` … `Caller`, `PredefinedParty`, `TenantRole`". `prd.md:215` (FR-7) states "The caller source is retired: `ApproverPolicySourceKind.Caller` is on the FR-23 deprecate-and-reject register", and `prd.md:501` requires it to be "rejected server-side with a typed rejection wherever it is presented". The spine renders a configuration row for a source every server will refuse, so an administrator can build and publish a policy that silently contributes no Approver — and, combined with the finding above, one whose only apparent authority is the caller. *Fix:* remove `Caller` from the builder's source list; if the value must remain visible for legacy policies, render it as a read-only `{colors.status-severe}` row with the typed retirement reason and block publication while it is present.

- **[high]** There is no edit-time eligibility check, so a self-inflicted deadlock is reachable and the PRD's remedy is missing. `EXPERIENCE.md:216` makes Edit unconditionally available in `Pending`, `Edited`, and `Regenerated`, and enforces segregation only at approval. `prd.md:235` requires the opposite: "At edit time, an edit is rejected with a typed reason before it is recorded unless at least one Eligible Approver would remain after it, the editor and the caller both excluded", and `prd.md:66` states the scenario explicitly — "had no second Approver been resolvable for this proposal, the system would have refused her edit rather than strand the proposal". Under the spine, a lone Approver edits, cannot approve her own edit, and the proposal sits until `Expired`; the audit record then reads as an expiry, not as a governance refusal. *Fix:* Edit is `DisabledFocusable` with the typed reason whenever no Eligible Approver would remain after the edit, sourced from the same Story 7.4 flag family as `CanCurrentUserApproveSelectedVersion`; the reason names that a second Approver is required, not that approval is blocked.

- **[medium]** `CanCurrentUserApproveSelectedVersion` is specified to carry two of the five Eligible Approver conditions. `EXPERIENCE.md:181` and the contracts-that-must-grow row at `EXPERIENCE.md:617` describe it as the dirty/last-editor/sole-approver pre-state; `prd.md:91` names five conjuncts, including current Participant membership at the moment of the action and current read access, both of which FR-7 requires to be re-checked "at discovery, edit, regeneration, approval, rejection, abandonment, and audit-content inspection". A pre-state narrower than the server rule produces an enabled Approve that the server refuses — recoverable, but it trains approvers to treat refusal as a glitch. *Fix:* state that the flag returns the full predicate with one typed reason per failing conjunct, and that the reason is the copy rendered on the `aria-disabled` control.

### Provider catalog: the authorization split and what the read path leaks

- **[high]** `Agents.PlatformProviderAdministrator` does not exist. `EXPERIENCE.md:80` states as fact: "Policy constants registered in `AgentsFrontComposerRegistration`: `Agents.Administrator`, `Agents.Approver`, `Agents.Operator`, `Agents.AuditOperator` are tenant-scoped, and `Agents.PlatformProviderAdministrator` is platform-scoped." The shipped file declares exactly four constants — `AgentsAdministratorPolicy`, `AgentsApproverPolicy`, `AgentsOperatorPolicy`, `AgentsAuditOperatorPolicy` — and registers `/agents/providers` at nav order 2 with `RequiredPolicy: AgentsAdministratorPolicy`. The § Shipped-code corrections table (`EXPERIENCE.md:625-637`) catches nine smaller divergences, including "Launch readiness gated by `Agents.Administrator`", and misses this one, so the single new authorization constant in the whole Update has no owning story and reads to a story-dev as already done. *Fix:* add two rows to § Shipped-code corrections — the missing constant and the Provider catalog's tenant-scoped `RequiredPolicy` — with absorbing story 5.3 or 5.7; state in the IA rules that the constant is to be registered, not that it is registered.

- **[high]** The catalog read path shows a tenant administrator what FR-33 reserves to the Platform Operator. `EXPERIENCE.md:63` and `:179` give `Agents.Administrator` the whole platform-scoped grid: "enablement, configured state, capability limits, pricing version …, currency, unit prices, `CapabilityVersion`, readiness triple, freshness", with `ConfigurationReferenceId` "operator-only visible" and masked. `prd.md:417` (FR-33 row 2, `[ASSUMPTION A-10]`) states: "A tenant sees and can select only Providers/models **enabled for it**; secret references and configured state are visible **only to the Platform Operator**." The spine scopes the *mutation* correctly and never scopes the *read* at all — no per-tenant enablement filter, no suppression of configured state, and `provider-status-badge` renders `Blocked / Blocked / <ReasonCode>` (`EXPERIENCE.md:292`) over a reason vocabulary that includes `Unconfigured` and `SecretUnavailable` (`launch-readiness-register.md § Provider Readiness Contract`), which discloses secret-configuration state through the status channel even where the reference itself is masked. "Operator-only visible" remains a disclosure category with no policy behind it on a page whose only server gate is `Agents.Administrator` — the predecessor's low finding 19, still open. *Fix:* the tenant read is filtered to models enabled for the acting tenant; configured state, `ConfigurationReferenceId`, and any secret-derived `ReasonCode` (`Unconfigured`, `SecretUnavailable`) collapse to one undifferentiated `Blocked` reason for a non-Platform-Operator reader; name the policy that may see the masked reference and state it is a subset of the mutation policy.

- **[high]** A zero-priced model turns budget enforcement off, and the spine treats it as a warning. `EXPERIENCE.md:179` requires a zero unit price to be typed explicitly and carries "a warning line that a zero-priced model reserves nothing"; `DESIGN.md:203` colours a zero-priced model `{colors.status-warning}` — "attention soon". `prd.md:616` makes the reservation the enforcement mechanism ("reserves the estimated attempt cost … priced at the current pricing version") and `prd.md:620` makes an unconfigured cap a readiness blocker. A model priced at zero reserves zero, settles zero, and never reaches 80% or 100% on any cap: `BudgetBlocked` becomes unreachable for every tenant using it, from one platform-scoped edit, with no readiness consequence. That is the budget bypass, and it is one field. *Fix:* a zero unit price is a Provider readiness blocker (`Blocked / Blocked / Unpriced` or a new reason code), not a warning; the `ProviderCatalogMutation` confirmation states that setting zero disables cost enforcement for every tenant using the model and names the tenant count; Cost controls renders an explicit "cost enforcement inactive for {model}" blocker on any tenant whose selected model is zero-priced.

- **[medium]** "Absent, not disabled" is stated three times and contradicted by the layout contract. `EXPERIENCE.md:80`, `EXPERIENCE.md:179`, and `DESIGN.md:299` all say mutation controls are absent without the platform policy; `DESIGN.md:248` maps the Provider catalog to "Primary region: the grid / Accordion items: **Editor**" unconditionally, and the `provider-catalog-grid` variant list (`DESIGN.md:299`) has no no-mutation variant. A story-dev renders the Editor accordion item and hides its buttons — which is "disabled" with extra steps, and leaks the field inventory including the secret-reference control. *Fix:* add a `read-only (no mutation policy)` variant to the component's variant list and state in the layout table that the Editor accordion item itself is absent in that variant.

### Terminal-state and posting integrity

- **[critical]** `retry posting` is filed under the operation family that omits the two gates a Conversation write needs. `EXPERIENCE.md:222` and `:231` place it "under family `ProposalResolution`". `launch-readiness-register.md § Gate Sets And Non-Circular Evaluation` defines `ProposalResolution` as requiring `LR-TOPOLOGY`, `LR-EVENTSTORE`, `LR-TENANT-ACCESS`, `LR-AUDIT-PROTECTION-DELETION` — and defines a separate `ConversationPosting` family requiring, additionally, `LR-CONVERSATIONS-MEMBERSHIP-POSTING` and `LR-SAFETY`. The register states the matrix "is the single contract consumed by API, BFF, UI, workflow, and the `launch-readiness` projection" and that "consumers may not maintain local subsets". The spine correctly identifies retry posting as "a Conversation write … governed as one" and then assigns it the family that governs it as something else, so every retry attempt is admitted with the membership and safety gates unevaluated — against FR-18's own pre-post re-validation list (`prd.md:397`), which requires membership and then-current safety to be re-checked before each post. *Fix:* `retry posting` is family `ConversationPosting`; its confirmation's authorization-basis line names both families where the resolution and the write differ; § Confirmation contents by family moves the retry row under `ConversationPosting` and keeps the `ApprovedVersionId`, typed reason, attempts-used, and pre-post re-check contents.

- **[high]** The retry bound was moved from Architecture to tenant configuration and lost its time window. `EXPERIENCE.md:222` states "the maximum number of attempts is a `hexa` configuration field", and `EXPERIENCE.md:177` lists `hexa` configuration fields with documented defaults and ranges for expiry and the regeneration ceiling but none for retries. `prd.md:396` fixes the bound: "a bounded audited retry of **at most 3 attempts over 15 minutes** `[ASSUMPTION A-7]`", owned by Architecture per `prd.md:749`. A tenant-configurable attempt count with no upper bound and no window is a retry storm against Conversations with an audit trail that says every attempt was authorized. *Fix:* the bound is 3 attempts within 15 minutes of the first failure, not tenant-configurable; the confirmation renders attempts used of 3 and the time remaining in the window; window expiry is a distinct exhaustion reason from attempt exhaustion.

- **[high]** Both PRD exits for an exhausted `PostingFailed` proposal are missing, so the spine strands a proposal the PRD guarantees always has an exit. `EXPERIENCE.md:219` gives `PostingFailed`, retries exhausted exactly one action: "`Start a new Agent Call` only". `prd.md:396` names four exits — an Eligible Approver abandons, **the Tenant Agent Administrator abandons it under the audited FR-33 row that needs no Conversation read access**, **an audited administrative retry succeeds**, or the system abandons it — and concludes "A `PostingFailed` proposal therefore always has an exit and always reaches the §9 retention clock". `prd.md:425-426` gives both administrative rows their own FR-33 entries. Neither exists in the spine: proposal detail is `Agents.Approver` only (`EXPERIENCE.md:69`), retry posting requires *current* Conversation read access (`EXPERIENCE.md:222`), and there is no administrator-facing proposal surface at all. A proposal whose Source Conversation became inaccessible is therefore unreachable by anyone, and never reaches the retention clock. *Fix:* add both FR-33 rows to the action rail as administrator-visible actions on the same detail route under a second policy — administrative abandon (no Conversation read access required, audited, justification) and administrative retry after exhaustion (audited, justification, its own bound) — each with its own confirmation contents row.

- **[high]** The blanket `Expired`-on-any-read rule overwrites states the PRD says never expire. `EXPERIENCE.md:334` says `Expired` is "Rendered whenever `ExpiresAt` has passed on any read, regardless of timer delivery", with no state precondition. `prd.md:395` states: "`ExpiresAt` applies only while a proposal is awaiting a decision. A proposal whose stored `ExpiresAt` has passed **while `Pending`, `Edited`, or `Regenerated`** is treated as `Expired` … **Approval freezes expiry: `Approved`, `PostingPending`, and `PostingFailed` proposals never expire.**" Twenty-four hours after a posting failure the spine renders `Expired` over a `PostingFailed` proposal, which by `EXPERIENCE.md:220` collapses the rail to `Start a new Agent Call` — the retry path and the administrative exits vanish, and the audit surface reports a decision that expired rather than a post that failed. *Fix:* scope the rule: `Expired` is rendered on read only while the proposal is `Pending`, `Edited`, or `Regenerated`; state in the `Approved`, `PostingPending`, and `PostingFailed` rows that expiry is frozen.

- **[medium]** The `Abandoned` reason set omits the typed system reasons. `EXPERIENCE.md:333` lists "(Approver, policy, Agent removed from Conversation)". `prd.md:392` requires the typed reason `NoEligibleApprover`, `SourceConversationUnavailable`, `RemovedInConversations`, or kill switch, carried "in Audit Evidence and in status", excluded from the SM-3 denominator and "reported separately under FR-25 and SM-C4". Two of the four have no rendering anywhere in the spine, and `prd.md:236` makes `NoEligibleApprover` the outcome of the segregation-of-duties re-check — the very abandonment an approver most needs explained. *Fix:* enumerate the four typed reasons in the `Abandoned` row with a localized label each; state that system-abandoned proposals are counted separately from Approver-abandoned on Operational status per FR-25.

- **[low]** Terminal-state integrity is otherwise airtight, and I tried to break it. `Approved`-once-projection-confirmed removes edit, regenerate, and approve and pins `ApprovedVersionId` read-only (`EXPERIENCE.md:217`, `:328`, `DESIGN.md:307`), so no second version can be approved. Double-posting is closed three ways: the deterministic `MessageId` reused across attempts (`EXPERIENCE.md:331`), "Duplicate submission is blocked per session, resource, and family" (`EXPERIENCE.md:183`), and `duplicate submission (idempotent)` showing the existing pending state rather than a second one (`EXPERIENCE.md:346`), with EventStore optimistic concurrency named authoritative "across tabs, sessions, retries, replay, and restarts" (`EXPERIENCE.md:368`). The expiry/approval race compares both sides against server time carried on the read (`EXPERIENCE.md:334`) and resolves to the display-only `expired at approval` (`EXPERIENCE.md:345`). Retry after losing Conversation access is blocked by the current-read-access condition on the rail. No fix.

### Governed acts with no surface

- **[high]** The per-tenant kill switch has no surface, and the spine declares surface closure final. `EXPERIENCE.md:84` states "Surface closure status: **final**. Every stated need has a surface and every surface has a journey below." `prd.md:422` makes pulling the kill switch an FR-33 operation (Platform Operator, or Release Operator on recorded triggers) and `prd.md:622` gives it precise proposal semantics: it "disables Agent Calls tenant-wide, lets proposals awaiting a decision be rejected or abandoned **but not approved**, lets `Approved` and `PostingPending` proposals complete or fail on their own terms, and deletes no proposal or Audit Evidence". None of that is renderable from these spines: there is no control, no confirmation family, no readiness state, no proposal-editor variant in which approve is absent for this reason, and no `Abandoned` reason for it. *Fix:* add a kill-switch item to the Launch readiness or a governance surface under the Release/Platform Operator role with its own confirmation family and required justification; add a `kill switch active` Agent readiness state; add a proposal-editor variant in which Approve is absent with the kill-switch reason while Reject and Abandon remain.

- **[high]** The Agents-owned per-Conversation block is missing, and the spine describes the design the PRD rejected. `EXPERIENCE.md:104` states "Removal of `hexa` is a Conversation-owned action. Agents reacts by blocking further calls in that Conversation." `addendum.md § Options Considered` records the opposite decision: "Removal notification. Requiring Hexalith.Conversations to notify Agents of participant removal was **rejected** in favor of an Agents-owned per-Conversation block, which needs no new Conversations seam." `prd.md` FR-2 makes the block Agents-owned, "set and cleared only by those authorities" — Tenant Agent Administrator or Conversation Facilitator, `[A-9]` — with "every set and clear … audited", and `prd.md:428` gives removal, block, and re-admit its own FR-33 row. The spine has no surface to set, clear, or re-admit, no audit rendering for either transition, and builds its Conversation-seam behavior on the rejected alternative. *Fix:* add block/re-admit to an Agents surface under the FR-33 authorities, each a governed write with justification and audit; restate the seam paragraph so removal is an Agents-owned block reacting to a Conversation signal, not a Conversations-owned action.

- **[high]** A denied or unauthorized attempt leaves no visible evidence anywhere. The single `not available` state (`EXPERIENCE.md:81`, `:140`, `:383`) is correct as isolation — identical copy, class, and timing for unauthorized, foreign-tenant, and non-existent — but nothing in the spine renders the *other* side of it: `operational-status-panel` (`EXPERIENCE.md:191`) counts calls blocked by context, safety, cost, rate limit, and capacity but not authorization denials, and `audit-evidence-panel` (`EXPERIENCE.md:192`) enumerates evidence for acts that happened. `prd.md:826` (SM-4) requires "zero successful cross-tenant or unauthorized Agent Calls, proposal actions, or audit inspections" to be demonstrable, and `prd.md:622` makes "any confirmed cross-tenant or unauthorized action (SM-4)" the immediate kill-switch trigger. A trigger nobody can observe is not a control. `prd.md:454` (FR-20) requires authorization decisions to be auditable "at a level sufficient to explain denial or approval". *Fix:* add a denied-attempt evidence class to `audit-evidence-panel` — actor, tenant, operation family, resource class, denial reason code, timestamp, without the inaccessible resource's content or identity — and a per-tenant denial count to Operational status, so SM-4 and the kill-switch trigger have a surface.

### Audit evidence completeness

- **[high]** Compliance inspection is absent, and the fail-closed rule the spine does state makes retained evidence permanently uninspectable — which FR-24 forbids in terms. `EXPERIENCE.md:192` says "Evidence containing Conversation-derived content requires current Conversation read access and fails closed otherwise." `prd.md` FR-24 states the opposite outcome: "When the Source Conversation no longer exists or is inaccessible, participant-based access is unavailable and the evidence remains inspectable under compliance inspection only. **Retained evidence is never made uninspectable by the loss of its Conversation.**" It further requires that compliance inspection be "scoped to a named Conversation or case identifier, requires a recorded justification and either second-party approval or post-hoc review by the Tenant Agent Administrator, is rate-visible on an audit surface the Tenant Agent Administrator can read, and is itself recorded as Audit Evidence"; the glossary entry at `prd.md:94` and the FR-33 row at `prd.md:424` say the same. None of the five mechanics — the case scope, the justification, the second-party-or-post-hoc gate, the rate-visible surface, the recording of the inspection itself — appears in either spine. The failure runs both ways: a legitimate inspection is blocked, and the one read path in the product that is meant to be *audited as a read* is not. *Fix:* add a compliance-inspection flow to `/agents/audit`: a scoped request naming a Conversation or case identifier with a required justification, opened by an explicit control, recorded as its own governance evidence entry; a second-party approval stage or a declared post-hoc-review mode visible to the Tenant Agent Administrator; an inspection-rate item on an administrator-readable surface; and a rule that evidence whose Source Conversation is gone renders under compliance inspection only, never as `not available`.

- **[medium]** `/agents/audit` as an id-entry surface removes the ability to ask whether anything is missing. `EXPERIENCE.md:72` and `DESIGN.md:241` declare it "an id-entry surface, not a grid: it accepts an interaction, proposal, or governance-operation reference and routes to the detail, so it declares no list contract, columns, filters, or sort". Discoverability is not the only casualty. FR-24 requires compliance inspection to be "rate-visible on an audit surface the Tenant Agent Administrator can read" — a rate is a list property. `prd.md:827` (SM-5) requires that "every posted Agent Response in the qualification cohort has complete Audit Evidence", and FR-24's first consequence is traceability across a population. On a surface where you must already know each identifier, the inverse question — is every governed act auditable, and is any entry missing — cannot be asked at all. - **[medium]** The governance evidence contract names the actor but not the tenant or the outcome. `EXPERIENCE.md:192` specifies "actor, operation family, resource identity, old-to-new values where safe to expose …, published version, concurrency token, projection id and version, justification, and the acceptance stages the command passed". `prd.md:434` requires that "Every authorized operation records the role basis on which it was permitted", `prd.md:441` requires audit/status queries to return only tenant-authorized records, and FR-33's consequence set requires a typed denial before any side effect. ### Proposal disclosure and tenant isolation

- **[high]** The queue's listing rule is wider than FR-13's. `EXPERIENCE.md:180` states `proposal-queue-grid` "Lists only proposals whose Source Conversation the Approver can currently read". `prd.md:338` states the rule in two parts: full content only for readable Source Conversations, existence and state only for a requester "on which the requester was **previously resolved as an Approver** but has since lost read access", and — the part the spine dropped — "Proposals on which the requester was **never resolved as an Approver** are not listed, counted, or otherwise disclosed." Since `Agents.Approver` is a tenant-wide policy constant and Conversation read access is a Conversations fact, any holder of `Agents.Approver` who happens to be a participant of a Conversation sees, counts, and opens proposals for which the Approver Policy never resolved them. The pending count (`EXPERIENCE.md:190`) inherits the same rule through `needs my action`. *Fix:* the listing predicate is resolution under the proposal's Approver Policy snapshot **and** current read access; `existence only` applies to previously-resolved Approvers who lost access; a never-resolved requester gets the `not available` state on a deep link and no row, no count, and no filter suggestion.
