## Foundation

Hexalith Agents is a desktop-first responsive web experience composed through **Hexalith.FrontComposer**. The shell owns header, navigation, account controls, theme and density settings, command palette, skip links, keyboard shell behavior, shell localization, and shell status chrome. The Agents domain owns registered navigation entries, page bodies, domain copy, page-level live regions, Agent and proposal workflow behavior, and BFF/API-facing interaction.

The UI system is inherited: Microsoft Fluent UI Blazor v5 through FrontComposer. `DESIGN.md` is the visual identity reference and names the pinned package. This spine references `DESIGN.md` tokens by name and does not restate visual styling. Agents defines no custom theme.

This is an internal governed operational tool, not regulated UX. Product-required authorization, tenant isolation, approval, proposal versioning, provider-secret safety, cost governance, and audit evidence are first-class. Copy is plain and precise.

V1 exposes only `hexa` as product behavior. V1 excludes long-term memory, tools, project and folder content, ambient triggers, external channels, customer-facing billing, and business actions beyond posting automatic or approved replies to Conversations. These non-goals are stated here once.

By decision, no mockups or wireframes were produced; the spines are the sole reference. The decision, its date and its denominator are stated once in `DESIGN.md § Brand & Style`.

## Inspiration & Anti-patterns

The PRD addendum positions Hexalith Agents against Slack AI, Microsoft 365 Copilot in Teams, Zoom AI Companion, and Atlassian Rovo Agents.

- Lifted: clear admin control over AI availability and caller access.
- Lifted: answering in place from Conversation context rather than in a separate AI workspace.
- Rejected: generic summarization as the product promise; this is governed participation by a named Party identity.
- Rejected: unapproved generated content in the durable Conversation record.
- Rejected: Provider and model opacity; identity is in audit evidence without secrets.

## Authorization roles

Every authorization rule in this spine resolves to one of the six PRD FR-33 roles at a stated scope. FR-33 is the authoritative matrix (OQ-21); this section binds each role to the policy constant a route or control is gated on, and nothing in this spine grants an operation FR-33 withholds.

| FR-33 role | Scope | Policy constant | Resolved by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform Operator | Platform | `Agents.PlatformOperator` | Platform role assignment; never tenant-scoped |
| Tenant Agent Administrator | Tenant | `Agents.Administrator` | Tenant role assignment |
| Release Operator | Tenant | `Agents.Operator` | Tenant role assignment |
| Approver | Proposal | `Agents.Approver` plus per-proposal resolution under FR-7 | The proposal's Approver Policy snapshot; the constant alone authorizes no proposal action |
| Compliance Inspector | Tenant | `Agents.AuditOperator` | Tenant role assignment |
| Conversation Participant | Conversation | none | Conversation membership and current read access through the Conversations seam, plus the Agent call permission below |

Two authorities are not Agents roles and never become policy constants. The **Conversation Facilitator** is a Conversations role resolved normatively from `ParticipantRole.Facilitator` (A-3) and is an authority on two rows only: Approver Policy resolution (FR-7) and removing, blocking, or re-admitting `hexa` (FR-2, A-9). **Security approval** is a condition on `PolicyPublication` (FR-26, FR-33), not a role and not a policy constant. AD-30 binds it as **a recorded Security approval reference carried on the `PolicyPublication` command**, so this spine renders it as a required, separately-sourced reference field on the publish confirmation rather than as a second pending stage.

The Compliance Inspector role is carried on the stable policy identifier `Agents.AuditOperator` and labeled Compliance Inspector on every surface, exactly as `ApproverPolicySourceKind.ConversationOwner` is labeled Conversation Facilitator. AD-30 is the naming authority for every policy constant and maps the six FR-33 roles onto five of them; `prd.md` names the roles and no constants. Renaming the identifier requires an AD-30 amendment, not a UX decision.

Rules:

- A missing, stale, or unavailable role assignment fails closed and never falls back to a more permissive row.
- Every role except Platform Operator is tenant-scoped: an assignment in one tenant grants nothing in another (FR-19).
- **Tenant binding for a platform-scoped operation acting at tenant scope.** Six FR-33 rows are platform-authorized and tenant-scoped: enable a Provider/model for a tenant (A-10), declare a `DataHandlingVersion` tightening, configure cost caps and rate limits, override a reached cost cap, pull and release the per-tenant kill switch, and request approved deletion. Each resolves its target tenant through the **shell tenant switcher** — the already-established shell tenant context — and through nothing else. **No Agents surface renders an in-surface tenant selector and none accepts a free-text tenant id**: a selector enumerating tenants would publish the tenant inventory to the surface FR-19 exists to protect, and free text puts the kill switch one typo away from suspending the wrong tenant. The **resolved tenant renders in the confirmation** and is carried in the audit record **as part of the authorization basis**, beside the FR-33 role and scope (FR-19, FR-33, FR-20). § Confirmation contents by family states the confirmation half once, as a required content of every family, not per row.
- Every governed write renders its **authorization basis** — the FR-33 role and scope it was permitted on — in its confirmation, and the audit record carries the same basis (FR-33, FR-20). § Confirmation contents by family states this once as a required content of every family.
- **Agent call permission.** Every Conversation Participant may call `hexa` where it is active, unless the Tenant Agent Administrator restricts calling to a tenant role (A-11). That restriction is the Agent call permission that FR-8 and FR-20 enforce, and it is a field on `agent-config-form`. The server decision is authoritative.
- Constants to be registered in `AgentsFrontComposerRegistration` (AD-30): `Agents.PlatformOperator` is platform-scoped; `Agents.Administrator`, `Agents.Operator`, `Agents.Approver`, and `Agents.AuditOperator` are tenant-scoped. The shipped registration declares four of the five and is missing only `Agents.PlatformOperator`; § Shipped-code corrections carries that one divergence.
- The rows carrying an `[ASSUMPTION]` key in FR-33 — A-9 removal authority, A-10 per-tenant Provider enablement, A-11 call permission, A-12 deletion approval — are unretired Product assumptions, so each is an `RQ-1` blocker of type `UnretiredAssumption` that `launch-readiness-panel` renders (FR-28).

Rules:

- `/agents/conversation-call` is a pre-integration harness, not an alternate entry point. It is delisted from navigation now. **Story 6.7 removes it: the `Order: 4` "Conversation call" entry in `AgentsFrontComposerRegistration.cs` is unregistered and `Components/Pages/ConversationCall.razor` is deleted before 6.7 closes**, because `AlternateInvocationGuardTests` must prove absence and a merely blocked route is still an `[Authorize]` surface. While it exists it carries `Agents.Administrator`, is excluded from `LR-UI-CONFORMANCE`, and writes nothing in a production-like `EnvironmentProfile`. The terminal-state `Start a new Agent Call` action navigates to the Source Conversation, never to the harness.
- Every `RequiredPolicy` cell above is derived from the FR-33 matrix row for that operation; the role-to-constant binding and its rules are stated once in § Authorization roles. Where a surface splits read from write, the narrower constant governs the write and the mutation controls are **absent**, not disabled, for a reader who lacks it.
- **A surface carrying operations of two authorization tiers is gated at the route on the _broadest admitting constant_, with every control on its own narrow constant and the server decision authoritative** (decision, 2026-09-12). Gating such a route on the tenant constant alone denies the page to the very Platform Operator whose control it carries: Launch readiness (kill switch), Provider catalog (every `ProviderCatalogMutation`), Content safety policy (platform publication), Cost controls (caps, rate limits, audited override), Operational status and Audit evidence all have this shape. Every one of those `RequiredPolicy` cells is written in the Launch readiness shape — the admitting constants named with `or`, the narrow constant named beside each control — and the route admits the broadest of them. **No composite constant is minted for the union and no AD-30 amendment is filed**: AD-30's five constants are the whole vocabulary, and a route may not invent a sixth. A control the viewer's constant does not grant is **absent**, not disabled, and the server decision governs whatever the route admitted.
- The residual of the rule above is the **nav entry, not the route**: `FrontComposerNavEntry.RequiredPolicy` is one nullable string consumed by a single `<AuthorizeView Policy="@entry.RequiredPolicy">`, so an entry can declare exactly one constant, and registering a second entry per constant would put two rail tiles on one surface. **That duplication question is filed as a FrontComposer request** — a `RequiredPolicy` that accepts a set, or one entry admitting several policies without duplicating the tile — on the same terms as the six outstanding nav-glyph requests in `DESIGN.md § Brand & Style`: a capability the curated shell lacks is requested and never worked around by a deliberate duplicate. It is recorded here rather than there because it is a registration-contract request and not a glyph, and it is cross-indexed on the Policy-gated nav row of § FrontComposer Readiness. Until it lands, each surface registers **one** entry on the constant its primary role holds; an admitted role outside that constant reaches the surface by direct navigation, which withholds no grant because navigation hiding is disclosure, not authorization. Showing a cross-tenant blast-radius count in a confirmation is disclosure control, not authorization, and never substitutes for the split (FR-19).
- Navigation hiding is disclosure, not authorization. Unauthorized entries are hidden. Direct navigation to an unauthorized, foreign-tenant, or non-existent id renders the single `not available` state defined under State Patterns. Every page also carries `[Authorize(Policy = ...)]` and the server decision is authoritative.
- Nav glyphs come only from `FcFluentIcons`. An unset or unresolvable icon key is **not** a no-glyph path: `FrontComposerNavigation.ResolveNavEntryIcon` falls back to `Apps20`, which is the Agents overview glyph, so an entry awaiting a request renders a duplicate of Overview rather than nothing. The six affected entries, the accepted duplication, and the outstanding requests are listed in `DESIGN.md § Brand & Style`.

Three governed operations FR-33 names have no route of their own and are carried as controls on existing surfaces: the **per-tenant kill switch** (FR-28) on Launch readiness; **removal, block, and re-admit of `hexa` in a Conversation** (FR-2, OQ-16, A-9) on Operational status, beside that Conversation's `safety blocked` history; and the **abandon and administrative retry of a `PostingFailed` automatic posting record** (FR-11, FR-33) on Operational status, beside that record's failure row, because the automatic path creates no proposal and § Proposal editor action rail therefore cannot carry them. All three are `high-impact-confirmation` families with a required justification, and every one of them is audited.

Surface closure status: **final for V1**. Every stated need has a surface and every surface has a journey below; the three operations above are controls, not routes, by decision. Behavior at the seam:

- **Call hexa** renders visible but `DisabledFocusable` with the safe blocker text whenever `hexa` is not proven callable for the tenant, resolved through `GetCallabilityAsync`. It is absent for Parties without Conversation access or Agent call permission.
- None of the panel's events happen on an Agents route, so the panel routes every announcement to the persistent contributed region and returns focus only through the Conversations-owned focus-return contract. This split is an `EXT-CONV-UI-1` requirement rather than only a spine rule.
- `hexa` joins a Conversation as a participant at the first accepted Agent Call, before generation, under the platform Agents service principal. The join is idempotent. Membership failure fails the call closed with no Provider work, proposal, or Conversation Message. Membership is visible on the same terms as any other participant.
- Removal of `hexa` from a Conversation originates in Conversations, but Agents **detects it lazily at the next FR-2 membership step** rather than receiving a push, and records it as `ExternallyRemoved` in the Conversation Agent State (OQ-25). Agents then blocks further calls in that Conversation and abandons every proposal the FR-18 table permits abandoning, with the typed reason `RemovedInConversations`, versions preserved. `PostingPending` is never interrupted by it. This is the detection path; the **Agents-owned block, clear, and re-admit controls** are a separate authority, specified under § Conversation Agent State.
- **Announcement division.** Agents' one-pair rule is scoped to Agents routes and to the closed FrontComposer speaker list, and the Conversation view is neither: it is a chat surface owned by another module, and the single most likely thing a chat surface announces is the arrival of a new message — which is exactly the event Agents' persistent region announces as `Posted`, on the product's only V1 invocation path, with `agent-response-marker` contributed into that same message. Agents cannot close this alone, so the seam carries the division: **the host declares its own announcement behaviour for contributed content, and Agents suppresses whichever of `Posted` or new-message arrival the host already speaks.** Until that declaration exists this is an **open seam item** with the risk stated rather than resolved: Agents speaks `Posted`, the duplicate stands where the host also speaks, and NFR-14's `LiveRegionAnnouncedTick` observes the Agents node only, so the duplicate corrupts no measurement and would ship invisible. Story 6.7 closes it with the declaration, not with an Agents-side guess.
- `agent-response-marker` is contributed through the per-message decoration slot, not Story 6.7's action contribution, which cannot carry per-message attribution. Its rules are in the `agent-response-marker` row.
- Conversation content is untrusted data. No contributed surface presents Conversation content as an instruction, and a recorded redirect attempt is visible in audit as a safety outcome (FR-31).

### Proposal editor action rail

The rail holds Edit, **Save**, **Discard**, Regenerate, Approve, Reject, Abandon, and — for `Agents.Administrator` alone — **`administrative retry`**. **There is no Approver-facing Retry posting control in this rail**: FR-33 grants no Approver a retry of any kind. Availability is a function of state, not of policy alone. Save commits the draft as a new preserved version under operation family **`ProposalEdit`** and opens `high-impact-confirmation` with that family's contents; Discard is **UI-local**, writes nothing, declares no operation family, and reverts the draft. Both render only while the editor is dirty and both are announced in the politeness table — `draft saved` and `draft discarded`. Focus after either one goes to **Approve**, not back to the activated control: Save and Discard each clear the dirty state by definition, so both unrender at the exact commit that would return focus to them, dropping focus to `document.body`. Approve is the control the dirty state was blocking and becomes activatable at the same commit.

| Proposal state | Available actions |
|---|---|
| `Pending`, `Edited`, `Regenerated` | Edit, Save and Discard while dirty, Regenerate, Approve, Reject, Abandon. Approve is `DisabledFocusable` while the editor is dirty, or when `CanCurrentUserApproveSelectedVersion` is false. Regenerate is `DisabledFocusable` at the ceiling, while a generation is in flight, while any resolution is pending, or when the server verdict says excluding this requester would leave no Eligible Approver; that last guard runs before Provider work. |
| `Approved`, `PostingPending` | Retry posting is not yet applicable; abandon where permitted; audit. Edit, Regenerate, and Approve are **absent** once `Approved` is projection-confirmed, and the editor pins `ApprovedVersionId` read-only, so no other version can be approved or posted and no edit can land while posting is pending (FR-17, FR-18). |
| `PostingFailed`, retries remaining | Abandon where permitted; audit. For `Agents.Administrator`, `administrative retry` also renders as an audited, justification-required `ProposalResolution` command needing no Conversation read access; it is absent when the failure reason is a safety verdict. An Eligible Approver may abandon but never request retry. |
| `PostingFailed`, retries exhausted | Abandon where permitted; audit; `Start a new Agent Call`. `Administrative abandon` also renders for `Agents.Administrator` when the Source Conversation is gone or inaccessible, audited and justification-required without Conversation read access. Administrative retry is absent once the three-attempt bound or `PostingWindowElapsed` is exhausted. The proposal stays `PostingFailed` until a permitted exit completes. |
| `Posted`, `Rejected`, `Abandoned`, `Expired` | Read-only. `Start a new Agent Call` only. |
| **Any state while the Agent is `Disabled` or the tenant is `Suspended`** | Per § Agent lifecycle and tenant suspension, which owns the matrix for both stops. The rail consequence: the blocked actions are **absent**, with the typed reason naming which condition holds, so the editor never renders Approve for the server to refuse. |

`Start a new Agent Call` navigates to the Source Conversation. The only retry control in this rail is `administrative retry` for `Agents.Administrator`. Its public request is lock-bearing `ProposalResolution` and requires neither Conversation read access nor a browser-side posting grant. Once accepted, its `MessageId` lookup, pre-post re-validation, and posting attempt are workflow-only `ConversationPosting` steps that evaluate `LR-CONVERSATIONS-MEMBERSHIP-POSTING` and `LR-SAFETY`; the confirmation's authorization-basis line names the public family and the subsequent workflow family distinctly.

**Operation-family decision (2026-09-12):** `ConversationPosting` is workflow-only and never owns a browser advisory lock. `ProposalResolution` owns the administrative-retry request and its audit evidence; an accepted request then dispatches `ConversationPosting` activities for lookup, re-validation, and posting. Each attempt is an **attempt row** in audit — an outcome event, never a `ProposalVersion`. The bound is **3 attempts within 15 minutes** of the first `PostingFailed` command's `EvaluatedAt` (A-7, Architecture-owned, not tenant-configurable). The deadline is **origin plus window plus `PausedDuration`** (AD-12): the clock pauses while the Agent is `Disabled` or the tenant is `Suspended`, the two causes accrue as the **union** of wall-clock time either was active so an overlap counts once, and the clock resumes only once both have cleared. The confirmation renders attempts used of 3 and the time remaining **computed with `PausedDuration`**, never as `now - firstFailure`, which would expire a countdown while the server's window is still open. Window expiry is a distinct exhaustion reason from attempt exhaustion.

The retry control renders only from the server's `CanAdministrativeRetry` verdict, which combines a remaining attempt in the shared three-attempt budget, adjusted 15-minute deadline, unelapsed `PostingWindowElapsed` bound, non-safety failure reason, active Agent, unsuspended tenant, and usable latest `MessageId` lookup. An administrator may use any remaining shared attempt; there is no separate automatic sub-budget to exhaust first. Automatic and administrative submissions carry the same expected proposal revision, so a concurrent loser consumes no attempt and refreshes the verdict.

**The `MessageId` existence lookup runs before every retry attempt and before every exit from `PostingFailed`, including abandonment by any actor** (FR-18, OQ-26), with three outcomes: **absent** — the exit proceeds; **present** — the proposal moves to `Posted` with **`LateConfirmed`** recorded in Audit Evidence and status, and the requested retry or abandon is **rejected with a typed reason**, because a proposal whose approved version is in the Conversation is never recorded `Abandoned`; **unavailable** — the exit is refused and the proposal stays `PostingFailed`, the lookup repeating on the next read, command, or recovery. The lookup is **skipped for every exit from `Approved`**, where no post was ever attempted (AD-5).

An attempt-exhausted `PostingFailed` therefore has **five** non-time exits: an Eligible Approver abandons it; the Tenant Agent Administrator abandons it where the Source Conversation is gone or inaccessible; the system abandons it because the Source Conversation is gone; the `MessageId` lookup finds the post, giving `Posted` with `LateConfirmed`; or a removal or block is detected, giving `Abandoned` with `RemovedInConversations`. A sixth exit, the system's `PostingWindowElapsed`, closes the lane on time. A new decision, after any exhausted or terminal outcome, requires a new Agent Call.

### Provider catalog rules

Platform-scoped record with a **scoped read and a scoped write** (FR-33, A-10). A tenant reader holding `Agents.Administrator` sees only the Providers and models **enabled for that tenant**, and never sees configured state or `ConfigurationReferenceId`: for that reader a secret-derived readiness reason (`Unconfigured`, `SecretUnavailable`) collapses to one undifferentiated `Blocked`, because the reason vocabulary is otherwise a disclosure channel for secret-configuration state. The full platform catalog, the configured-state and secret-reference columns, per-tenant enablement, and every `ProviderCatalogMutation` require `Agents.PlatformOperator`; those controls and the Editor accordion item itself are **absent**, not disabled, for a reader without it. Per-tenant enablement is a first-class column and filter, and `agent-config-form`'s Provider selection offers only the tenant-enabled subset. Columns include enablement, per-tenant enablement, configured state (Platform Operator only), capability limits, pricing version (pinned, server-assigned, read-only), currency, unit prices, `CapabilityVersion`, readiness triple, freshness. The pricing editor has no default values. One platform-scoped model serves many tenants with many budget currencies, so the catalog validates only ISO 4217 well-formedness. The **tenant budget currency on Cost controls is authoritative**. A per-tenant mismatch is the named `ProviderReadinessReasonCode` **`CurrencyMismatch`**; it renders `{colors.status-important}` on that tenant's readiness view and blocks reservations there, never on the shared catalog row. Before Story 8.4 ships the budget policy read model there is no comparison value, so currency renders as entered with no mismatch state. Pricing edits, enable, and disable are `ProviderCatalogMutation`. That by-Provider/model query is not in Story 5.5's acceptance criteria and the disable action ships in Story 5.3, before it, so until that query lands the confirmation renders the in-scope-Agents list and cross-tenant count as a `{colors.status-subtle}` deferred metric rather than omitting them, and names the platform scope of the action. Cross-tenant impact renders as an aggregate count, or is omitted per disclosure category, never as tenant names. `CapabilityVersion` is the concurrency token; outdated or regressed submissions render `superseded by another decision`. The UI never accepts a secret value. `ConfigurationReferenceId` is a reference only (`EXT-SECRETS-1`), operator-only visible, and masked with autocomplete off when editable. ### Provider data handling

Every Provider/model record carries a **data-handling record** of four fields — the Provider's data-retention term for submitted content, its training-use status (opted out or not), its processing region, and a reference to the contractual terms establishing them, resolving to a retained document the Compliance Inspector can inspect. The record carries its own **`DataHandlingVersion`**, incremented only when one of those four fields changes (FR-4, OQ-29).

**Enablement is not acceptance.** A model whose data-handling record is absent or incomplete **cannot be enabled for any tenant**, so the per-tenant enable control is absent rather than merely warned. Platform enablement permits selection; the **Tenant Agent Administrator must accept a named `DataHandlingVersion`** before the first activation that selects the model. After a version change, fresh acceptance is required unless the tenant remains within a valid declared-tightening grace. The version in force for a tenant is always the last version it accepted.

| Condition | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Never accepted, or a new version has landed | The model is blocked for the tenant **immediately**, typed reason **`DataHandlingAcceptanceLapsed`** (FR-5), until accepted. This is the fail-closed default. |
| A change the Platform Operator **declared a tightening change** | Continues under the version in force **without re-acceptance for 30 days**, notified on the FR-25 surface with the deadline rendered as a countdown. After 30 days without acceptance the model cannot be called for that tenant, with the same typed reason. |
| A change that lengthens retention, moves training use away from opted out, broadens the normalized processing-region set, changes the contractual reference in any way, is undeclared, or is incomparable | **Requires fresh acceptance and may not be declared tightening or receive grace.** A contractual-reference change is ineligible even when presented as stronger. |
| The Tenant Agent Administrator **declines** a version | Blocks the model for the tenant immediately. Declining a declared tightening change is how a disputed declaration is registered. |

The Platform Operator's version-recording control carries a **declare tightening** action, and the declaration must carry the recorded **field-level diff** between the tenant's last accepted record and the current record; only non-lengthening retention, movement toward training-use opt-out, and a narrowing normalized processing-region set are eligible. Any contractual-reference change, undeclared field change, or incomparable value is structurally refused for tightening and requires fresh acceptance. The declaration and its diff are Audit Evidence the Compliance Inspector can inspect.

Acceptance renders through the `DataHandlingAcceptance` confirmation family, whose contents are in § Confirmation contents by family and which carries both an Accept and a **Decline**. It submits the displayed current `DataHandlingVersion` with the current catalog and tenant-enablement revisions; a superseded version is rejected and refreshes the surface. A tightening grace exposes the server-owned exclusive `GraceExpiresAt`; it begins with the first unaccepted version compared cumulatively with the last accepted record and later unaccepted versions never extend it. Audit Evidence records, for each Provider attempt, **both** the Provider's current `DataHandlingVersion` and the version in force — the last version the tenant accepted — which differ only during a tightening grace (FR-24).

Tenant-supplied Provider credentials are out of V1, so the catalog offers no tenant-side credential entry field of any kind.

### Proposal queue rules

The listing predicate is FR-13's, in two parts plus an exclusion: full content for a proposal the viewer was **resolved as an Approver under that proposal's Approver Policy snapshot** and whose Source Conversation the viewer can currently read; **existence and state only** where the viewer was previously resolved but has since lost read access; and a proposal on which the viewer was **never resolved as an Approver is not listed, not counted, and not otherwise disclosed** — a deep link to it renders `not available`, and no filter suggests it. Holding `Agents.Approver` and happening to be a Participant is not resolution. **`needs my action`** means either of two things: a proposal in `Pending`, `Edited`, or `Regenerated` where the viewer is an authorized Approver who did not last edit the selected version; or a proposal in `PostingFailed` with retries remaining. A proposal another Approver has already moved to `Approved`, `Posted`, or a terminal state is neither counted nor listed under it. Filters render as Agents-owned `aria-pressed` toggles per § Grid rules. Filters: state, Agent, Source Conversation, caller, expiry. Empty and filtered-empty are distinct. Expiry renders as an absolute culture-formatted timestamp plus a static relative label refreshed at most once per minute, outside any live region, which is the origin of the general rule in § Live regions. Polled re-renders never steal focus: `ItemKey` is bound, and a re-sort or page shift is deferred while the status node announces `{count} proposals changed. Refresh list.` with an explicit refresh action, the deferred order applying only when the user activates it. The deferral arms **whenever the grid is the user's current reading context**, not only while focus is inside the grid body: a screen-reader user reads in browse mode with a virtual cursor and holds no DOM focus inside the grid, so a focus-only guard never arms for the reader it most protects. In practice it arms while focus is anywhere within the grid region and after any interaction with the grid since the last refresh, and the announcement fires either way. That deferral is also this spine's WCAG 2.2.2 pause mechanism for the queue and is claimed as one in § Other criteria.

Props, events, and variants: Per § Grid rules. At 320 CSS px the surviving columns are state, expiry, and the open action. Default sort: nearest `ExpiresAt` first among `needs my action`, then most recent. Page size 25. Age is computed from `CreatedAt` server time.

### Approver policy rules

Builds authority from the three buildable `ApproverPolicySourceKind` values: `ConversationOwner`, labeled Conversation Facilitator and resolved from `ParticipantRole.Facilitator` (A-3); `PredefinedParty`; and `TenantRole`. **`Caller` is retired** and is not offered. Retirement is split across two stories: **5.4** rejects the value server-side before mutation and keeps it absent from new configuration choices, and **5.10** removes it from this authoring surface, maps the legacy `ConversationOwner` wire value to the rendered label Conversation Facilitator, and proves stale UI state cannot bypass the rejection. FR-7 and OQ-14 put it on the FR-23 deprecate-and-reject register, because the Eligible Approver predicate excludes the caller of every proposal the source could apply to, and the server rejects it with a typed rejection wherever it is presented. A legacy policy still carrying it renders the row read-only in `{colors.status-severe}` with the typed retirement reason, and publication is blocked while it is present. Each row declares a disclosure category; the default is operator-only; a source revealing restricted-role membership cannot be user-visible. Validation rejects a source that cannot satisfy Conversation access at call time, and rejects a configuration that could never yield an Eligible Approver once the caller is excluded — precisely, a policy that names no Conversation-dependent source (Conversation Facilitator or a tenant role) **and** names fewer than two predefined Parties. A Facilitator-only policy is valid, because the Facilitator is eligible whenever the Facilitator is not the caller. The error copy states which of the two halves failed. Ambiguous or unavailable sources fail closed and block Confirmation Response Mode activation. Row order is edited with buttons or a menu, never drag-only, and because those controls are this spine's **only** substitute for a drag operation (2.5.7) they carry a name, a focus and an announcement contract rather than only an existence one. Row order is load-bearing — it is the order in which Approver sources are resolved — so a keyboard user who can operate the control and cannot tell what it did has been given the affordance and not the operation. **Name:** each reorder button's accessible name carries the row identity, `Move {kind}: {basis} up` and `Move {kind}: {basis} down`, so a six-source policy renders twelve distinctly named buttons rather than twelve called `Move up` or `Move down`; the row's own accessible name (below) is not a substitute, because the button is what the user lands on. **Focus:** focus **follows the moved row's activated button to its new position**, never staying put while the row the user is moving jumps away underneath them; a button that cannot move further — up on the first row, down on the last — is `DisabledFocusable` with its reason per § Interaction Primitives rather than absent, so the tab order does not change shape mid-reorder. **Announcement:** the polite node announces `{source} moved to position {n} of {total}` once per move, per § Live regions. **The builder has an add control and a per-row remove control**, and each takes the same three contracts. A source list with no way to add or remove a row is not a builder, and the validation predicate above — which refuses a policy naming "fewer than two predefined Parties" — is unstatable against a surface that cannot name one. **Add source** is a single `FluentButton` below the last row; it appends a row whose kind is chosen in the row itself rather than in a dialog, so the confirmation rule that no dialog opens from a selection event is not reached. **Remove** is a per-row `FluentButton` whose accessible name carries the row identity on the same terms as the reorder buttons — `Remove {kind}: {basis}` — never a bare `Remove` repeated down the list. Neither writes: both edit the draft policy, and only **Publish** opens `PolicyPublication`. Focus after **Add source** moves to the new row's kind control; focus after a removal returns to the row that takes the removed row's position, or to **Add source** when the removed row was the last — the one focus case the deterministic focus ladder in § High-risk pending commands does not cover, because nothing is pending. The polite node announces `{source} removed. {total} sources remain.` once per removal, on the same terms as the reorder announcement.

**How each row's basis is chosen.** The kind control is a `FluentSelect` over the three buildable `ApproverPolicySourceKind` values; what it offers beside the kind is per kind, and none of the three accepts a free-text identifier:

| Kind | Basis control | Source |

### Proposal editor rules

Exists only after successful generation. Authorized Approvers edit, regenerate, approve a selected version, reject, or abandon. Editing creates a new preserved version with editor Party and timestamp; an edited version is never presented as generated. **Edit is `DisabledFocusable` whenever no Eligible Approver would remain after excluding the caller and editor** (FR-7); **Regenerate is `DisabledFocusable` before Provider work whenever no Eligible Approver would remain after also excluding the requesting Party**. Both reasons state that a second Approver is required. Without these guards, a lone Approver could create a version they cannot approve and strand the proposal until expiry. Approval is `DisabledFocusable` while the editor is dirty. Approval requires the viewer to be an **Eligible Approver** for the selected version, which is one predicate with six conjuncts (PRD §3, FR-7): resolved by this proposal's Approver Policy snapshot; a current Participant of the Source Conversation; holding current read access to it; **not the caller of the Agent Call**; not the Party that last edited the version; and not the Party recorded as requesting regeneration of that version. Approval is `DisabledFocusable` when any conjunct fails, and the server applies the same predicate. Story 7.4 supplies a per-version `CanCurrentUserApproveSelectedVersion` flag returning the full predicate with one typed reason per failing conjunct; the UI never compares raw Party identifiers or clocks. Regenerate is also `DisabledFocusable` at the regeneration ceiling or while an attempt is in flight, and its copy states that regeneration is chargeable under cost caps and rate limits. On regeneration failure the editor keeps the proposal non-terminal with prior versions intact, records a linked failure record, announces the failure in the persistent status region, and keeps approve, reject, and abandon available. Approval re-runs the current Content Safety Policy on the exact selected version. Which actions are available in which state is in § Proposal editor action rail. An Approver who lost Conversation access sees existence and state only.

Props, events, and variants: Exit is an explicit button with unsaved-changes confirmation. No client-side length limit. Regenerate while any resolution is pending is `DisabledFocusable`; regeneration is chargeable, so while one is in flight Regenerate is also `DisabledFocusable` with a local pending badge, which guards against a double Enter. **Regenerate is operation family `ProposalRegeneration` and Save is `ProposalEdit`, and each opens `high-impact-confirmation`** with its contents from § Confirmation contents by family; neither is lock-bearing under AD-12, so neither takes a browser advisory lock and neither carries a justification. Resolution actions open `high-impact-confirmation` (`ProposalResolution`).

### Operational status rules

Groups readiness and runtime outcomes by recovery: configure Provider, fix policy, wait for approval, retry posting, inspect audit, start a new call. Per-item state uses `FluentBadge`; `FluentMessageBar` is page-level notice only. ### Launch readiness rules

Reads one `launch-readiness` projection checkpoint (Story 8.7). ### Audit evidence rules

Support-safe evidence: caller, Agent, Source Conversation, Provider/model, response mode, computed Safe Context Budget, measured context size, `CapabilityVersion`, context mode, safety decisions at each of the four points, versions, editing Party per edited version, approving Party and policy basis per disclosure category, projection references, timestamps, posting outcome, final Conversation Message. Every entry also carries the **tenant** it was scoped to, the **FR-33 role basis** the operation was permitted on, and the **typed terminal outcome** — accepted, rejected on concurrency, or denied on authorization — because the acceptance stages a command passed describe progress, not outcome. Conversation-derived evidence is inspectable at **two levels** (FR-24, OQ-21): posted provenance by a current Participant with read access; and unposted versions, rejected content, and context metadata by a Party resolved as an Eligible Approver for that proposal, or under a **compliance inspection** by `Agents.AuditOperator`. A compliance inspection is scoped to a named Conversation or case identifier and is itself recorded as Audit Evidence (Story 8.8). Its four constraints are **mechanics the surface enforces**, not prose (OQ-30, OQ-21, AD-22):

1. **Computed subject set.** The system computes and displays the subject set — every caller, editor, Approver, decision actor and Facilitator in scope, plus the specific Tenant Agent Administrator principal(s) whose `ConfigurationVersion` was in force — and refuses a second party inside it, or the Inspector themselves, with a typed reason that explains why. Two Compliance Inspectors cannot approve each other within a rolling **30 days**.
2. **Post-hoc review is narrow.** It is offered **only** at single-proposal or single-Conversation scope, within the A-19 window of **7 days**, with the countdown rendered. Outside that scope only prior approval is offered. Where the case concerns the Tenant Agent Administrator, the approver is the Platform Operator or a second Compliance Inspector.
3. **Wider than one Conversation needs the Platform Operator**, approved **before the first content read**. A *case* identifier is by definition wider than one Conversation, so a case-scoped inspection can never proceed on a post-hoc review — least of all the post-hoc review of the administrator under examination. More than **5 distinct Conversations** post hoc in a rolling 30 days is one wide inspection, and the sixth requires prior Platform Operator approval (A-23).
4. **Unreviewed inspections are a persistent visible signal.** An `UnreviewedInspection` governance signal, plus the inspection rate, renders on the `audit-evidence` surfaces the Tenant Agent Administrator and the Platform Operator read, and persists until a review is recorded; an Inspector holding an unreviewed inspection cannot open another post-hoc one. No further automatic escalation is defined for V1.

Scope, computed second party and review mode are rendered in the inspection's justification-entry confirmation, under family `AuditInspection`. Evidence whose Source Conversation is deleted or inaccessible remains inspectable under compliance inspection and is never rendered `not available` to that path: FR-24 states that retained evidence is never made uninspectable by the loss of its Conversation. Inspection fails closed outside these two paths. Named authority is the Conversation Facilitator where that source applied. A second evidence class, **configuration and governance change evidence**, satisfies FR-24 and FR-32 and is inspectable both at `/agents/audit` and in the History item of each write surface: actor, operation family, resource identity, old-to-new values where safe to expose (including prior and new cap values), published version, concurrency token, projection id and version, justification, and the acceptance stages the command passed. An override additionally shows its scope, ceiling, and expiry.

**The change-and-inspection evidence list** is the fourth `FluentDataGrid` (§ Grid rules) and the only enumerable list on this route; the Conversation-derived classes are id entry only and declare no columns, filters or sort. Its `ViewKey` is **`agents.audit-evidence`**, with its own localized `DetailPanelAriaLabel` and `EntityPlural` in the localization inventory, because `FcExpandInRowDetail` marks `ViewKey`, `HasExpanded`, `ChildContent` and `DetailPanelAriaLabel` `[EditorRequired]` and `FcFilterEmptyState` does the same for `ViewKey`, `TotalCount` and `EntityPlural`; every one of those takes a value and a view-key mismatch fails closed. Columns, in order: `ObservedAt`, actor with the FR-33 role basis it was permitted on, operation family, resource class and identity, tenant, typed terminal outcome, and — on a compliance-inspection row — inspection mode (advance or post-hoc), scope kind (proposal, Conversation, or case), and review state carrying the **`UnreviewedInspection`** flag. `ItemKey` is the evidence record id. Default sort: unreviewed inspection rows first, then most recent `ObservedAt`. Page size 25, the queue's. At 320 CSS px the surviving columns are `ObservedAt`, operation family, and review state, with the remainder in `FcExpandInRowDetail`; justification text, old-to-new values and projection references live in that detail panel and never in a cell. The list is **tenant-scoped and carries no Conversation content**, which is the only reason it can be enumerated at all. The persistent `UnreviewedInspection` signal and the running **inspection rate** render **outside the grid and outside the accordion**, beside the id-entry form (`DESIGN.md § Layout & Spacing`), because a signal that must stay visible cannot sit behind an accordion interaction or be filtered away, and because AD-22 requires the rate to be visible to the Tenant Agent Administrator and the Platform Operator.

**Audit Evidence is append-only, and this surface says so.** A record is never altered after it is written: **no surface in this product offers an edit, amend, redact or delete affordance on an evidence record**, and a correction is a new record referencing the earlier one rather than a mutation of it, so the two records and their order are both evidence. The **only** paths by which retained content leaves the store are the governed `DeletionRequest` of § Audit governance rules — with its A-12 two-person staging, its computed scope and its `DeletionDeferredByHold` signal — and the **365-day retention clock**, which runs from the interaction's terminal state; in both cases sensitive content is cryptographically erased or redacted, projections are purged, and immutable EventStore history retains only a safe tombstone (OQ-8), never a silently rewritten record. A record inside a **live legal hold is exempt from both**: the hold takes precedence over every deletion signal including retention expiry, which is what `DeletionDeferredByHold` implies and this states, and the hold's own release is itself two-party staged, so the protection cannot be lifted by one party and the record deleted in a second dialog. This is asserted rather than assumed because reconstruction — the reason this surface exists — presumes the record is trustworthy, and `LR-UI-CONFORMANCE` cannot observe an affordance the spine never forbade.

**`EntryMissing` is never rendered on this surface.** Evidence for a terminal interaction resolves its Provider and model from the interaction's immutable snapshot plus authorized Audit Evidence, and renders neither `EntryMissing` nor `not available` because the catalog entry is gone (FR-24). The scope of the carve-out is stated once in § Provider and model.

Props, events, and variants: Per § Grid rules for the list; per-item state uses `FluentBadge`.

### Audit governance rules

Callable operations (FR-30): apply and release legal hold, request export, request deletion, each through `high-impact-confirmation` (`LegalHold`, `ExportRequest`, `DeletionRequest`). A justification typed into the confirmation before Confirm becomes available is required on **all ten AD-12 lock-bearing families, with no exempt family** (AD-22) — `ProposalResolution`, `PolicyPublication`, `TenantBudgetUpdate`, `DataHandlingAcceptance`, `LegalHold` (also `LegalHoldRelease`), `ExportRequest` (also `ExportDownload`), `DeletionRequest`, `ProviderCatalogMutation` (also `TenantProviderEnablement`), `AgentSetupMutation` (also `TenantKillSwitch` and the Conversation block and clear commands), and `AgentActivation` — not over FR-30's narrower five. That subset is closed at ten, `DataHandlingAcceptance` being AD-22's additive tenth member, so the two matrix-v3 proposal families the UI also issues — `ProposalEdit` and `ProposalRegeneration` — are **outside** it: they render a confirmation and audit evidence and carry no justification, and they neither enlarge the count nor create an exempt lock-bearing family. The justification is part of the audit record, and an empty or whitespace value is rejected client-side and server-side. Confirm is `DisabledFocusable` while the justification is empty or whitespace, with an `aria-describedby` reason (`Explain why this operation is required before confirming`); it is never `Disabled`, which inside a focus-trapped dialog would leave the textarea and Cancel as the only reachable controls and nothing to explain that Confirm exists or what unlocks it. The same rule governs every gated Confirm. Confirmation contents are in § Confirmation contents by family. **Hold release, export and deletion all carry the same request / approve / execute staging**, because FR-30 requires it of all three: "Export requires prior second-party approval and hold release a separate approver (FR-33)." Giving the staging to deletion alone let one Compliance Inspector release the hold protecting the evidence and then export it, in two dialogs.

| Operation | Requester | Approver, required before execution |
|---|---|---|
| `DeletionRequest` | `Agents.PlatformOperator` | A distinct `Agents.AuditOperator` holder (A-12, AD-22) |
| `LegalHoldRelease` | `Agents.AuditOperator` | A **second** Compliance Inspector, or the Platform Operator acting solely as the recorded second-party approver (AD-30) |
| `ExportRequest` | `Agents.AuditOperator` | A second party **outside the computed subject set** (AD-22), **never post hoc** |

**Where the second party approves.** The approval is a **Pending approvals** item on `audit-governance-panel`, on the same route and under the same policy constants as the requests it approves. It carries the three contracts a second-party act needs. **Discovery** is the item itself — its accordion title renders the count of requests awaiting *this viewer's* approval as the whole string `{count} requests are waiting for your approval`, and the item is expanded on first paint whenever that count is non-zero, so an approver who opens the route for any reason sees it without looking for it. **In-product only**, on the same terms as `proposal-notification`: V1 sends no mail and no external notification, and the count is never rendered in the shell nav rail, per the `proposal-notification` row. **The control** is one **Approve** `FluentButton` per pending row, opening `high-impact-confirmation` under the **same family as the request it approves** — `DeletionRequest`, `LegalHoldRelease` or `ExportRequest` — so no family is minted, the AD-12 lock-bearing set stays closed at ten, and the register roster at `OperationGateMatrixVersion = 3` stays complete; each family's approve contents are the rows in § Confirmation contents by family. The approval carries its own required justification and its own audit record, distinct from the request's, which is what lets `audit-governance-panel` show **both** justifications on every release and export row. A **Decline** control is not offered in V1: a request the second party does not approve simply stays unapproved and `DisabledFocusable`, and the requester withdraws it. **Rows disclose** the requester, the scope and counts, the time requested, and — where the viewer is inside the computed subject set or is the requester — the typed refusal reason instead of the control, so the refusal is legible rather than an absent button. `Agents.AuditOperator` sees the `LegalHoldRelease` and `ExportRequest` rows it may approve; the `DeletionRequest` approval row is `Agents.AuditOperator`'s too, per A-12, and the Platform Operator's `LegalHoldRelease` second-party row per AD-30. No pending-approval list contract exists on the Stories 8.1 to 8.3 progress projections today, so it is a § Contracts that must grow row owned by 8.1 to 8.3, and until it lands the item renders the `{colors.status-subtle}` deferred treatment rather than an empty list that would read as "nothing awaits you".

In all three the requester's own approval is refused with a typed reason, the approver identity and the never-post-hoc rule are rendered in the confirmation, and `audit-governance-panel` shows requester, approver and **both** justifications on every release and export row. Each action is `DisabledFocusable` while its approval is absent, and deletion additionally while a matching legal hold is active — rendered as the `DeletionDeferredByHold` signal (FR-30) — or while `EXT-PROTECTION-1`/`EXT-SECRETS-1` is not `Available`, whose typed reason is **`PayloadProtectionUnavailable`** rather than a generic unavailability. Releasing a hold and requesting deletion are never the same gesture, and a hold release names the records it exposes. Export rows show manifest state, artifact expiry, and download availability; the panel never renders artifact contents or keys, and the export envelope key is delivered to the requesting Inspector principal outside Agents. The deletion confirmation states that **a posted Conversation Message carrying erased content remains readable in Conversations** after Agents-side erasure completes (AD-22), so completion is never rendered as though the content were gone everywhere. Shows the 365-day rule, hold state, export manifest state, deletion progress including DEK destruction receipt, projection purge, workflow-state purge, and tombstone confirmation. Success only after authoritative evidence; partial failure stays restrictive and actionable.

## State Patterns

Canonical states are used consistently. PascalCase names are contract tokens; lowercase names are UX states that have no contract token yet.

Contract tokens this spine needs that do not exist yet are listed in § Known gaps.

```text
Submitted -> AuthoritativePending -> ProjectionConfirmed
Approved -> PostingPending -> Posted
Approved -> PostingPending -> PostingFailed -> (retry posting) -> PostingPending
generation failed -> separate failure record; no proposal
```

`AuthoritativePending` exists only after the UI receives and renders a server/EventStore-accepted pending identity plus projection/version reference; it is a durable accepted outcome and is never re-submitted. `ProjectionConfirmed` exists only after the authoritative projection/version is received and rendered. Every read exposes the projection id, version, and freshness it was served at. Optimistic client state, a timeout, a SignalR nudge, or an unrelated projection change proves neither stage.

| Item | Rule |

| Standard | The Agents truth-flow helper (`AgentCommandAcceptance`, `ProviderCatalogCommandAcceptance` polling); `FcPendingCommandSummary` is out for V1. |

Every read renders its projection id, version and freshness whatever its state. |

### Agent lifecycle and tenant suspension

Two **orthogonal** axes, never collapsed into one badge. Collapsing them loses exactly the distinction FR-3 added them for: an operator must be able to tell a suspended tenant from a disabled Agent.

| Axis | Values | Where it renders |
|---|---|---|
| Agent lifecycle (`AgentLifecycleStatus`) | `Draft` (provisioned, not yet valid for activation), `Active`, `Disabled`. `Unknown` is the FR-23 sentinel and is never displayed. | `agent-readiness-badge` on Agents overview and `hexa` configuration. |
| Tenant status (FR-25) | `Suspended` while the per-tenant kill switch is pulled, and nothing else. **`Suspended` is not a lifecycle value** (FR-3, OQ-27). | A separate tenant chip on Operational status and Launch readiness. |

`Draft` is the state provisioning leaves behind, so it is the initial state `agent-config-form` renders for, not an empty surface. **The Platform Operator provisions `hexa` once per tenant at tenant enablement, create-only and idempotent** (OQ-28); the Tenant Agent Administrator then configures and activates it. No tenant role can create a second Agent, delete `hexa`, or change its Party identity in V1, so **Create Agent and Delete Agent controls are absent, not disabled, for every tenant role**, and the Party identity panel is inspect-only with no link or replace control at all — the shipped link and replace commands are deprecate-and-reject (OQ-25).

Activation is refused with a typed rejection while the tenant carries a `TriggerReviewOverdue` condition (FR-28). That is a launch-governance condition blocking a configuration action, so the Activate control on `hexa` configuration renders `DisabledFocusable` with a reason naming the condition and linking to Launch readiness, where it is owned.

While the Agent is `Disabled` **or** its tenant is `Suspended`, the two conditions degrade proposal actions identically, per proposal state:

| Proposal state | Permitted | Blocked, with a typed reason |
|---|---|---|
| `Pending`, `Edited`, `Regenerated` | Reject, abandon | Approve, edit, regenerate |
| `Approved` | Reject is not offered; abandon by an Eligible Approver or the Tenant Agent Administrator | Posting does not start. The proposal **waits in `Approved`**, consuming no retry attempt, and is re-validated when the condition lifts. |
| `PostingPending` | Nothing | **Uninterruptible.** The attempt completes or fails on its own terms; no removal, block, suspension, or abandonment acts on a post in flight, and each is applied on exit if the attempt did not post. |
| `PostingFailed` | Abandon | Automatic and administrative retry are suspended and the retry-window clock is **paused**. |

`ExpiresAt` keeps running under both conditions and the countdown is never frozen on screen. The two differ in one place only, and only in accounting: expiry while the Agent is `Disabled` is an ordinary `Expired` that stays in the SM-3 and SM-C5 denominators, because the disable is the tenant's own decision, while expiry while the tenant is `Suspended` is recorded `ExpiredWhileSuspended` and leaves them, so the switch cannot manufacture its own launch-health miss (FR-3, FR-28, OQ-27).

An `Approved` proposal waiting under either condition renders a **waiting, not posting** treatment that is visibly distinct from `PostingPending`; a paused retry clock renders as paused rather than counting down.

### Conversation Agent State

The FR-2 membership step is a state machine (OQ-25), and it is what Operational status renders per Conversation. Five membership states and one versioned current-mirror record:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `NeverJoined` | No accepted Agent Call has joined `hexa` to this Conversation yet. |
| `Joined` | `hexa` is a member. Recorded at a membership step, never optimistically. |
| `ExternallyRemoved` | Removal originating in Conversations, detected lazily at the next membership step. Cleared by the Tenant Agent Administrator or any current Conversation Facilitator (A-9). |
| `Blocked` | The Agents-owned block is in force, versioned by `BlockVersion`, which is the concurrency token for every set and clear. Cleared only by the setting authority evaluated at clear time, or by the Tenant Agent Administrator (A-9). |
| `ReadmitPending` | Recorded when a block is cleared. The re-admission add is its own mirror entry and the state records `Joined` only **at the next membership step**, so a clear never renders as re-admitted immediately. |

| Derived status | Treatment |
|---|---|
| `MirrorPending` | The current `Remove` or `Readmit` mirror has not been confirmed. Informative, `{colors.status-informative}`; transient failures are retried under a timeout. Pending remove leaves `Blocked`; pending readmit leaves `ReadmitPending`. |
| `MirrorRefused` | A typed permanent refusal ended retry for the current attempt. **Actionable**, `{colors.status-severe}`: refused remove leaves `Blocked` and offers re-set or authorized clear; refused readmit leaves `ReadmitPending` and offers authorized clear again or re-set. Either successor replaces Refused with Pending. SM-C4 counts only the current Refused outcome. |

The two labels above are mutually exclusive derivations of `CurrentMirror(BlockVersion, Direction, Outcome, AttemptId)`, where direction is `Remove` or `Readmit` and outcome is `Pending`, `Confirmed`, or `Refused`; a confirmed current record renders neither label. A newer set or clear supersedes every lower-version mirror, and late outcomes change no current UI state. Re-set or an authorized clear creates a new current attempt and replaces Refused with Pending; no standalone “clear mirror error” action exists.

Set and clear authority is owned by § Operational status rules.

### Proposal lifecycle

Aligned to `ProposedAgentReplyState`. The PRD names the failed-post state `PostFailed`; the contract name `PostingFailed` is used here. The initial generated version is the first `Pending` version, not a separate state. FR-18 names seven states and the contract enum carries ten: `Edited`, `Regenerated`, and `PostingPending` are contract values the PRD folds into their neighbors, with `Unknown` as the FR-23 sentinel that is never a recorded state. **The enum governs**: both spines render all ten with a color role each, because those three are separately renderable and separately audited. Aligning the PRD text to the contract is a documentation reconciliation and not a gate, and is a deferred item with Product.

| State | Terminal | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `Pending` | No | Queue-visible; notification-eligible; `{colors.status-informative}`. |
| `Edited` | No | New preserved version with editor Party and timestamp; prior versions visible. |
| `Regenerated` | No | New generated version under fresh gates; counts toward the ceiling; prior versions visible. |
| `Approved` | No | Progress; a version is selected; posting not implied. Expiry no longer applies: approval freezes it. Non-terminal for posting only: once projection-confirmed, edit, regenerate, and approve are absent and `ApprovedVersionId` is pinned read-only, so no other version can be approved or posted and no edit can land while posting is pending. |
| `PostingPending` | No | Progress; not yet a Conversation Message. Expiry no longer applies. |
| `Posted` | Yes | `{colors.status-success}`; link evidence to the posted message. |
| `PostingFailed` | No | `{colors.status-danger}`; typed reason; bounded audited `retry posting` reusing the deterministic `MessageId`, at most 3 attempts within 15 minutes of the first failure (A-7, Architecture-owned). Expiry no longer applies. An exhausted proposal **remains `PostingFailed`** and always has an exit, so it always reaches the retention clock; the exit inventory is owned once by § Proposal editor action rail. |
| `Rejected` | Yes | Versions and evidence preserved. |
| `Abandoned` | Yes | Versions preserved. The system reason is one of **four** typed values, each with a localized label: `NoEligibleApprover`, `SourceConversationUnavailable`, `RemovedInConversations` (Story 6.8, AC-1), and `PostingWindowElapsed` (FR-18), which fires when a proposal has been in `Approved` or `PostingFailed` for longer than its original expiry duration in total, excluding time paused under FR-3 and FR-28, and counts as decided for SM-3. A human abandonment carries no system reason. **The kill switch is never an abandonment reason**: Story 7.7, AC-4 states that the switch "never system-abandons or deletes the proposal or evidence", and a suspended tenant instead pauses the lane per § Agent lifecycle and tenant suspension. A system-abandoned proposal is excluded from the SM-3 denominator and counted separately from Approver-abandoned on Operational status (FR-25, SM-C4). |
| `Expired` | Yes | Rendered when `ExpiresAt` has passed **while `Pending`, `Edited`, or `Regenerated`**, on every read and every command, regardless of timer delivery; sole action is a new Agent Call. Approval freezes expiry, so this is never rendered over `Approved`, `PostingPending`, or `PostingFailed` (FR-18, OQ-3). The comparison uses the server time carried on the read, never the browser clock, so a skewed client neither expires a live proposal nor leaves Approve enabled past `ExpiresAt`. |

**Nearing expiry** is a rendering flag, not a state: it begins at 10% of the configured expiry window or 1 hour before `ExpiresAt`, whichever is smaller, floored at 15 minutes so a short window still leaves an actionable warning. It is computed from `ExpiresAt`, the configured window, and the server read time, adds a `{colors.status-warning}` chip to `proposal-state-badge`, and announces once per proposal per § Accessibility Floor.

## Interaction Primitives

- FrontComposer shell shortcuts and palette remain available. Agents registers no palette commands and no domain shortcuts in V1.
- Grids follow § Grid rules.
- High-risk actions follow § High-risk pending commands: `high-impact-confirmation`, then `pending-command-indicator`, then the deterministic focus rule.
- Wherever this spine says a control is `aria-disabled`, its **reason is programmatically associated with the control**, not merely adjacent to it: the control carries `aria-describedby` pointing at the localized whole string that states the reason, and that same string is the visible adjacent text. Visual adjacency is not an accessible description. This binds every blocked control in this spine: every confirmation family, every rail action, **Call hexa**, export, deletion, and the Provider and policy editors. The control-side binding is `FluentButton.DisabledFocusable="true"` on a Fluent control, or the `aria-disabled` attribute with the control left focusable and its activation suppressed on a hand-authored one; `Disabled` and `Loading` are never used for a blocked-but-explicable state, because both remove the control from the tab order and take the reason with it. The binding is a **plain `aria-describedby="@id"` attribute on the Fluent control**, captured and splatted through `FluentComponentBase`'s unmatched-attribute parameter — no Fluent component in the relevant set (`FluentButton`, `FluentCompoundButton`, `FluentBadge`, `FluentRadioGroup`, `FluentMessageBar`) exposes a typed `AriaDescribedBy`, and none is needed. Where a control genuinely does not splat, the description goes on a wrapper referenced from the control. `LR-UI-CONFORMANCE` asserts that every `aria-disabled` control exposes its reason through `aria-describedby`, and the AT matrix verifies it.
- Editing a proposal is explicit; regeneration is a distinct action; approval applies to the selected version only.
- `Esc` closes transient UI without committing and never discards typed content. On a route this covers the proposal editor. **Inside a dialog it never opens a second dialog**: Fluent v5 is explicit that a `FluentDialog` must not be opened from a `FluentDialog`, and nesting one would break the outer trap's inertness and leave focus return undefined between two traps, which would reach every justification-gated confirmation. Text inside a dialog is instead protected by an **inline confirm strip rendered in the same `FluentDialogBody`**, with focus moved to it and a second `Esc` cancelling the strip; the required **Call hexa** prompt follows the same pattern, or keeps the draft for the session. Focus returns to the trigger, or follows the deterministic focus rule when the trigger is gone.
- Hover-revealed row actions also render on row focus-within and are dismissible; tooltips are hoverable and `Esc`-dismissible. No required action or reason is hover-only.
- Accordion use and primary regions per surface are in `DESIGN.md § Layout & Spacing`.
- Policy, cap, mode, expiry, and ceiling changes state their future-only effect in the confirmation.

## Accessibility Floor

WCAG 2.2 AA is the binding behavioral floor across every interactive V1 route and high-impact state. Contrast and icon pairing live in `DESIGN.md`.

### Live regions

**Agents owns one node of each politeness per route, and every event is announced by exactly one node.** Mandatory FrontComposer components render live regions of their own; the rule governs which node announces an event, not how many nodes the DOM holds. The carrier is a single Agents-owned `AgentsPageStatusRegion` component placed once per page body, holding both nodes; no badge, panel, or row component carries `role="status"` of its own.

**`AgentsPageStatusRegion` is infrastructure and is deliberately outside the 22-component contract.** `DESIGN.md § Components` names and orders 22 components in three places and requires any change to land in all three; this carrier is in none of them, not by omission but because **it has no visual specification to bind**: it renders no visible text, no badge, no border and no spacing, it takes no token, and it occupies no layout slot in `DESIGN.md § Layout & Spacing`. It is therefore specified here in full, and the 22-component count stays 22.

What the **host** announces for contributed content is not Agents' to decide and is the announcement-division clause in § Conversation Integration Seam, which is an open `EXT-CONV-UI-1` item rather than a closed rule.
- **Rendered time is never live.** Every countdown and relative-time label this spine renders takes the queue's discipline, stated here once so a countdown added later inherits it: it refreshes **at most once per minute** and lives **outside every live region**. That reaches five, not one — the proposal queue's expiry label (§ Proposal queue rules); the 30-day declared-tightening grace deadline (§ Provider data handling); the `PausedDuration`-adjusted retry-window remaining time rendered on proposal detail (§ Other criteria); the A-19 7-day post-hoc inspection window (§ Audit evidence rules); and the 7-day trigger-review decision validity (§ Launch readiness rules) — and four of the five sit on surfaces that already own a polite node, where a per-second countdown inside or beside `role="status"` is the canonical screen-reader flood. Time-sensitive change is announced **only** through the named once-per-item warnings in the politeness table — the nearing-expiry announcement, the nearing-retry-window warning, and `retry window expired` — and through nothing else; a rendered duration is read on demand, never spoken on a tick.

Every **display-only outcome** listed in § Proposal lifecycle — `safety blocked at approval`, `stale proposal`, `regeneration ceiling reached`, `duplicate submission (idempotent)`, `expired at approval`, `superseded by another decision`, `authority unresolved` — has its own row in the table below and is announced by the node its severity selects. `expired at approval` and `safety blocked at approval` are entries distinct from the Agent-call states of the same name, which describe a call rather than a rejected approval command. Each display-only outcome is announced because each is carried visually by an adjacent badge whose text changes, and no badge may be a live region of its own. Any event this spine names and this table omits is a defect in the table, not a deliberate silence.

The NFR-14 `LiveRegionAnnouncedTick` observes the localized mutation in the Agents node after render commit, so a duplicate announcement corrupts the measurement as well as the experience.

| Politeness | Events |
|---|---|
| `role="alert"` | denied; generation failed; posting failed; expired (proposal detail only, or on the queue when the expiring row is the focused one — see § Announcement volume for the queue aggregate); safety blocked; capacity rejected; rate limited; **tenant suspended**; **no eligible approver**; **conversation blocked**; **budget blocked**; **unknown outcome**; **safety blocked at approval**; **expired at approval**; **authority unresolved**, which fires when Tenants or Conversations drops mid-session and would otherwise silently disable every resolution control on the page; **retry window expired**; the domain consequence of connection loss. |
| `role="status"` | submitted; authoritative pending; proposal created; approved; posting pending; posted; regenerated (`Version {n} generated` with a go-to action); draft saved; draft discarded; applied version changed, on **View version** activation only and never on a draft selection move (`Showing version {n}`, per the `version-history` row); **approver-policy row reorder** (`{source} moved to position {n} of {total}`); response mode unchanged after cancel; context blocked; expiry warning (once per proposal); superseded by another decision; status refreshed; capacity queued; catching up; **awaiting projection** (with the refresh action named); **pending in another session**; **connection restored**; list changed after a deferred re-sort; **stale proposal**; **regeneration ceiling reached**; **duplicate submission (idempotent)**; the **audit id-entry form's `not available` outcome**; the **nearing-retry-window warning**; and — as one generic rule rather than a row per family — **every confirmation family's `ProjectionConfirmed` result**, as a localized whole string naming the operation and the resource. #### Announcement volume

Announcement volume must not grow with list length. The queue polls 25 rows sorted nearest-expiry-first, so a burst at the top of the hour must not produce 25 announcements: per-proposal expiry alerts are scoped to proposal detail, and on the queue an aggregate is announced at most once per poll cycle in the polite node, never as `role="alert"` unless the row that expired is the focused one.

### Route heading and focus

- Every route renders a non-blank localized heading with `HeadingTabIndex=-1` and a `PageTitle`, focused through `FocusHeadingAsync()`. `FcAggregateListPage` takes those as parameters. `FcAggregateDetailPage` does not: it has no heading parameters and its non-ready slots replace the body entirely. The routes actually bound to `FcAggregateDetailPage` are **four**: `hexa` configuration, Approver policy, Proposal detail/editor, and the Audit evidence detail. On those four the domain renders `FcPageHeader` as the first child of each of the **five real state slots** — `LoadingContent`, `UnauthorizedContent`, `NotFoundContent`, `UnavailableContent`, and `ReadyContent` — each carrying `HeadingTabIndex="-1"`, because `FocusHeadingAsync()` throws rather than no-ops when the heading is blank or the tab index is null, which would fail at the focus call in production on the error path. `StaleBanner` and `DegradedBanner` are **not slots**: they render above the ready body, which already carries the ready header, so they carry no heading and no `PageTitle`. The `not available` slot's heading is the route name, not `Agents.Surface.NotAvailable.Title`, which is the body copy. "Heading present and focused in the `not available` state" is part of the `LR-UI-CONFORMANCE` lane.
- `FcAggregateDetailPage` renders a back link by default. Every detail route supplies a localized `BackLinkLabel` or sets `ShowBackLink="false"`; an unset label yields an anchor with no accessible name, and HFC1050 does not fire on framework components to catch it.
- Focus moves to the heading on navigation, when proposal detail opens from the queue, after a forced refresh (with `Status refreshed. Approval is still pending.` announced), and **whenever `FcAggregateDetailState` changes the rendered slot** — the ordinary cold load of every Constrained detail route is such a swap, as are Ready to `Unavailable` when a dependency drops and Ready to `NotFound` when access is revoked mid-session. Each slot is a separate component instance with its own `h1`, so without this rule focus falls to `document.body` silently. On the transition focus moves to the newly rendered slot's heading and the polite node announces the new state, loading excepted.
- `BackHref` is required and non-empty on every detail route — the parent list — because it defaults to `string.Empty`, which renders a named link to the current URL, outside the state slots, and therefore self-referential even on `not available`.
- Every route sets `FcPageHeader.HeadingId` and the shell's `ContentLabelledBy` to it, so the single `main` landmark is named by the route heading rather than shipping unnamed.
- The routes bound to no `Fc*` page component — Agents overview, Operational status, Launch readiness, the Audit evidence list, and the four Constrained form surfaces (Conversation context policy, Content safety, Cost controls, Audit governance), which are forms rather than aggregate details and so take neither `Fc*` page component — are hand-authored and carry the domain `FcPageHeader` and the same state-slot pattern directly, including the heading, `PageTitle`, focus target, and the loading, error, `not available`, and `Degraded` treatments.
- Heading levels inside a route follow `DESIGN.md § Layout & Spacing`.
- After regeneration, focus stays on Regenerate and the status region announces the new version.

### Forms

- Every control has a visible label. Errors are associated through `aria-describedby` and `aria-invalid`; an error summary receives focus on submit and links to the first error.
- Error text is one localized whole string naming the field, the fault, and the fix (WCAG 3.3.1, 3.3.3), never a bare "Invalid value".
- Required fields carry a visible required marker in addition to `aria-required`; the marker is never color alone.
- Validation runs on submit. After the first submit, a field re-validates on blur; before it, typing never produces an error.
- Numeric fields state units and currency in the label.
- Secret-adjacent fields are named by their configured state (`Secret reference: configured`), never by value.
- Policy-source rows have an accessible name including kind and basis.

### Other criteria

- Reflow: every route stays operable at 320 CSS px width (400% zoom), including proposal detail, version history, and `high-impact-confirmation`. Evidence at 320 px is part of `LR-UI-CONFORMANCE`.
- Target size (2.5.8): every actionable target is at least 24 by 24 CSS px in all three densities, or meets the spacing exception; Compact density is in the conformance lane.
- Focus not obscured (2.4.11): no sticky element overlaps the content scroll area, or scroll-padding equals sticky heights.
- Dragging (2.5.7): no drag-only operation.
- 3.2.6 Consistent Help: N/A, no help mechanism is repeated across pages. 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication: N/A, authentication is shell-owned.
- Reduced motion never hides generation, approval, or posting state changes. No `MessageBarAnimation` is enabled, and no control activates on `pointerdown` or `mousedown`; Fluent's click activation satisfies 2.5.2 and this states the inherited behavior so a hand-authored control cannot drift from it.
- Language of parts (3.1.2): generated content, the editor textarea, the preview, and Conversation-derived evidence carry a `lang` attribute from the Provider result or the Conversation language when either is known, because that text is not necessarily in the UI culture; where neither is known the region inherits the page language.
- Secrets, raw payloads, and other-tenant data never appear in accessible names, tooltips, copied text, diagnostics, or announcements.
- Timing (2.2.1): proposal expiry is claimed under the **essential exception**. The limit is essential because the decision being timed is a governed one: the approved version is pinned to the Conversation Context and the Content Safety Policy version it was generated under, and a reservation is held against a cost cap for its duration. Removing the limit would not slow the activity down, it would invalidate it — an approval posted against stale context, under a superseded policy, on an indefinitely held reservation is not the same act. None of the other three allowances is satisfied, and the deferral records that plainly: there is no **Turn off**; **Extend** is unavailable because there is no in-UI extension at all, so the 20-seconds-and-ten-extensions path is not open; and **Adjust** is unavailable because the window is configurable by a Tenant Agent Administrator from 1 hour to 30 days, not by the Approver who meets it. The nearing-expiry threshold and its 15-minute floor are a **usability commitment**, not a 2.2.1 mechanism: they neither strengthen nor are required by the essential exception, and the sign-off must not be recorded as though they were.
- Timing (2.2.1), **second limit**: the A-7 **retry window** — 3 attempts within 15 minutes of the first `PostingFailed` — is a content-imposed time limit in its own right and needs its own essential argument. It is essential for the same class of reason as expiry but on different facts: the retry reuses the **deterministic `MessageId`** and re-runs the full pre-post re-validation set, and both are only sound within a bounded window — outside it the deterministic identity may collide with a post that has since landed by another path, and the re-validation would be attesting to state it no longer describes. None of the other three allowances is satisfiable: there is no **Turn off**; **Extend** does not exist; and **Adjust** is explicitly unavailable because AD-21 fixes the bound in AD-5 and makes it **not tenant-configurable** — so, unlike expiry, not even an administrator can change it. Because the window can therefore only be met, it carries the same warning obligations as expiry: a **nearing-window rendering flag** on the same terms as nearing expiry, announced once, computed with `PausedDuration` from the server read time; the remaining time rendered on the proposal detail itself rather than only inside the retry confirmation, since an Approver who never opens that dialog would otherwise lose the exit with no signal at all; and **`retry window expired`** as an announced outcome in the politeness table.
- Pause, Stop, Hide (2.2.2): claimed explicitly rather than left to the reader, because four Agents surfaces update without the user asking and a reviewer signing `LR-UI-CONFORMANCE` otherwise has no row to sign. Nothing in Agents moves, blinks, scrolls, or auto-plays, so only the auto-updating clause applies. Three of the four are claimed under the criterion's **essential exception**, where the update *is* the information and pausing it would render something the spine forbids rendering: the 250 ms / 8 s post-write catch-up poll, whose entire purpose is to replace an unproven optimistic state with an authoritative one, and which terminates at catch-up exhaustion into `awaiting projection` with a manual refresh action; the readiness re-evaluation on poll and window focus, where a lapsed `ValidUntil` must not keep rendering a prior Success; and the pending-command sequence, which resolves to a terminal state and stops. Each is bounded, each ends, and each has the refresh action as its manual control. The fourth — the **queue's periodic re-sort**, which moves content under a reader for no reason the reader asked for — is **not** claimed essential, and its mechanism is the deferral in § Proposal queue rules, named here as the 2.2.2 mechanism it is: the re-sort is held, the polite node announces `{count} proposals changed. Refresh list.`, and the new order applies only on the explicit refresh, with the deferral armed by reading context rather than by DOM focus so it arms for a screen-reader user too. A build that auto-refreshes the grid under the reader therefore fails this criterion rather than merely diverging from a stated preference. Rendered countdowns are not an auto-updating display under this criterion: per § Live regions they refresh at most once a minute, outside every live region, beside an absolute timestamp that does not move at all.
- Conformance evidence: an Agents-owned axe lane over every interactive V1 route in Light, Dark, and forced-colors, plus the manual AT matrix (NVDA with Firefox, JAWS with Chrome, VoiceOver with Safari), is `LR-UI-CONFORMANCE` evidence. FrontComposer override diagnostics (HFC1050 to HFC1055) cover FrontComposer overrides only; hand-authored pages rely on the Agents lane.
- Whole-string English/French parity is required for every route and high-impact state. Missing routes, keys, live-region evidence, viewport evidence, or conditional skips make `LR-UI-CONFORMANCE` `InsufficientEvidence`.

## Key Flows

Each flow names its stories. UJ-1 to UJ-4 mirror the PRD's own journey titles.

### UJ-1 - Nora configures `hexa` for a tenant launch

Stories: 5.2, 5.3, 5.4, 5.5, 5.7.

1. Nora opens the Agents overview and sees `hexa` as `active, not proven callable` or `invalid configuration` with safe blockers.
2. She opens `hexa` configuration and reads the Agent Party identity status; it is read-only, and a missing or ambiguous identity is a blocker resolved by platform provisioning, not a command she can issue here.
3. She selects a Provider/model that passes the eligibility set, accepting its named `DataHandlingVersion` for the tenant before activation (FR-4, FR-33); a callable `Degraded` result stays Warning.
4. She enters Agent Instructions.
5. She chooses Automatic Response Mode or Confirmation Response Mode with `response-mode-toggle`.
6. If Confirmation Response Mode is selected, she configures the Approver Policy, including a Conversation Facilitator source and disclosure categories, and validation confirms Conversation access and segregation of duties can be satisfied.
7. She sets the proposal expiry duration and the regeneration ceiling; the confirmation states future-only effect.
8. Readiness lists remaining blockers: cost cap and rate limit unconfigured. **Neither is Nora's to clear**, and the flow names the role change inline rather than implying her tier can set the boundary it is bounded by: **initial configuration of a cap or a rate limit, and any raise, belongs to Priya, the Release Operator (`Agents.Operator`), or Raul, the Platform Operator (`Agents.PlatformOperator`)** (FR-33; § Information Architecture; `cost-control-editor`; UX-J5 step 4). Configuring an unconfigured value is by definition not a lowering, and the lower-only rule exists precisely so the boundary is not self-set. Nora sees each blocker as a localized row with its recovery owner named, and her own tier renders only the lower-only editor; the Operator editor and Overrides accordion items are **absent** for her, not disabled. Activation stays blocked until an Operator clears them.
9. She submits activation through `high-impact-confirmation` (`AgentActivation`); the UI shows `Submitted`, then `AuthoritativePending` with the projection reference.
10. `ProjectionConfirmed` shows lifecycle `active` separately from callability.
11. **Climax:** `agent-readiness-badge` shows `callable` only when the Story 5.5 result at the shown `RegistryRevision` proves it; active alone stays non-Success.
12. Resolution: the overview shows Provider/model, lifecycle, mode, callability, `EnvironmentProfile`, freshness, and safe blockers.

Failure path: no eligible Provider/model. Activation is blocked with the eligibility reason; no one can call `hexa` until the blocker is resolved.

### UJ-2 - Milan calls `hexa` from a Conversation and receives an automatic reply

Stories: 6.1 to 6.4, 6.6, 6.7.

1. Milan is in a Source Conversation he can access; **Call hexa** is enabled because `hexa` is callable.
2. He opens the dialog, reads the effective mode, submits a prompt; the UI shows `submitted`.
3. The UI shows `authoritative pending` after rendering the accepted interaction identity and projection reference.
4. `hexa` joins the Conversation at this first accepted call; membership appears as a participant.
5. Context loads within the Safe Context Budget; safety passes; cost is reserved; capacity admits.
6. Generation runs; Milan sees the in-flight state tied to the Conversation.
7. In Automatic Response Mode the call status moves to `AgentInteractionStatus.PostingPending`; nothing is a message yet. These are call statuses, not the `ProposedAgentReplyState` values of the same name: the automatic path creates no proposal to borrow them from.
8. The client renders the projection whose call status is `Posted`, carrying the posted `MessageId`.
9. **Climax:** the response appears as durable Conversation content attributed to `hexa`, with `agent-response-marker`; only this proof is Success.
10. Resolution: participants continue the Conversation.

Failure paths: Milan lacks permission, so the action is absent or `denied` before Provider invocation. Context exceeds the budget, so `context blocked`. Safety, budget, rate limit, or capacity blocks, each distinct and non-Success. Posting fails, so `PostingFailed` with the same bounded audited retry as the proposal path. Generation fails, so a failure record only. Provider outcome unknown, so `unknown outcome` with the reservation held.

### UJ-3 - Anika approves a proposed `hexa` response before it enters the Conversation

Stories: 7.1 to 7.7.

1. A participant calls `hexa` in a Conversation configured for Confirmation Response Mode; a `Pending` proposal is created after successful generation.
2. Anika, authorized by the Approver Policy and holding Conversation read access, discovers it through the pending count in the Agents overview readiness summary (§ Agents overview rules), the proposal queue, or the **Conversation status entry** — the Approver half of the dual-purpose fourth `EXT-CONV-UI-1` artifact kind in § Conversation Integration Seam, which is the only path that reaches her without leaving the Conversation.
3. She opens proposal detail; focus lands on the heading; she reviews the generated content, context metadata, caller, Provider/model, mode, and `ExpiresAt`.
4. She requests one regeneration; before Provider work the server confirms that a second Eligible Approver remains, then the proposal becomes `Regenerated`, focus stays on Regenerate, and `Version 2 generated` is announced. If none remains, the request is refused and the existing proposal stays decidable.
5. A second Eligible Approver discovers the regenerated proposal, views versions one at a time with the version radio group, and selects version 2.
6. That second Approver approves through `high-impact-confirmation`, which renders the `VersionId`, kind, author, timestamp, full content, and the safety re-check statement; the proposal becomes `Approved`. Anika cannot approve the version she requested to regenerate.
7. `PostingPending` renders; still not a message.
8. The client renders the projection whose outcome is `Posted`.
9. **Climax:** the approved version is a Conversation Message with approval evidence linked; only now is the proposal Success.
10. Resolution: the Conversation holds only the approved response; history preserves every version, the editing Party of any edit, and the policy basis per disclosure category. From the moment `Approved` is projection-confirmed, edit, regenerate, and approve are gone from the editor, so no later action can post a different version.

Segregation of duties: Anika cannot approve a version she edited or requested to regenerate; a second Eligible Approver decides it. Edit and regeneration are both refused before mutation or Provider work when that second Approver would not remain.

Failure paths: the proposal expires, so `Expired` and a new call. The second Approver's approval races expiry, so `expired at approval`. Another Approver resolves first, so `superseded by another decision`. The then-current policy fails the version, so `safety blocked at approval`. The editor is dirty, so approval is `aria-disabled` until saved or discarded. Either Party loses Conversation read access, so `existence only`. Posting fails, so `PostingFailed` with bounded retry.
