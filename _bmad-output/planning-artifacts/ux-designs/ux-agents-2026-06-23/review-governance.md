# Governance & Audit Review — Hexalith Agents

Reviewer lens: governance, authorization, audit. Run 2026-09-09 against `DESIGN.md` and `EXPERIENCE.md` as they stand after the FR-33 Update, the 16-fix prose pass, and the `Agents.AuditOperator` revert. Read in full and verified rather than accepted: `prd.md` (FR-18, FR-19, FR-33, FR-24, FR-28, FR-30, FR-32, OQ-21, OQ-28, §8.1 assumptions) and `addendum.md`; `ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md` AD-8, AD-12, AD-15, AD-22, AD-30; `launch-readiness-register.md § Gate Sets And Non-Circular Evaluation` (`OperationGateMatrixVersion = 2`); `external-dependency-register.md`; `epics.md` Stories 6.6, 7.x, 8.1–8.8; the shipped `src/Hexalith.Agents.UI/Composition/AgentsFrontComposerRegistration.cs`; and the predecessor `.working/validation-2026-09-09-post-fr33/review-governance.md`. Adversary assumed throughout: a story-dev who builds exactly what the spines say; a tenant administrator who wants the platform tier's powers; a Compliance Inspector who is also the subject of the case; an operator pulling an emergency stop and relying on the confirmation to tell them what it does.

## Overall verdict

The FR-33 Update did the hard part and did it well. The § Authorization roles section is real, correct, and traceable: the six roles bind one-to-one onto the five AD-30 policy constants, the `Agents.AuditOperator`/Compliance Inspector label split matches AD-30's own `ConversationOwner`/Conversation Facilitator precedent, and the revert of the invented `Agents.ComplianceInspector` is verified sound against AD-30 — `prd.md` names roles and no constants, so AD-30 is the naming authority and the spine now says so. Twenty of the predecessor's thirty enumerated findings are genuinely closed and I could not reopen them: the caller can no longer approve their own call (five conjuncts, stated identically wherever the predicate appears), the `Caller` source is retired, edit-time eligibility is enforced, cost caps are a two-tier lower-only editor with a Platform-Operator-only override, content safety splits into a platform publication with a rendered Security-approval stage and a stricter-only tenant delta, deletion carries the A-12 two-person rule, retry posting moved to `ConversationPosting` with the A-7 bound restored, and the FR-13 never-resolved exclusion is exact.

What it did not do is finish. The Update changed the *roles* and left the *plumbing* describing the old world, and it did so in the pattern the predecessor's Update did: counts, rosters, cross-references and downstream statements that were true before the change and are false after it. Three consequences are serious. First, no route in the spine is gated on `Agents.PlatformOperator`; every platform-scoped operation is a control on a route whose `RequiredPolicy` is a tenant-scoped constant, on a spine whose own rule is that each page carries `[Authorize(Policy = …)]` and the server decision is authoritative — so the role that owns the catalog, the safety policy, the override and the kill switch cannot enter the pages that carry them, while in shipped code the missing constant makes those same operations fall back to `Agents.Administrator`, which § Authorization roles forbids in terms two lines earlier. Second, the emergency stop's confirmation misstates the emergency stop: it says `Approved` and `PostingPending` proposals "complete or fail on their own terms" where FR-28 and AD-12 say an `Approved` proposal is *held* without starting a post, omits the edit/regeneration prohibition, the paused retry clock, and `ExpiredWhileSuspended`, and then lists the kill switch as an `Abandoned` reason that FR-18 says it can never be. Third, the segregation of duties that deletion finally got was not extended to the two operations beside it: a single Compliance Inspector still releases the hold that protects the evidence and exports it, against FR-33, prd.md:622 and AD-22, and the compliance-inspection rule the Update wrote lets the Tenant Agent Administrator post-hoc-review an inspection of which they are the recorded subject. The ten-family confirmation roster, meanwhile, is now a local subset of a matrix that says in terms that consumers may not maintain local subsets. Both spines say `status: final`; on the authorization model they are not.

## Findings

- **[critical]** The Platform Operator has no route it can enter, and the spine records a fallback its own rule forbids. § Authorization roles states "`Agents.PlatformOperator` is platform-scoped" and "A missing, stale, or unavailable role assignment fails closed and never falls back to a more permissive row" (`EXPERIENCE.md:75`, `:79`), and § Shipped-code corrections then records the live state as "`Agents.PlatformOperator` is not registered; the four tenant-scoped constants are. Every platform-scoped row therefore has no constant to gate on and falls back to `Agents.Administrator`" (`EXPERIENCE.md:733`) — verified against `AgentsFrontComposerRegistration.cs`, which declares exactly `Agents.Administrator`, `Agents.Approver`, `Agents.Operator`, `Agents.AuditOperator` and registers `/agents/providers` at `RequiredPolicy: AgentsAdministratorPolicy`. That is a whole scope tier failing *open* into the tenant tier, on a page that has already shipped, recorded as a clerical row with absorbing stories and no interim rule — unlike `EXT-SECRETS-1`, where the spine states the fail-closed interim explicitly (`EXPERIENCE.md:320`). The design side of the same defect: the IA gives no route-level constant for platform work. Launch readiness reads "`Agents.Operator`; the per-tenant kill switch `Agents.PlatformOperator`" (`EXPERIENCE.md:98`) — a route gated on a tenant-scoped constant carrying a control only the platform role may pull, so under the spine's own rule that "Every page also carries `[Authorize(Policy = ...)]`" (`EXPERIENCE.md:108`) the Platform Operator is denied the page. Provider catalog, Content safety policy and Cost controls have the same shape, while Operational status and Audit evidence show the spine *can* write a disjunctive cell. *Fix:* state in § Information Architecture that a surface carrying operations of two tiers is gated on the disjunction and each control on its own constant, and write Launch readiness, Content safety and Provider catalog as disjunctions the way Operational status already is; add to the § Shipped-code corrections row the interim that until `Agents.PlatformOperator` is registered every platform-scoped mutation control is **absent** and every platform-scoped route renders `not available`, never gated on `Agents.Administrator`.

- **[critical]** The kill-switch confirmation states the wrong semantics, and the wrong ones are the permissive ones. § Launch readiness rules has the confirmation state "proposals awaiting a decision may be rejected or abandoned **but not approved**; `Approved` and `PostingPending` proposals complete or fail on their own terms; no proposal or Audit Evidence is deleted" (`EXPERIENCE.md:308`). `prd.md:698` and AD-12 state four things this drops or inverts: awaiting proposals may not be approved **nor edited nor regenerated**; an `Approved` proposal "is held in `Approved` without starting a post and without consuming a retry attempt" — it does *not* complete on its own terms, only a `PostingPending` attempt already in flight does; the `PostingFailed` retry-window clock is **paused** and automatic and administrative retries are suspended (AD-12 records the accrual as `PausedDuration`); and `ExpiresAt` keeps running, so an expiry during suspension is recorded `ExpiredWhileSuspended` and leaves the SM-3 and SM-C5 denominators. `Suspended` is also a distinct `agent-setup` state under AD-12 and appears nowhere in either spine (`Suspended`: zero occurrences). Built from this spine, pulling the emergency stop still lets an Approver edit and regenerate, still starts posts for approved proposals, and still burns retry attempts. *Fix:* restate the confirmation to FR-28/AD-12 in full — calls disabled tenant-wide; awaiting proposals rejectable or abandonable only, never approved, edited or regenerated; `Approved` held without starting a post or consuming an attempt; an in-flight `PostingPending` uninterruptible; retries suspended with the clock paused; `ExpiresAt` unchanged with `ExpiredWhileSuspended` recorded; nothing deleted or system-abandoned — and add `Suspended` as an Agent readiness state distinct from `disabled`, plus the `ExpiredWhileSuspended` reason on the `Expired` row.

- **[critical]** The `Abandoned` reason set names the kill switch, which FR-18 says can never be a reason. `EXPERIENCE.md:416` reads "The reason is one of four typed values, each with a localized label: `NoEligibleApprover`, `SourceConversationUnavailable`, `RemovedInConversations`, or kill switch (FR-18)." `prd.md` FR-18's consequence set states the opposite in terms: "A proposal abandoned by the system carries its typed reason (`NoEligibleApprover`, `SourceConversationUnavailable`, or `RemovedInConversations`) … The kill switch (FR-28) is never a system-abandonment reason: while it is pulled, proposals awaiting a decision remain for humans to reject or abandon." AD-12 repeats it ("The switch never system-abandons or deletes a proposal"). The Update added the predecessor's requested typed-reason enumeration and invented a fourth value; three exist. A rendered fourth reason is a rendered claim that the emergency stop destroys pending decisions, which is the one thing FR-28 promises it does not do. *Fix:* three typed reasons, not four; state on the `Abandoned` row that the kill switch is never an abandonment reason and that awaiting proposals survive a pull for human decision.

- **[critical]** Legal-hold release and export still have no second party, on a surface where deletion now has one. § Audit governance rules gives `LegalHold` and `ExportRequest` a justification, an `EXT-SECRETS-1` fail-closed and a hold interlock, and gives deletion the A-12 two-person rule — "a Platform Operator submits deletion and a distinct Compliance Inspector must approve it before execution" (`EXPERIENCE.md:320`) — while the IA gates hold and export on `Agents.AuditOperator` alone (`EXPERIENCE.md:100`). `prd.md` FR-33 states "Release legal hold | Compliance Inspector, **with the audited approval of a second Compliance Inspector or the Platform Operator**" and "Request authorized export | Compliance Inspector, **with prior approval by a second party on the FR-24 terms; never post hoc**"; `prd.md:622` repeats both; AD-22 adds that `LegalHoldRelease` and `ExportRequest` carry the same computed-subject-set and anti-collusion terms as an inspection, that the export envelope key is delivered to the requesting Inspector principal, and (Second-update boundary) that a `Platform` principal may dispatch `LegalHoldRelease` only as the recorded second-party approver. `prd.md:843` already flags this as an open Spine defect ("AD-22 names the Tenant Agent Administrator as the sole inspection second party and gives export and hold release none, against FR-24 and FR-33"); the UX spine inherits it rather than correcting it. One Inspector therefore releases the hold protecting the evidence and exports it in two dialogs, which is the same single-actor chain the predecessor named for deletion. *Fix:* `LegalHoldRelease` and `ExportRequest` each render a second, separately authorized approval stage before the operation is submitted — a second Compliance Inspector or the Platform Operator for a release, a second party outside the computed subject set for an export, never post hoc; the requester's own approval is refused with a typed reason; `audit-governance-panel` shows requester, approver and both justifications on every release and export row.

- **[critical]** The Audit evidence IA cell grants posted provenance to three role constants, contradicting its own rules section and FR-33. `EXPERIENCE.md:99` reads "Operational and posted-provenance evidence `Agents.Administrator`, `Agents.Operator`, or `Agents.PlatformOperator`; posted provenance **also** to a current Participant with read access". § Audit evidence rules states the correct rule two hundred lines later: "Conversation-derived evidence is inspectable at **two levels** (FR-24, OQ-21): posted provenance by a current Participant with read access; and unposted versions … by a Party resolved as an Eligible Approver … or under a **compliance inspection**" (`EXPERIENCE.md:314`). FR-33's row is "Inspect the provenance of posted Agent Responses (FR-24) | A current Participant with read access to the Source Conversation | Conversation", and AD-22 is explicit that "a current Participant with read access inspects posted provenance only". Posted provenance is Conversation-derived content; the IA cell hands it to every tenant administrator, Release Operator and Platform Operator without membership or read access, and it is exactly the ambient-participation route Story 8.8 exists to prevent. A contradiction between the IA `RequiredPolicy` column and the owning rules section is the redundancy failing in the direction that matters. *Fix:* the cell reads operational evidence to the three roles; posted provenance to a current Participant with read access only; unposted protected content to Eligible Approver authority or the Story 8.8 inspection — matching § Audit evidence rules verbatim.

- **[critical]** The compliance-inspection second-party rule is materially weaker than OQ-21 and AD-22, and permits self-review. `EXPERIENCE.md:314` states an inspection "carries a recorded justification and **either second-party approval or a declared post-hoc review by the Tenant Agent Administrator**". `prd.md` OQ-21 requires approval "by a second party who is neither the Inspector nor a subject of the case — the Platform Operator or a second Compliance Inspector where the case concerns the Tenant Agent Administrator — with post-hoc review allowed **only for single-proposal or single-Conversation scopes** within the A-19 window", and FR-33's row adds "the Platform Operator approves any inspection wider than one Conversation". AD-22 is stricter still: the subject set is *computed* (every caller, editor, Approver, decision actor or Facilitator in scope, plus the TAA principals whose `ConfigurationVersion` was in force), a second party inside it or the Inspector themself is a typed rejection, two Inspectors cannot approve each other within a rolling 30 days, a **case** identifier is by definition wider than one Conversation and therefore always needs Platform Operator approval, and an unreviewed post-hoc inspection is recorded as a persistent visible flag. The spine's sentence permits a case-wide inspection to proceed on the post-hoc review of the very Tenant Agent Administrator whose configuration is under examination. *Fix:* state the four constraints as rendered mechanics — the second party is computed and the surface refuses an approver inside the subject set with a typed reason; post-hoc review is offered only when the scope is a single proposal or single Conversation, with the 7-day A-19 window rendered; any case scope requires Platform Operator approval before the first content read; an unreviewed inspection renders as an `UnreviewedInspection` governance signal on the surfaces the TAA and Platform Operator read.

- **[high]** The ten-family confirmation roster is a local subset of a matrix that forbids local subsets. `EXPERIENCE.md:222`, `:298`, `:442`, `:480`, `:496` all say "the ten families" and § Confirmation contents by family enumerates exactly ten (`ProposalResolution`, `ConversationPosting`, `ProviderCatalogMutation`, `PolicyPublication`, `TenantBudgetUpdate`, `AgentActivation`, `AgentSetupMutation`, `LegalHold`, `ExportRequest`, `DeletionRequest`). `launch-readiness-register.md § Gate Sets And Non-Circular Evaluation` at `OperationGateMatrixVersion = 2` states "Every public command and every workflow activity declares exactly one operation family … This matrix is the single contract consumed by API, BFF, UI, workflow, and the `launch-readiness` projection … consumers may not maintain local subsets", and carries `TenantKillSwitch`, `TenantProviderEnablement`, `LegalHoldRelease`, `ExportDownload`, `AuditInspection`, `ProposalEdit` and `ProposalRegeneration` as families in their own right. The Update added governed writes for five of those — the kill switch, the block and re-admit, per-tenant Provider enablement, hold release, and the compliance inspection — without a family, a gate set, or a confirmation-contents row, while `EXPERIENCE.md:111` calls the kill switch and the block "`high-impact-confirmation` families with a required justification" that the family table does not contain, and `EXPERIENCE.md:258` states "the rendered set is what the audit record carries". Those five operations therefore have no specified audit record. *Fix:* raise the roster to the matrix v2 families the UI actually issues, add a contents row for each (`TenantKillSwitch`: trigger condition invoked, tenant-wide semantics, justification; the block/clear commands under `AgentSetupMutation` per AD-12, with the abandonment consequence and `ReadmitPending`; `TenantProviderEnablement`; `LegalHoldRelease` and `ExportDownload`; `AuditInspection`: case scope, computed second party, review mode), and replace every "the ten families" with a reference to the register's matrix version rather than a count.

- **[high]** Justification is required on five families; AD-22 requires it on nine with no exemption. § Audit governance rules states "Per FR-30, each of these plus `PolicyPublication` and `TenantBudgetUpdate` requires a justification typed into the confirmation before Confirm becomes available" (`EXPERIENCE.md:320`) — `LegalHold`, `ExportRequest`, `DeletionRequest`, `PolicyPublication`, `TenantBudgetUpdate`. AD-22 states "the reject-on-missing-or-whitespace-justification rule applies to all nine AD-12 lock-bearing families — `ProposalResolution`, `PolicyPublication`, `TenantBudgetUpdate`, `LegalHold` (also `LegalHoldRelease`), `ExportRequest` (also `ExportDownload`), `DeletionRequest`, `ProviderCatalogMutation` (also `TenantProviderEnablement`), `AgentSetupMutation` (also `TenantKillSwitch`, and the block/clear commands …), and `AgentActivation` — with no exempt family". The four the spine omits are the four whose confirmation contents it specifies without a justification field: approval and rejection of a proposal, a catalog mutation, a setup mutation, an activation. The server will reject exactly those submissions, and the UI as specified offers no field to satisfy them. *Fix:* state the justification rule once over the AD-22 lock-bearing set rather than over FR-30's subset, and add the required-justification line to the `ProposalResolution`, `ProviderCatalogMutation`, `AgentSetupMutation` and `AgentActivation` contents rows.

- **[high]** Block and re-admit are stated with symmetric authority the sources deny, on a route the named authority cannot enter. § Operational status rules and `EXPERIENCE.md:111` give both transitions the same authority — "the Tenant Agent Administrator or Conversation Facilitator (FR-2, A-9)" (`EXPERIENCE.md:302`). FR-33 splits them: setting a block is TAA **or** Facilitator, but clearing it is "The authority that set the block, evaluated at clear time, or the Tenant Agent Administrator; **a Conversation Facilitator cannot clear an Administrator's block**", records `ReadmitPending`, and an `ExternallyRemoved` record is cleared on different terms again; `epics.md:2097` states the same rule as a Story 6.6 acceptance criterion. The spine as written lets a Facilitator undo a tenant administrator's governance decision. Compounding it, the controls sit on `/agents/status`, whose `RequiredPolicy` is "`Agents.Administrator`, `Agents.Operator`, or `Agents.PlatformOperator`" (`EXPERIENCE.md:97`) — the Conversation Facilitator is not an Agents role and holds no constant (§ Authorization roles), so the FR-33 authority the spine names has no reachable surface, while Release and Platform Operators, whom that FR-33 row does not name, see and can operate the control. *Fix:* state clear authority as the setting authority re-evaluated at clear time or the Tenant Agent Administrator, with a Facilitator refused on an Administrator-set block by a typed reason; render `ReadmitPending` and the distinct `ExternallyRemoved` clear path; and either give the Facilitator-facing block control a Conversation-scoped surface or state that the Facilitator exercises it through Conversations, not here.

- **[high]** Retry posting is offered on every `PostingFailed` with attempts remaining, including a safety-verdict failure. § Proposal editor action rail gives "`PostingFailed`, retries remaining | Retry posting; abandon where permitted; audit" with no exclusion (`EXPERIENCE.md:250`), and § Confirmation contents by family names the four pre-post re-checks but not the exclusion. `prd.md` FR-18 states "a `PostingFailed` proposal whose reason is a safety verdict is **not retryable and accepts abandon only**", and `epics.md:2613` carries it as an acceptance criterion. Under the spine, an Approver retries a post the then-current Content Safety Policy refused, once per attempt, three times. *Fix:* Retry posting is **absent** (or `DisabledFocusable` with the typed reason) when the `PostingFailed` reason is a safety verdict; the row states that abandon is the only exit; the same exclusion applies to the administrative retry.

- **[high]** No `MessageId` existence lookup and no `LateConfirmed`, so the spine can record `Abandoned` over a message that posted. § Proposal lifecycle and the action rail specify retry and both administrative exits with no pre-exit lookup, and `LateConfirmed` occurs nowhere in either spine. `prd.md` FR-18 requires that "Before every retry attempt and before any exit from `PostingFailed` — including abandonment by any actor — the system looks the message up by its `MessageId` … If the message is present, the proposal moves to `Posted` with the `LateConfirmed` flag recorded in Audit Evidence and status, and the requested retry or abandon is rejected with a typed reason; a proposal whose approved version is in the Conversation is never recorded `Abandoned`", and that an unavailable lookup refuses the exit. AD-15's parity list names `LateConfirmed` as a public contract value; `epics.md:2636` owns `FR18.MessageId-before-exit` and `FR18.LateConfirmed`. The audit record for the affected proposal would say the reply was withdrawn while it sits in the Conversation. *Fix:* add the pre-exit lookup to the retry and both administrative-exit confirmations as a stated pre-check with its three outcomes (absent → proceed; present → `Posted` with `LateConfirmed` and the exit refused; unavailable → exit refused, proposal stays `PostingFailed`); add `LateConfirmed` to § Contracts that must grow and a display treatment to the `Posted` row.

- **[high]** Three FR-33 rows have no surface, under a closure statement that says every stated need has one. `EXPERIENCE.md:113` declares "Surface closure status: **final for V1**" and § Authorization roles asserts "nothing in this spine grants an operation FR-33 withholds" — but says nothing about operations FR-33 grants that the spine omits. Missing: **Accept a Provider/model's named `DataHandlingVersion` for the tenant, before first activation and on each change** (Tenant Agent Administrator, `[A-10]`) — `DataHandling` has zero occurrences in either spine, and `prd.md:199` adds a 30-day grace after which the model cannot be called for that tenant, which is a fail-closed state with no rendering; **Confirm an SM-4 cross-tenant or unauthorized event** (Platform Operator, recorded) — the Update added the denial *count* to Operational status but not the confirmation act that FR-28 makes the immediate kill-switch trigger; and **Convene and record the kill-switch trigger review** (Release Operator, within one business day), together with AD-12's `TriggerReviewOverdue`, which "blocks qualification" and has no row class on `launch-readiness-panel` beside `UnretiredAssumption`. *Fix:* add a data-handling acceptance item to `agent-config-form` with the accepted `DataHandlingVersion`, the re-acceptance prompt on change, and the grace expiry as a blocker; add an SM-4 confirmation act beside the denial count under `Agents.PlatformOperator`; add the trigger-review record and the `TriggerReviewOverdue` blocker to `launch-readiness-panel`; then re-state closure, or scope it to what is actually closed.

- **[high]** The Disable confirmation tells the administrator the opposite of what AD-12 does, and the proposal editor has no rendering for either stop. `agent-config-form` states "The Disable confirmation names the Agent, states that in-flight calls complete and new calls are rejected before Provider invocation, and states that **pending proposals stay resolvable**" (`EXPERIENCE.md:206`). AD-12: "A disabled Agent blocks new side effects for its proposals: awaiting proposals may be rejected or abandoned but **never approved, edited, or regenerated**; an `Approved` proposal cannot begin posting; … a `PostingFailed` … retry is suspended with its retry-window clock paused". FR-18's transition guards carry the same condition ("Agent `Active` and tenant not `Suspended`") on edit, regeneration and approval. "Resolvable" over-promises by exactly the three operations that matter, and § Proposal editor action rail has no variant in which Approve, Edit and Regenerate are absent for a disabled Agent or a pulled kill switch — so the editor renders Approve and the server refuses it. *Fix:* the Disable confirmation states that awaiting proposals may be rejected or abandoned only, that approval, edit and regeneration stop, that an `Approved` proposal will not begin posting, and that retries pause; add one action-rail variant covering both stops in which Approve, Edit and Regenerate are absent with the typed reason while Reject and Abandon remain.

A story-dev building the form from `DESIGN.md` ships three kinds, and the Story 8.8 inspection has no entry point. *Fix:* update the `DESIGN.md` line to the five kinds, or drop the enumeration there and defer to the EXPERIENCE row.

A dev renders all three items and hides the buttons, which is the field-inventory leak the provider-catalog fix exists to prevent. *Fix:* add a `read-only (lower-only)` variant to `cost-control-editor` and a `tenant delta only` variant to `content-safety-policy-editor`, and state in the layout table that the Operator editor and Overrides items are absent in the first and the Platform draft/publication item absent in the second.

Unchanged from the predecessor's medium, now load-bearing for a governed read path rather than only for completeness.
