Reviewer lens: governance, authorization, trust. Run 2026-09-12 against `DESIGN.md` (390 lines) and `EXPERIENCE.md` (888 lines), both `status: final`, `updated: 2026-09-12`, after the 2026-09-10 UX Update and the 2026-09-12 architecture round.

Read in full and verified rather than accepted: `prd.md` FR-33 (the authoritative matrix, 36 operation rows), FR-19 to FR-21, FR-24, FR-28, FR-30, FR-32, and `addendum.md`; `ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md` at `ARCH-A-INDEX-6`; `launch-readiness-register.md § Gate Sets And Non-Circular Evaluation` at `OperationGateMatrixVersion = 3` (24 operation families); the four 2026-09-12 architecture reviews (`review-2026-09-12-adversarial-divergence.md` through its Current-Tree Data-Handling Closure, `review-2026-09-12-verified-current.md` through its Data-Handling Wording Recheck, `reconcile-prd-2026-09-12.md` and `reconcile-ux-2026-09-12.md` through their final addenda); `reconcile-validation-2026-09-10.md`; and the prior round's `.working/validation-2026-09-10-round4/review-governance.md`.

Adversary assumed throughout: a story-dev who builds exactly what the spines say and nothing more; a Platform Operator who needs a tenant to act on and will invent one; a Tenant Agent Administrator who wants the platform tier's powers; a Compliance Inspector who cannot reach the route their own governed flow lives on; an Approver who approves once and discovers there is no way back.

## Overall verdict

The round-4 Update landed. All six of the prior round's criticals and all three of its regressions are genuinely closed, and I could not reopen any of them: the two-tier disjunctive route rule is stated as a general rule and the `Agents.PlatformOperator` shipped-drift row now carries the fail-closed interim it lacked; the kill-switch confirmation states FR-28/OQ-27/AD-12 semantics in full including the held `Approved` proposal, the paused retry clock and `ExpiredWhileSuspended`; the `Abandoned` reason set is four contract tokens none of which is the kill switch, with the contradiction's source named; hold release and export carry the same request/approve/execute staging deletion had; the Audit evidence tiers no longer merge; and the compliance inspection is four enforced mechanics rather than prose. The justification rule is stated once over all ten AD-12 lock-bearing families with no exemption, the confirmation roster is cited by matrix version rather than a count, and the whole 2026-09-12 architecture reviewer set — adversarial-divergence, verified-current, reconcile-prd, reconcile-ux — closes at PASS against the current tree, so the spine-versus-architecture surface is the healthiest part of this document pair.

What remains is a different failure mode from the prior round's. Round 4 found statements that contradicted their sources. This round finds **three governed things the spine never says at all**, each of which a story-dev can implement in more than one way without violating a written word. First, the Audit evidence routes carry disclosure tiers in the `RequiredPolicy` column and no route-level authorization expression, on a spine whose own rule is that every page carries `[Authorize(Policy = …)]` and whose own disjunction paragraph asserts — incorrectly — that Audit evidence "already show[s] the disjunctive form"; two of the three tiers resolve to no policy constant at all, so the Participant, the Eligible Approver and the Compliance Inspector are all gated out of the evidence surfaces, and the Story 8.8 inspection has no entry point on any surface. Second, the Platform Operator is "never tenant-scoped" and performs six FR-33 operations whose scope column reads **Tenant** — kill switch, per-tenant Provider enablement, caps and rate limits, the audited override, deletion, data-handling tightening declaration — and neither spine says how the target tenant is chosen, where it is rendered, whether it is part of the audit record, or how a tenant picker avoids disclosing the tenant inventory that FR-19 exists to protect. Third, `ProposalEdit` and `ProposalRegeneration` are operation families in the authoritative matrix that the UI unambiguously issues, and both spines are silent on them while the spine's own rule says a family the UI issues and the table omits is a defect in the table and the register says consumers may not maintain local subsets.

Beneath those, two governance properties an operator must be able to rely on are simply absent: **approval is irreversible in V1** — the retraction seam `EXT-CONV-AI-1` seam 7 is `Uncommitted` and explicitly unusable — and the approve confirmation never says so, while the deletion confirmation carries an irreversibility line for a lesser act; and **audit evidence immutability** is asserted nowhere, `immutable`, `append-only` and `tamper` returning zero hits across both files. A governance document whose whole premise is reconstructing why an agent said what it said does not tell the reader the record cannot be edited.

Both spines still say `status: final`. On authorization routing, tenant binding, and the two operation families above, they are not.

## Findings

- **[critical]** The Audit evidence routes have no route-level authorization expression, and two of their three disclosure tiers resolve to no policy constant (`EXPERIENCE.md:100`, IA row `Audit evidence`; rule at `:109`; rule at `:110`). The `RequiredPolicy` cell reads "Three disclosure tiers, never merged: **operational and configuration/governance evidence** to `Agents.Administrator`, `Agents.Operator`, or `Agents.PlatformOperator`; **posted provenance** to a current Participant … **only**; **unposted protected content** to Eligible Approver authority or … `Agents.AuditOperator`." A Conversation Participant holds **no** policy constant (`:68`, the `Conversation Participant` row's `Policy constant` cell reads `none`); an Eligible Approver holds `Agents.Approver`, which is not listed; `Agents.AuditOperator` appears only inside the third tier's prose. The spine's own rules are that "Every page also carries `[Authorize(Policy = ...)]` and the server decision is authoritative" (`:110`) and that a two-tier surface "is gated at the route on the _disjunction_ of the tiers" (`:109`) — and that same sentence asserts "Operational status and **Audit evidence** already show the disjunctive form", which for Audit evidence is false: the cell lists content tiers, not a constant disjunction. A story-dev reading the cell top-down ships `[Authorize(Policy = Agents.Administrator)]` (or the three-way operator disjunction) on `/agents/audit` and `/agents/proposals/{AgentInteractionId}/audit`, and the Participant inspecting posted provenance, the Eligible Approver inspecting unposted versions, and the Compliance Inspector running the Story 8.8 inspection are all denied the page that carries their FR-33 rows. The failure is closed rather than open, which is why it is not worse — but it makes three FR-33 grants unreachable and silently defeats the disclosure model the Update just built. *Fix:* write the cell the way Launch readiness is written — `Route: Agents.Administrator, Agents.Operator, Agents.PlatformOperator, Agents.Approver, or Agents.AuditOperator; a Participant with current read access reaches the posted-provenance detail through the Conversation-derived path with no Agents constant; each tier's content decided by its own predicate` — and correct the `:109` claim, which currently certifies a surface that does not have the shape it claims.

- **[critical]** Nothing in either spine binds a **tenant** to a platform-scoped operation, and six FR-33 rows need one (`EXPERIENCE.md:63`, `:77`, `:91`, `:94`, `:95`, `:99`, `:101`, `:285`, `:305`). § Authorization roles states "`Agents.PlatformOperator` is platform-scoped … never tenant-scoped" (`:63`) and "Every role except Platform Operator is tenant-scoped" (`:77`). FR-33's scope column reads **Tenant** for: enable a Provider/model for a tenant (A-10); declare a `DataHandlingVersion` tightening; configure cost caps and rate limits; override a reached cost cap; pull and release the per-tenant kill switch; request approved deletion. `tenant selector`, `tenant context`, `tenant switch` all return **zero hits** in `EXPERIENCE.md`. § Confirmation contents by family requires "the action, resource identity, future-only effect, authorization basis" (`:285`) — the tenant is not among them, and only `DeletionRequest` (`:300`) and `TenantProviderEnablement` (`:305`) name a tenant in their per-family additions, while `TenantKillSwitch` (`:303`) and `TenantBudgetUpdate` (`:294`, `:295`) do not. So a story-dev must invent the mechanism: a free-text tenant id, a dropdown enumerating every tenant in the platform, or reuse of the shell tenant switcher — and the middle option publishes the tenant inventory to a surface FR-19 exists to protect, while the first makes the kill switch a typo away from suspending the wrong tenant with no rendered confirmation of which one. This is the shape the review brief calls critical: an implementer can get it badly wrong without contradicting a written word. *Fix:* state in § Authorization roles that a platform-scoped operation acting at tenant scope resolves its target tenant through one named mechanism, that the resolved tenant is rendered in the confirmation and carried in the audit record as part of the authorization basis, and that the selector discloses only tenants the acting principal is authorized to see; add the tenant to the required contents in `§ Confirmation contents by family` rather than to three of its rows.

- **[medium]** The visual contract does not distinguish denial from failure; only Operational status does (`DESIGN.md:211`, `:227-234`; `EXPERIENCE.md:522`, `:355`). `DESIGN.md:211` defines the role as "`{colors.status-danger}` means failure **or** denial: denied, generation failed, `PostingFailed`, `Rejected`, Provider error, superseded by another decision" — one semantic role and one glyph (`DismissCircle16`) carrying an authorization denial, a runtime failure, a human rejection and a concurrency outcome. `{colors.status-severe}` by contrast is precise ("blocked but not a runtime failure") and `{colors.status-important}` is precise ("uncertain and must resolve before side effects"), so the pair *can* draw the line and does not draw it here.
