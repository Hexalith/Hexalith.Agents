## Overall verdict

All five v2 findings are addressed in the text, and the fixes are, on the whole, well-targeted rather than cosmetic: two (justification enforcement, PartyId global invariant) are unambiguous, complete closures; two (legal-hold residual risk, HTTP-instrumentation leakage) close the finding as *scoped* — they convert an unbounded gap into either a real register-tracked blocker or an explicit configuration requirement, which is exactly what v2 asked for, but neither one eliminates residual exposure by itself; one (User role freshness) is a faithful mirror of the Administrator language and is textually solid, with one small unclosed seam noted below. Two of v2's three "secondary observations" (tenant-check-before-freshness ordering, `ProposalResolution` justification) were also fixed as a side effect of the AD-17 and AD-22 edits, though they weren't asked for. One new finding surfaces from this pass: AD-29's now-more-precise command-payload idempotency fingerprint (the new presence-byte marker language) sharpens, rather than resolves, a pre-existing ambiguity about whether that fingerprint — which for `ProposalEdit` hashes actual edited proposal content — is required to be HMAC-keyed like every other sensitive-content digest in the same AD, or is allowed to be the unkeyed SHA-256 the shared canonicalizer literally specifies.

## Finding 1 — Legal hold / erasure does not bind the posted copy in Conversations

**Status: PARTIALLY CLOSED — converted from a silent gap into a named, register-tracked residual risk with a defined off-ramp, but the off-ramp is not yet exercised and nothing in the spine or register requires it to be exercised before `RQ-1` can pass.**

**What changed:** AD-22's closing sentence now reads: *"EventStore history is never rewritten; posted Conversation Messages remain governed by Conversations retention, independent of an Agents-side `LegalHold` — a hold that outlives Conversations' own retention window for the posted copy is a named residual risk, not a silently absorbed one: it is tracked inside `LR-AUDIT-PROTECTION-DELETION`'s evidence contract in `launch-readiness-register.md` and as spine assumption ARCH-A-10 until a Conversations-owned preservation seam is committed or Legal confirms the residual risk is accepted."* A new assumption row exists: `ARCH-A-10 | A posted Conversation Message's Conversations-owned retention is independent of an Agents-side LegalHold; no seam propagates an Agents hold into Conversations preservation | AD-22 | Governance + Compliance | A Conversations-owned preservation seam is committed, or Legal confirms the residual risk is accepted (tracked inside LR-AUDIT-PROTECTION-DELETION)`.

**Stress-testing the register side (this was the specific ask):** the register row is genuinely updated, not a restatement:

> `LR-AUDIT-PROTECTION-DELETION` | Payload protection, authorized audit, retention/legal hold/export, cryptographic erasure/redaction, named projection purge, and safe tombstone evidence. **Includes evidence that a `LegalHold` either propagates to (or has a confirmed accepted-risk waiver for) the posted copy of held content in Conversations, since AD-22 concedes posted Conversation Messages are otherwise governed only by Conversations' own independent retention (spine assumption ARCH-A-10).** Invalidated by protection, retention, export, deletion, propagation-seam, or projection-inventory changes.

This is precise enough to function as a real gate later, for three concrete reasons:
1. It names a disjunctive but bounded evidence requirement ("propagates to ... or has a confirmed accepted-risk waiver for"), not an open-ended aspiration — either condition is independently checkable against the general record schema (`EvidenceReference`, `SourceVersion`, `Owner`).
2. `LR-AUDIT-PROTECTION-DELETION` is already wired into `QualificationExecutionGateSet` and into the `OperationGateMatrix` rows for every lock-bearing family that touches content (`LegalHold`, `DeletionRequest`, `ExportRequest`, `AgentActivation`, `ProviderCatalogMutation`, `AgentSetupMutation`, `ProposalResolution`, `PolicyPublication`, `TenantBudgetUpdate`, `ConversationPosting`, `AuditInspection` (v2), `TenantKillSwitch` (v2), `TenantProviderEnablement` (v2)) — so a failure to close this sub-clause blocks essentially every governance-relevant operation family, not a side path that could be quietly skipped.
3. The register's current instance of the row (`| LR-AUDIT-PROTECTION-DELETION | TBD | production-like | InsufficientEvidence | Security Engineering | TBD | TBD | TBD | Levels 4 and 5 | TBD | Protection/retention/hold/export/deletion/projection contract required | EvidenceNotRecorded |`, line 260) is currently `InsufficientEvidence`, which per the register's own state table (`No record exists ... implicit Block`, and `InsufficientEvidence` when "required timestamps, samples, live evidence, manifest fields, or evidence level are absent") means `RQ-1` cannot return READY today. The propagation-or-waiver sub-clause is one more thing this already-blocking gate must satisfy, not a new independent control that could be silently left unevaluated.

**What is still open, and why this is PARTIALLY, not fully, CLOSED:**
- The fix is a *process* commitment (build a propagation seam, or get Legal to accept the risk) recorded as evidence obligations, not an *architectural* mechanism. No seam, activity, or command is named anywhere in the spine (AD-6, AD-22, or the External Prerequisites table) that would let an Agents-side `LegalHold` actually reach into Conversations and block/preserve the posted message. If the eventual answer is "Legal accepts the risk," the underlying technical gap (Finding 1's original concrete failure scenario — litigation hold expires the posted record while Agents' own `Active` state and `audit-evidence` projection still assert protection) is never engineered away, only sanctioned. That may be a legitimate business outcome, but the spine should be read as having *deferred* the decision, not *resolved* it.
- Nothing in AD-22, AD-6, or the register's evaluation rule requires the waiver to be re-confirmed if Conversations' retention policy changes; the register row says it is "Invalidated by ... retention ... changes," which does cover this, but only insofar as whoever owns Conversations' retention policy remembers to flag the change against this gate — there's no explicit cross-module invalidation trigger named (e.g., a version pin on the Conversations retention policy that `LR-AUDIT-PROTECTION-DELETION`'s `SourceVersion` field must track). This is a smaller, second-order gap, not the finding itself.
- AD-6 (the Conversations boundary AD, cross-referenced in the original finding) is unchanged: it still says only that Agents posts through supported seams and never writes Conversation streams directly. No sentence in AD-6 acknowledges the hold/retention interaction; all of the new language lives in AD-22 and the register. That's an acceptable division of labor (AD-22 is the audit-governance AD) but means a reader of AD-6 alone would still see no hint of the residual risk.

**Verdict rationale:** this is exactly the recommendation direction v2 offered ("or explicitly document the residual risk and require it to be surfaced as a named blocker in `launch-readiness-register.md`") — the authors took the documented-residual-risk branch rather than building the propagation seam, and did so with real teeth (a blocking gate, not a comment). That satisfies the letter and reasonable spirit of the v2 recommendation. It does not eliminate the underlying data-integrity exposure, which is why this is PARTIALLY CLOSED rather than CLOSED: a compliance reviewer reading only AD-22 today would correctly conclude the risk is real, tracked, and currently blocking release — which is the intended, honest outcome — but the risk itself still exists in the system until one of the two ARCH-A-10 exit conditions is met.

## Finding 2 — Audit-justification enforcement doesn't cover all lock-bearing families

**Status: CLOSED.**

**What changed:** AD-22's enforcement clause now reads: *"the reject-on-missing-or-whitespace-justification rule applies to all nine AD-12 lock-bearing families — `ProposalResolution`, `PolicyPublication`, `TenantBudgetUpdate`, `LegalHold` (also `LegalHoldRelease`), `ExportRequest` (also `ExportDownload`), `DeletionRequest`, `ProviderCatalogMutation` (also `TenantProviderEnablement`), `AgentSetupMutation` (also `TenantKillSwitch`), and `AgentActivation` — with no exempt family."*

This directly closes the gap: all nine families named by AD-12 are now explicitly listed against the rejection rule (not just described by the general "every lock-bearing-family command... carr[ies]... justification" clause), and the closing "with no exempt family" forecloses the "the six-item list is illustrative vs. exhaustive" ambiguity v2 flagged. `TenantKillSwitch` and `ProviderCatalogMutation` — the two operations the v2 concrete-failure scenario centered on — are now explicitly in scope. This also resolves v2's third secondary observation (`ProposalResolution`'s administrative retry/abandon was audited but not justification-gated even in the general clause's spirit) as a side effect, since `ProposalResolution` is now named.

## Finding 3 — Role-claim freshness hardened for `Administrator`/`Platform` but not `User`

**Status: CLOSED, with one residual textual asymmetry worth tracking (not severity-worthy on its own).**

**What changed:** AD-30 now reads: *"`User` (`TenantId`, `PartyId`, resolved FR-33 roles re-read from the Tenants-projection role assignment at every command ingress, mirroring the `Administrator` freshness rule below — never trusted from a cached or token-embedded role claim alone)"*.

This is a verbatim mirror of the `Administrator` clause's rigor: same source of truth (Tenants projection), same trigger ("at every command ingress" vs. Administrator's "populated only by the Agents API ingress after a fresh Tenants-projection role check"), and the same explicit negative ("never trusted from a cached or token-embedded role claim alone" vs. "never from JWT roles alone"). The v2 concrete failure scenario (an off-boarded Tenant Agent Administrator's stale session token continuing to pass role-gated commands until natural token expiry) is closed: the rule now requires the role assignment itself to be re-read from the authoritative Tenants projection on every command, not merely "resolved" once. This is enforceable and testable the same way the pre-existing Administrator rule was already accepted as enforceable in v2 (via `LR-TENANT-ACCESS`'s "Fresh tenant access ... for every affected public/runtime path. Invalidated by authorization policy, tenant projection, identity, or role mapping changes" contract) — nothing in this pass suggests the mechanism is aspirational text with no backing gate.

**Residual asymmetry (not a new finding, just worth naming for the next reviewer):** AD-12's explicit list of what "every side-effecting step... re-reads" — *"Agent lifecycle, tenant enablement, the per-Conversation block, and the per-tenant kill switch"* — is unchanged and still does not name role rights; AD-12 still separately says only *"role rights from the FR-33 matrix carried by the AD-30 principal."* Because AD-30 now guarantees the principal's roles are fresh *at ingress*, this is consistent rather than contradictory — but it does mean role freshness is guaranteed once, at the API boundary, per command, rather than being part of the same continuous per-side-effecting-step re-evaluation loop that lifecycle/enablement/block/kill-switch get. For a synchronous command handler this is a distinction without a difference. For any command whose side effects are dispatched asynchronously well after ingress (the spine's own workflow model defers Provider invocation, posting, and retries into Dapr Workflow activities under the `Workflow` principal, which carries no role claim at all and is scoped by allowlisted command sets instead), it doesn't matter either, since those steps run under `Workflow`, not `User`. The asymmetry therefore has no live exploit path in the current design; it is noted only because it's the kind of phrasing gap a future edit could reintroduce risk through if AD-12's re-read list is ever used as the sole definition of "what gets checked before a side effect."

## Finding 4 — "PartyId reference only, never PII" stated in exactly one AD

**Status: CLOSED.**

**What changed:** AD-7 gained: *"This PartyId-reference-only rule binds every Party-adjacent field anywhere in the Agents domain and its projections — the `Agent` Party identity link (AD-2), `ApproverPolicy`'s predefined Parties (AD-8), and every principal's `PartyId` (AD-30) alike — none of which may cache a Party attribute (display name, email, or other PII) without an AD-7 amendment."*

This is a complete, direct fix, and better than the "restate at every AD" recommendation direction v2 offered: rather than duplicating the constraint three more times (with the attendant risk of the copies drifting), it makes AD-7 the single normative source and explicitly cross-references the exact three ADs (AD-2, AD-8, AD-30) the v2 finding named, closing the loophole the v2 concrete failure scenario exploited (an Admin UI convenience `DisplayName` cache on the `Agent` or `ApproverPolicy` projection). The added "without an AD-7 amendment" clause gives it teeth as a change-control gate, not just descriptive text.

## Finding 5 — No guard against secret/content leakage through generic Provider-call observability instrumentation

**Status: CLOSED as scoped (a required configuration behavior, not a self-verifying one) — same "documented requirement, not yet a proven control" caveat as Finding 1, but smaller in consequence.**

**What changed:** AD-14 gained: *"Any HTTP-client or SDK instrumentation wrapping the generation activity's outbound Provider call is configured to exclude request/response headers and bodies from span, metric, and log capture; this exclusion is part of the AD-17 execution-state content sweep."*

This closes the specific gap: it is now an explicit rule (not silence), it names the exact mechanism the v2 failure scenario exploited (default HTTP-client/SDK instrumentation auto-capturing headers/bodies), and — importantly — it folds the requirement into the AD-17 "execution-state content sweep" test gate by name, which was exactly the v2 recommendation ("this exclusion is itself part of the AD-17 execution-state / observability test gate"). AD-17's sweep list (*"execution-state content sweeps, FrontComposer UI conformance..."*) doesn't itself enumerate "instrumentation spans" as a checked artifact class, so a builder implementing the sweep literally could still read it as scoped to workflow/activity payloads unless they cross-reference AD-14's new sentence — but the cross-reference is now explicit and bidirectional-enough (AD-14 names AD-17; the obligation is discoverable from either AD) that this is a documentation completeness nit, not an open finding.

## New finding — MEDIUM — AD-29's command-payload idempotency fingerprint keying is still ambiguous, and the new presence-byte language sharpens rather than resolves it

**AD(s):** AD-29 (Deterministic Identity Derivation), cross-referenced against AD-12 (`ProposalEdit` relies on this exact mechanism) and AD-14 (edited content is sensitive conversation-derived content).

**What's new here:** the prior spine version already said *"API command idempotency is keyed by (`TenantId`, principal..., operation family, client key) plus the payload fingerprint from the shared canonicalizer."* The current version adds real specificity: *"every public command's fingerprint hashes its `Hexalith.Agents.Contracts` DTO through that same shared canonicalizer as its own, self-contained component list — each public property, taken in contract-declared order, is one length-prefixed component preceded by a one-byte presence marker (`0x00` absent, `0x01` present)... This is the one command-payload fingerprint function every command-handler story uses; it is distinct from the AD-13 Provider-attempt fingerprint (which hashes the prepared external request, not the command DTO) and from this AD's own id-derivation components (which never include full payload state)."*

**The gap:** the same AD closes with an unchanged rule: *"Every digest of sensitive content stored outside a `ProtectedContent` envelope (per-message digests, verdict-cache keys, evidence fingerprints) is an HMAC-SHA-256 under a per-tenant `DigestKey` custodied through `EXT-SECRETS-1`; unkeyed SHA-256 is used only for identities derived from identifiers."* The command-payload fingerprint is, by the new sentence's own words, explicitly *not* one of "this AD's own id-derivation components" — it is a separate, DTO-content-hashing function. That means it does not qualify for the "unkeyed SHA-256 is used only for identities derived from identifiers" carve-out, and by elimination should fall under the HMAC-SHA-256 requirement for any digest of sensitive content stored outside a `ProtectedContent` envelope. But the sentence introducing it says it hashes the DTO "through that same shared canonicalizer" — and the canonicalizer itself is defined, once, at the top of the rule, as using plain `SHA-256` (not HMAC): *"One shared `AgentsIdentity` canonicalizer (UTF-8, length-prefixed components, `U+001F` separators, SHA-256, lowercase hex, purpose tag as the first component)."* The spine never says the payload fingerprint substitutes an HMAC step, nor does it exempt the payload fingerprint from the HMAC rule by name (the HMAC rule's parenthetical examples — "per-message digests, verdict-cache keys, evidence fingerprints" — don't obviously include or exclude "command-payload idempotency fingerprint," which is a fourth, textually unaddressed category).

**Why this matters concretely:** AD-12 states *"`AgentCallAcceptance`, `ProposalEdit`, and `ProposalRegeneration` are not lock-bearing and rely on the AD-29 client idempotency key"* — i.e., `ProposalEdit`'s idempotency protection is exactly this command-payload fingerprint mechanism. `ProposalEdit`'s DTO necessarily carries the actual edited reply text as a public property (that is the content of the edit), and AD-14 classifies edited content as sensitive conversation-derived content. Per AD-29's own "self-contained component list" description, that text becomes one length-prefixed component hashed into the fingerprint. The resulting idempotency record — including this fingerprint — "lives on the target aggregate's stream," i.e., is durable EventStore state. If that fingerprint is unkeyed SHA-256 (the literal, unqualified spec of "the shared canonicalizer"), any principal with read access to that stream or its idempotency-record projection — which is a materially larger population than the AD-22 `ProtectedContent` envelope's authorized readers (Eligible Approvers, Compliance Inspectors under a scoped inspection) — could run an offline dictionary/rainbow-table attack against short, common, or guessable edited replies (e.g., "Approved.", "Yes, that's correct.", boilerplate corrections) and confirm their content without ever obtaining `ProtectedContent`-envelope authorization. This is precisely the class of attack AD-29's own HMAC rule exists to prevent for every other content-derived digest in the same AD (per-message digests, verdict-cache keys, evidence fingerprints) — the command-payload fingerprint looks like an unintentional fourth exception, not a deliberate one, since nothing explains why it would be safe unkeyed when its siblings are not.

**A builder could implement this exactly as written and reintroduce content-hash exposure:** literally following "hashes... through that same shared canonicalizer" (which is specified as plain SHA-256) for the command-payload fingerprint, while correctly implementing HMAC-SHA-256 for the three named digest categories, satisfies every sentence in AD-29 as currently worded — while leaving a durable, unkeyed, dictionary-attackable digest of edited proposal content sitting in a projection outside the `ProtectedContent` envelope's access-control boundary.

**Recommendation direction (not prescriptive):** Either (a) state explicitly that the command-payload fingerprint uses the same per-tenant HMAC-SHA-256 `DigestKey` as any digest derived from a DTO carrying sensitive-content properties (with unkeyed SHA-256 reserved strictly for the enumerated id-derivation formulas), or (b) if keeping it unkeyed is intentional (e.g., because idempotency conflict-detection has a latency/rotation reason to avoid the secret-custodied `DigestKey`), add an explicit sentence saying so and require that no DTO property classified as sensitive content under AD-14 (free-text edit bodies, prompts, justification narrative) be included in the raw fingerprint input — substituting a reference or a separately HMAC-keyed content digest for that component instead.

## Secondary observations (carried forward / newly noted, not top-tier findings)

- **Two of v2's three secondary observations are now fixed as a side effect of this pass's edits, not because they were separately requested:** AD-17 now states explicitly *"The AD-2 tenant-ownership check on a keyed read always strictly precedes any `Freshness`/`RevisionLag` computation for that key, so a foreign-tenant or absent key never reaches freshness evaluation and cannot be distinguished from 'not found' by timing or staleness"* — this closes v2's cross-tenant existence-oracle observation. AD-22's now-complete nine-family justification list closes v2's `ProposalResolution` observation. The third (EventStore tamper-evidence being a policy statement, not a cryptographic one) is unchanged and out of this spine's stated scope, as v2 already noted.
- **AD-21's new per-attempt `OnBehalfOfPartyId` attribution rule** (*"`AdmitCall`'s per-Party accounting is keyed by `OnBehalfOfPartyId` per attempt... never by `CallerPartyId` for the interaction's whole lifetime, and each attempt's charge releases independently... regardless of whether the interaction itself has terminated"*) was checked for new tenant-isolation or PII exposure and found clean: `OnBehalfOfPartyId` was already a defined, non-PII (`PartyId`-typed) field under AD-30, its early release-per-attempt is a cost/fairness refinement, and per-Party rate-limit consumption is charged to the Party who took the action (the requesting Approver for a regeneration), which is a self-attribution, not a cross-Party leak or an escalation path.
- **AD-4's new three-independent-counters rule** (`ConfigurationVersion`, `InstructionsVersion`, `ApproverPolicyVersion`) was checked and found to be a correctness/versioning refinement with no PII, secret, or tenant-isolation surface — it doesn't change what data any counter carries, only how many counters exist.

## What is done well (for calibration, carried forward from v2 and reaffirmed this pass)

- AD-9's `SecretReference`/`ConfigurationReferenceId` design remains a genuine, enforceable mechanism.
- AD-22's DEK-per-interaction/KEK-per-tenant envelope and two-phase `ApplyHold` remain well-engineered, and the AD-22 amendment this pass (the ARCH-A-10 residual-risk sentence) is a rare example of an architecture doc naming its own boundary honestly rather than implying completeness it doesn't have.
- AD-2's tenant-scoping plus AD-17's now-explicit tenant-check-before-freshness ordering closes a subtle cross-tenant timing side channel that v2 flagged only as a secondary observation — the fix is more complete than what was asked for.
- AD-7's new cross-referencing sentence is a better fix than the literal v2 recommendation (a single normative source with named cross-references beats three redundant restatements).
- AD-30's `User`-principal freshness fix is a faithful, non-watered-down mirror of the `Administrator` language, not a weaker paraphrase.
