## Recovery findings

**High · Recovery needs a durable intended version before it can close the correct child lease.** The directory can close an absent or reserved child only by its exact identity. Both possible interaction outcomes also bind a specific intended version. AD-29 derives that version from the aggregate-assigned ordinal and version kind. Stories 7.1 and 7.3 assign the ordinal when recording successful output. The prepared descriptor in Story 6.4 binds the attempt, budget and capacity identities but does not establish where recovery gets the intended version before a child exists. [Spine, AD-13, AD-27 and AD-29](../ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md) · [Story 6.4 and Stories 7.1–7.3](../../../epics.md) · [Operation matrix, lines 404–405](../../../launch-readiness-register.md)

Crash after a successful regeneration and before the first child reservation. An eligible edit can advance the proposal’s version ordinal while recovery is pending. If a surviving worker and a recovery worker compute the next version from different aggregate revisions, closure at one directory identity cannot fence the other. The target should also exclude multiple dispositions across the entire attempt. Bind the intended ordinal, kind and identity durably on the attempt before dispatch, or define an equivalent attempt-keyed owner mapping recoverable before child creation. Preserve existing recorded version identities and reuse the same assignment on every retry. Test the interleaved edit, an absent child, a resumed writer and duplicate recovery.

**Medium · The implementation handoff needs the directory receipt before a negative append.** AD-27 and the v8 matrix already order these operations. The conventions say to bind a committed mutation lease where one exists, append the conditional negative result, and cancel an unused reservation. Story 6.1 describes the race only under a committed child lease. Taken alone, these instructions permit a negative result while an unobserved worker can still reserve the child, or leave recovery unable to proceed without a committed child. Require the durable closed/no-child or cancelled receipt before the negative append, or the exact winning child commit revision. Unknown owner results leave the attempt pending. Exercise absence followed by late acquire, cancellation racing commit, and a committed child whose positive target append races the negative. ## Verified rules

- A confirmed paid generation with lost bytes keeps its actual usage and original budget disposition. The negative result produces no version or content reference. An already committed version wins a lost-acknowledgement replay. A new Provider dispatch cannot recover the old bytes. An unresolved Provider outcome uses the separate indeterminate path. [AD-13, AD-21 and AD-27](../ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md)
- A public terminal outcome can release the original caller’s slot, and an indeterminate attempt can acknowledge the still-encumbered budget. The independent allocator claim continues counting against both limits until authoritative closure. The deletion manifest cannot treat acknowledgement of continued occupancy as proof of release. [AD-13, AD-21 and AD-24](../ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md) · [Version 8 capacity and deletion rules](../../../launch-readiness-register.md)
- In Confirmation mode, the original caller’s slot is released at the committed call event `ProposalCreated`. Human review and expiry continue under the live proposal workflow. Its terminal proposal event starts the content-retention clock. Regeneration leaves the original caller’s slot and earlier proposal intact. Automatic mode ties the caller’s release to its terminal posting record. [AD-5, AD-13, AD-22 and execution sequence](../ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md)

These checks exercise the written protocol. Live recovery, Provider cessation and paid-usage evidence remain with the registered owners.
